KitLives — what cordless tools look like three years in, told by a tradesperson who runs them.

A £8/month Substack where one UK tradesperson tests every cordless drill, impact driver and grinder for three real years on site, and publishes the only long-term reliability data trades get before they buy.

When you're about to spend £800 on a cordless kit, every review you find is an unboxing. Nobody tells you, two years later, which chuck stripped first or which battery dropped a cell. KitLives is a paid weekly Substack where a working UK tradesperson buys every new cordless drill, impact driver, grinder and combi-saw with his own money, runs it on real UK sites for a minimum of three years, and publishes the failure data, repair costs, and current resale value. It is the only English-language publication built around that question.

This works because the cordless market is in a marketing war. DeWalt FlexVolt Advantage, Milwaukee M18 Gen 4, Makita 40V XGT, Hikoki 36V Multi Volt, Bosch Professional 18V and a wave of Chinese platforms all claim the top spot. YouTube reviewers are locked into the algorithm, which rewards new releases every week. After year one, the data just does not exist for the buyer. UK tradespeople already pay £80–£180 a year for trade media such as Builder magazine and Construction News, so the conversion to a narrowly useful paid title has a track record.

What holds is the dataset itself. Each month the operator keeps a tool running makes the archive more authoritative, and a new entrant would need three years of testing to catch up. Free short-form clips on UK trade TikTok (the broken chuck, the dead battery) act as the top of the funnel into paid Substack. Switching cost per reader is light, but the recurring trust compounds for the operator because the data only goes one direction — older.

Self-employed UK electricians, plumbers, joiners and bricklayers buying their own cordless kit. Secondary payer: small trade shops stocking £4,000–£15,000 of platform-branded tools for two or three operatives.

The major platforms all refreshed their top-tier cordless lines between 2023 and 2025, so tradespeople are being asked to choose again right now. YouTube reviews are saturated with first-impression content, and there is no mature English-language source for what those tools look like at the two- and three-year mark. UK trade TikTok has matured into a serious discovery channel, with several UK tradespeople running accounts well past a million followers, which makes a TikTok-into-Substack funnel realistic for one person to operate.
