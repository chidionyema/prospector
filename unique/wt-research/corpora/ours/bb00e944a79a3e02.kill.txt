AnswerDesk, fast answers to enterprise security forms.

A fixed-fee desk that turns around enterprise security and vendor-risk questionnaires within 48 hours for seed and Series A startups without a security hire.

A seed-stage SaaS or fintech founder gets the email every operator dreads: the enterprise buyer's procurement team has sent over a 150-question security and data-protection questionnaire, and the deal will not move until it is answered in the buyer's own format, in the buyer's own language, with evidence attached. There is no one in the company whose job this is. The founder either does it themselves over a lost weekend or the deal slips a quarter.

AnswerDesk is a small team of security and compliance consultants who take the questionnaire off the founder's hands entirely and hand back a completed, evidenced submission within 48 hours for a fixed fee per questionnaire, with a monthly retainer once a client is selling into enterprise or public sector accounts regularly. This borrows the way translation agencies keep a memory of every segment they have ever translated, which gets sharper and cheaper to reuse with every new client, and applies that same compounding memory to security questionnaire answers: every completed questionnaire is logged against the specific buyer and their specific template, so the next startup selling to that same buyer gets a submission built from answers that buyer's own risk team has already accepted.

The reason this holds rather than being copied by a generic AI autofill tool is that the compounding asset is buyer-specific pass data, not the client's own document library. A single-tenant tool bundled into a compliance platform only ever sees one company's history; it has never seen what phrasing NatWest's third-party risk team waved through last quarter, or what NHS England's Data Security and Protection Toolkit reviewers kick back every year. AnswerDesk only accumulates that signal by running many different clients' questionnaires against the same repeat buyers, which is structurally invisible to a tool that lives inside one customer's account.

The founder, COO, or first ops hire at a UK seed to Series A startup who has an enterprise or public sector deal stuck behind a vendor security review and no one internally who can own it, paying a fixed fee (roughly £600-£1,500) per questionnaire, or a monthly retainer once enterprise deals are recurring.
