ParkingAppealDesk – your private parking ticket appeal, fixed fee.

A fixed‑fee service that builds a personalised appeal against private parking charges, backed by data on what works.

Private parking companies (e.g., ParkingEye, Euro Car Parks, NCP) issue millions of tickets annually. Many are unenforceable because of inadequate signage, unclear terms, or failure to follow the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012 (e.g., not delivering a Notice to Keeper within the prescribed time). This service borrows the consumer‑complaint‑database model (used in airline compensation) and transplants it to private parking. For a flat fee of £25, the user uploads photographs of the ticket, the signage, and a brief description. The service then queries a proprietary dataset of successful appeal arguments, indexed by parking company and breach type, and generates a tailored appeal letter. If the appeal goes to POPLA (the independent appeals service), the service provides a further stage‑specific evidence pack. The dataset is built from user‑reported outcomes and freedom‑of‑information requests to POPLA, creating a compounding data asset that improves its accuracy over time.

A Gen Z worker (18–27) — for example, a delivery rider or a temporary care worker — who receives a £100 private parking charge while dropping off a parcel or visiting a client’s building. They pay £25 to have a realistic chance of cancelling the charge, rather than risking the full penalty or spending hours researching.

The volume of private parking tickets issued in Britain reached over 11 million in 2023–24, up from 4.2 million five years earlier (RAC Foundation data). A 2023 Supreme Court judgement (ParkingEye v Beavis) confirmed that some charges can be lawful, but many operators still issue tickets that fail the statutory notice requirements or rely on poor signage. Gig workers, who are constantly on the move, are particularly exposed, and inflation makes even a discounted £60 penalty a significant hit.
