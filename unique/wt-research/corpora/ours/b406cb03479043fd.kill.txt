DispenseFit — the daily-revenue console for an independent UK pharmacy's NHS clinical services.

Software that joins each independent UK pharmacy's NHS clinical consultations — Pharmacy First, CPCS, NMS, flu — into one flow, from eligibility check to BSA reimbursement, so paid work is documented on the spot.

The UK's 11,500 community pharmacies have a new growth line that most cannot run cleanly. NHS Pharmacy First launched on 31 January 2024 with seven common-condition pathways paid at £14 per consultation (£28 where a medicine is supplied), and the Government has signalled a 2025 expansion to higher-acuity pathways. The Community Pharmacist Consultation Service (CPCS), the New Medicine Service (NMS), locally commissioned services such as emergency contraception and stop-smoking support, and the seasonal flu vaccination round add several pounds per encounter. A pharmacy that runs every available NHS clinical pathway could add a low-to-mid six figures to annual revenue.

The problem is the operating stack. The pharmacy management system — Cegedim's RxWeb, Positive Solutions, Sonar — holds the patient medication record but was not built for clinical consultations. PharmOutcomes, the national template-and-forms system used by most English community pharmacies, supplies the NHS templates and nothing on either side of them. The indemnity-grade clinical record, the letter to the GP after the consultation and the NHS Business Services Authority reimbursement claim are three separate jobs the pharmacist does after the patient has left, usually on paper or in Word. Most independents lose minutes per consultation and routinely underclaim.

DispenseFit is the daily-revenue console for the clinical-services business — to community pharmacy what Toast is to a small restaurant. Each consultation opens a single workspace that: loads the patient's summary from the pharmacy management system and NHS Spine, runs the eligibility and red-flag check for the chosen pathway against the pharmacy's local formulary and patient group directions, walks the pharmacist through a guided script matched to their indemnity provider's preferred record format, generates the clinical record the pharmacist signs and archives, drafts the post-consultation letter to the GP, and assembles the exemption-checked BSA claim bundle. Closing the encounter, the same screen shows what was paid, what was denied on first submission and what is owed tomorrow.

What makes this hard to copy is the codification of the indemnity-grade clinical record — NHS England does not publish the format, so we build it with the major pharmacy indemnifiers — and the per-pharmacy dataset that compounds. After twelve months of use the pharmacy's consultation archive carries its own clinical mix, denials, antibiotic-stewardship patterns and per-pathway margin; to switch tools the pharmacy would walk away from the historical evidence that ties to General Pharmaceutical Council revalidation and to its indemnifier's defence file.

Distribution is partner-led through the 69 Local Pharmaceutical Committees across England, the National Pharmacy Association (around 4,000 independent members), the Company Chemists' Association and the Association of Independent Multiple Pharmacies; direct sales close the medium independents (Lloydspharmacy branches released in the 2023 disposal to new owners, Rowlands, Well, Cohens). Pharmacies pay a monthly subscription per store, with multi-store discounts, and the unit economics for the pharmacy pay back once the tool recovers the cost of a single missed BSA claim per week.
