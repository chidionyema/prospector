Weighbridge Rebate — the fixed-fee service that gets skip firms and groundworkers their landfill tax overpayments back.

We audit the tipping tickets of small UK skip hire and muck-away firms, then reclaim the landfill tax they overpaid on soil and rubble that qualified for the lower rate.

A skip hire owner with four lorries tips waste at a transfer station or landfill and pays a gate fee. Buried inside that gate fee is Landfill Tax. In England and Northern Ireland the standard rate is charged per tonne, and a much lower rate applies to qualifying materials such as rocks, soil, ceramics and concrete listed in the Landfill Tax (Qualifying Material) Order 2011. The gap between the two rates is large — the standard rate is tens of pounds a tonne, the lower rate is roughly a pound or two. Scotland and Wales run their own versions through Revenue Scotland and the Welsh Revenue Authority, with the same two-tier split.

Here is the friction the operator feels every week. The site weighs the load and the weighbridge operator picks a waste code on the spot. If a load of clean dig arisings gets coded as mixed general waste, the skip firm is charged the standard rate on every tonne. On a 20-tonne muck-away load that single keystroke can cost hundreds of pounds. The firm pays the invoice because arguing with the transfer station takes an afternoon they do not have, and because nobody in the yard is checking ticket-by-ticket.

We borrow a mechanism that is completely ordinary in freight and utilities but almost unknown in waste: the freight bill audit. Large retailers routinely hire firms that re-rate every carrier invoice against the contract and take a share of what they recover. We apply that same rate-audit discipline to weighbridge tickets and landfill tax coding. The customer sends us a year of tipping tickets and gate invoices. We match each ticket to the job, the site it came from, the waste transfer note, and the material actually loaded. Where a qualifying load was coded and charged at the standard rate, we build the evidence pack and pursue a credit from the landfill site operator, who in turn adjusts its own return to HMRC.

We charge a share of what we recover, plus a small monthly fee to keep checking new tickets going forward. The share means the operator risks nothing. The monthly fee is what turns a one-off recovery into a business.

What holds this is not cleverness, it is the file we build. After a few hundred audits we know which transfer stations mis-code which material, at which sites, on which shifts, and what evidence each of those operators has previously accepted to issue a credit. That is a record of counterparty behaviour that no software vendor and no waste firm can obtain, because each individual skip firm only sees its own tickets. We see thousands across dozens of firms. That file makes each new claim faster and more likely to succeed, and it is the reason a customer keeps paying the monthly fee rather than doing it themselves.

A team of four runs this: two people doing ticket review and claim assembly, one building the matching tooling, one selling. Ticket matching is largely automatable from weighbridge PDFs; the judgement calls and the phone calls to site managers are not.

Owner-operators of UK skip hire, grab lorry and muck-away firms running roughly 3 to 30 vehicles, plus small groundwork contractors who tip their own excavation waste. These are people who sign the cheques themselves and know exactly what a tipping ticket costs.

Landfill Tax standard rates have risen steeply year on year, so the gap between the standard and lower rate is now the biggest it has ever been — which makes a single mis-coded load expensive enough to chase. HMRC has also been running enforcement activity on landfill sites and unauthorised waste, which makes site operators far more careful about their own coding records and far more willing to correct an error you can evidence. Meanwhile digital weighbridge tickets are now standard at most transfer stations, so a year of tickets arrives as files rather than a shoebox.
