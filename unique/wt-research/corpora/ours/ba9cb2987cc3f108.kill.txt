Fabric Freeze, spare test gear when your lab HVAC dies.

A rental depot in Austin, Texas that ships pre-configured environmental chambers and thermal test rigs to hardware startups within 24 hours when their own lab climate control fails.

Hardware startups doing product qualification run thermal cycling and humidity soak tests on their own benchtop chambers. When a chamber compressor dies or a building HVAC failure knocks a lab out of spec, every certification test in progress is invalid and must restart. A test that takes three weeks becomes six.

This business keeps a small fleet of calibrated chambers, thermal shock rigs and data loggers in a warehouse near the Austin tech corridor. A hardware engineering manager calls, and a rig arrives on a flatbed the next morning with a current calibration certificate attached. Rental runs weekly with a delivery and setup fee.

This borrows the way film production houses rent camera and lighting packages by the day rather than selling them, and applies it to environmental test equipment that sits idle 80% of the year in any single company.

The compounding asset is the calibration and chain-of-custody record for each specific unit. Certification bodies want traceable calibration history for equipment used in qualification testing. Every rental adds another documented, witnessed use cycle to that unit's file, which makes the fleet progressively more usable for regulated work rather than less.
