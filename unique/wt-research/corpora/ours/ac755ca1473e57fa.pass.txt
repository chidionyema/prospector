Prepaid fee recovery service for parents of closed nurseries.

A fixed-fee transaction broker that, when a nursery or childminding agency collapses mid-term, recovers the parent's prepaid fees and deposits from the administrator, card issuer, funding authority and childcare-voucher scheme in parallel — then executes the child's transfer into a same-week replacement place, charging per recovery channel closed.

Lens: broaden — instead of one recovery route, the broker owns the WHOLE post-collapse value chain a carer cannot run while holding a job and a distressed child: (1) Section 75 / chargeback against the card used for the term invoice, (2) proof-of-debt filing in the administration, (3) reclaim of the local authority's Free Entitlement funding already paid forward for weeks not delivered (the LA can recover from the provider and redirect to the new setting — parents almost never know this), (4) release of stranded Tax-Free Childcare and legacy voucher balances tied to a deregistered provider ID, (5) negotiated waiver of the NEW setting's registration fee and deposit using the collapse as leverage. A solo AI-leveraged operator runs it as a per-collapse event desk: each closure produces a cohort of 40–200 identically-situated families reachable in one place (the parents' WhatsApp/Facebook group that forms within hours), so distribution is owned and free, and the marginal cost of the 60th family in the same collapse is near zero because the administrator's contact, the LA's funding officer and the provider's payment rails are already mapped. Value capture is per-channel-closed (£65 per channel, capped), not a subscription. The compounding asset is a proprietary per-collapse and per-local-authority playbook: which administrator responds to which proof format, which LA claws back and redirects Free Entitlement versus refusing, which card issuers accept a nursery as a Section 75 'supplier of services', typical days-to-payout — a dataset that only accrues to whoever has worked many collapses and is structurally unobtainable to anyone entering later.

Parent-carers of 0–4s at a specific closed private nursery or childminding agency (typically 40–200 households per collapse), who have prepaid a half-term or full term (£800–£2,600) plus a deposit and are simultaneously scrambling for a replacement place; secondary payer is the replacement setting, which pays a placement fee for a pre-vetted cohort of funded children arriving mid-term.

UK private nursery insolvencies have run at elevated rates since the 2024–25 expansion of funded hours pushed underfunded settings into cashflow failure while wage and NIC costs rose; the expanded entitlement means far more money is now paid FORWARD by local authorities per child, so a mid-term collapse now strands both parental prepayments and public funding at once — a recovery surface that did not exist at this size before the entitlement expansion, and one that appears suddenly and locally with a ready-made parent group.
