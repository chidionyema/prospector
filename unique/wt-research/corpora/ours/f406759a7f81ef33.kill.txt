CareForward — upfront cash for a share of your future caregiving wages.

A platform where California primary carers get cash today by selling a small share of their future state-paid caregiver income, so they can keep caring.

Primary carers often quit work or cut hours to look after a loved one. In California, the In-Home Supportive Services (IHSS) program pays family caregivers, but only after the care is provided and hours are submitted. Many carers need money now to cover rent, food, or equipment. CareForward is a transactional platform that lets them sell a portion of their expected future IHSS wages to investors in return for a lump sum. The investor receives a fixed percentage of the carer's IHSS pay over a set term, and the carer gets breathing room. CareForward charges a one-time facilitation fee from the funded amount.

The idea borrows the way income share agreements let students fund education, but applies it to a first-principles truth: a carer's sure future income is an asset that can be unlocked today. The platform builds a proprietary underwriting model using the care recipient's condition, the carer's work history, and real IHSS payment data to predict reliable earnings. That model improves with every funded carer, making credit scores irrelevant. CareForward handles the legal paperwork, automates the pay splits from the state, and stays within California’s securities and labour rules.

The wedge is proprietary data: no bank or lender has a dataset linking caregiving circumstances to IHSS payment outcomes, so they cannot underwrite this income stream. As more carers use the platform, the model sharpens and attracts more investors, which lowers the cost of capital for new carers – a classic two-sided network effect. Risk is shared, not dumped on the carer: if actual earnings fall short because care needs change, the payback adjusts down proportionally.

California primary carers who already provide or expect to provide IHSS-funded care and need cash now; they pay nothing upfront and repay only from the wages they eventually receive, with the platform’s fee taken once from the funded sum.

IHSS enrolment in California is climbing as the population ages, yet many family carers face a cash crunch before they can clock their first paid hour. At the same time, the SEC’s evolving guidance on income share agreements has opened a legal path for new platforms, and open-banking tools make automated wage verification and payment splits possible without manual effort.
