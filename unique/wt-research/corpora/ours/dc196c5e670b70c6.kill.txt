RigSafe, certified power kits for home AI rigs.

RigSafe sells UK-wiring-regs-certified power distribution kits that let developers run multi-GPU AI rigs at home without tripping breakers or voiding insurance.

A developer buys a second or third RTX 4090 or 5090 to run local LLM inference or fine-tuning at home, plugs it into a normal UK ring circuit, and the consumer unit's RCD trips the moment the rig, the kettle and the EV charger overlap. Fixing this properly means an electrician-certified rewire, a call to the landlord, or a three-phase supply upgrade — and National Grid ESO's own connection queues for anything beyond a standard domestic supply now stretch years in many areas, so that route is closed to almost everyone renting or on a normal 100A single-phase supply.

RigSafe sells a physical kit: a load-balancing power distribution unit engineered to BS 7671 18th Edition Amendment 2 rules, built with UK ring-final-circuit-aware phase splitting, per-circuit current monitoring, and a companion app that warns before a breaker trips rather than after. Every kit ships with a compliance pack — circuit diagrams, load calculations and a signed declaration — written in the format UK home insurers and landlords actually ask for when they query "electrical alterations," something a generic extension lead can never produce.

The wedge is the engineering and paperwork together, not the hardware alone: designing a device that safely spreads a 3–5kW GPU load across existing domestic circuits without an electrician, and pairing it with documentation an insurer will accept, is a specific piece of technical work RigSafe owns. A contract electronics manufacturer builds the units and a 3PL ships them, so a team of four or five — one electrical engineer, one firmware/app developer, one who handles certification liaison and support — can run this as a real ecommerce brand rather than a services business.

UK software developers and indie hackers self-hosting LLM inference or training rigs at home — especially renters and leaseholders who cannot get a landlord or freeholder to sign off on an electrician-led rewire, and who need to show their insurer the setup is safe.

Home GPU purchases for local AI work have risen sharply through 2025 as developers move inference off expensive cloud GPUs; National Grid ESO's connection queue for anything above standard domestic supply routinely runs into multi-year waits, closing off the easy fix; and UK home insurers have tightened questions about DIY electrical alterations following a string of high-profile fire-safety reviews, so an undocumented power setup can now void a claim.
