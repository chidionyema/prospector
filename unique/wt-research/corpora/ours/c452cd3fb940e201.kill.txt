SideDesk — gets your NHS, council or civil service side job declared and approved without risking your job.

A fixed-fee pack that checks your employer's own secondary-employment rules and writes your side-hustle declaration so it goes through first time, before you tell HR anything.

A nurse on a hospital ward, a teacher marking books at 9pm, or a caseworker at the Department for Work and Pensions all have the same quiet worry. They want to earn extra money outside their main job, maybe tutoring, maybe a small online shop, maybe weekend photography, but their contract says they have to declare any outside work first. Get that declaration wrong and the consequences are not small: an NHS trust can treat undeclared outside work as a conduct issue, a civil service department can see it as a breach of the Civil Service Management Code's rules on outside interests, and a school can involve safeguarding checks under the Teachers' Standards if the side work touches pupils. Nobody wants to be the test case.

The honest problem is that the free route, asking your own HR team, is not really free of risk. HR is the same department that decides whether to approve you, keeps the paperwork on file, and in the NHS or civil service that file can follow you into appraisals or even fitness-to-practise conversations years later. Most people do not want to raise their hand until they already know what a safe, approvable request looks like.

SideDesk sends you a short form: your employer, your role, a copy or summary of your trust's or council's actual secondary employment policy, and a plain description of the side work you want to do. In return you get a written declaration ready to submit, matched against real wording from that specific employer's own policy, plus notes on how similar requests at similar employers were actually decided. Over time the business builds a library of which kinds of side work NHS trusts wave through without a second look (an unrelated online shop, freelance graphic design) and which ones they routinely block or want more detail on (private nursing shifts, tutoring your own pupils, anything using your clinical title). No generic employment solicitor or single union rep sees enough cases across enough different NHS trusts, councils and government departments to know that pattern; a solo employment lawyer sees a handful of clients, not thousands of policy documents and outcomes. That accumulated pattern, not the document template itself, is what a customer is actually paying for.

An individual public sector employee, for example a band 5–7 NHS nurse or paramedic, a classroom teacher, or a caseworker at HMRC or DWP, pays around £89–£149 out of their own pocket before they say anything to their employer, because getting it wrong risks their job, their pension, or their professional registration.

ONS figures show the number of people holding a second job in the UK reached a record high through 2023–2024, and public sector pay has fallen behind private sector pay growth for several years running, pushing more NHS, council and civil service staff toward side income at the same time as employers are tightening enforcement of conduct and conflict-of-interest rules following a run of high-profile disciplinary cases. NHS England refreshed its guidance on managing conflicts of interest and standards of business conduct in recent years, and departments have been reminding staff more actively about the Civil Service Management Code's rules on outside work, so the risk of getting caught out with an undeclared side job is rising just as the financial pressure to take one on is rising too.
