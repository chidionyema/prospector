CarerPension — turns the years you spent caring into the State Pension you didn't lose.

A self-serve tool for UK primary carers that finds every National Insurance year the State owes them for caring, then files the credit claim. Typical recovery: £70,000+ of pension.

Plain version. UK primary carers who gave up paid work to look after a relative often have gaps in their National Insurance record, and those gaps shrink their State Pension for the rest of their lives. The full basic State Pension is £221.20 a week from April 2024 — about £11,500 a year. A decade of caring without NI credits can mean £11,500 a year lost forever, over £230,000 across 20 years of retirement. There are two specific statutory mechanisms to recover those years: Carer's Credit, a free NI credit created by the Carer's Credit Regulations 2010 for carers who don't qualify for Carer's Allowance; and Class 3 voluntary NI contributions, paid buybacks under Section 23 of the Pensions Act 2008, costing £907.20 a year for 2024-25. This tool pulls the carer's National Insurance record, identifies every year they cared without getting credit, models which mechanism is cheaper or free for each year, generates the claim forms with the medical and care evidence the Department for Work and Pensions and HMRC actually accept, and tracks each application to completion.

Why it works. The financial pain is enormous and almost invisible. Most carers don't realise they have lost State Pension until they check their State Pension Forecast on Gov.uk. The rolling 6-year limit for paying Class 3 voluntary NI contributions means every year a carer delays costs them an older year of pension they can never recover, so the older the carer, the more urgent the action. The £151 a week Carer's Allowance earnings threshold that decides whether a carer gets Carer's Allowance or only Carer's Credit is publicly knowable but rarely cross-referenced with the carer's actual weekly earnings and the cared-for person's qualifying benefit status. The tool does that cross-referencing once, picks the cheaper route for each year, and assembles the evidence pack the DWP and HMRC each require.

Why it holds. The DWP and HMRC cannot proactively tell every carer to claim. They do not know who is a carer in the first place — only the 1.4 million unpaid carers the 2021 Census identified, and that is a fraction of the real number. The Gov.uk State Pension Forecast shows the gap in pounds but does not recommend a year-by-year strategy, does not model Carer's Credit eligibility against the £151 threshold, and does not assemble the medical evidence a Carer's Credit claim needs. HMRC publishes the Class 3 NI form but cannot take the carer's side, cannot recommend a claim strategy (which would compromise its role as the impartial administrator of the National Insurance fund), and cannot chase DWP for missing evidence on a Carer's Credit claim. Each carer's NI record, care history, and chosen strategy becomes proprietary data the tool keeps and re-runs every Budget. The tool compounds: every claim it wins adds to its dataset of accepted evidence, decision patterns and processing times, so the next carer's pack is sharper.

Primary carers in the UK — typically women in their 40s and 50s caring for elderly parents or disabled relatives — who have at least 8 years of NI gaps from caring and who can afford a £149 one-off fee. Distribution routes: carer charities (Carers UK, Carers Trust, Age UK), the HMRC State Pension Forecast reminder emails, GP social prescribing link workers, Citizens Advice carer caseworkers, and Facebook carer communities. The fee is dwarfed by the typical recovery — present value of £25,000 to £60,000 of pension uplift after Treasury discount rates, depending on age and gap.

Three converging pressures. First, the rolling 6-year window for paying Class 3 voluntary NI contributions means every year a carer delays costs them an older year of pension they can never recover, so the older the carer, the more urgent the action. Second, Carer's Allowance overpayment enforcement has become markedly more aggressive since 2023 under the DWP Compliance Directorate, putting carers on alert about what they have failed to claim correctly. Third, the Carer's Leave Act 2023, in force since 6 April 2024, has put caring into the workplace conversation and surfaced tens of thousands of newly-identified carers who previously did not realise they had NI-related entitlements. The full basic State Pension rose to £221.20 a week in April 2024, raising the stakes of any gap.
