MeterClaim — finds the money your energy supplier owes you back.

A self-serve tool for small business owners that reads their gas and electricity bills, finds unlawful overcharges, and writes the recovery letter for them.

A small business owner running a shop, salon, warehouse or office rarely has time to check whether their energy bill is even correct. Many get rolled onto a 'deemed' or 'out of contract' rate automatically when a fixed term ends, often at two or three times the price, without the 28 to 60 day renewal notice suppliers are required to give under Ofgem's rules for microbusinesses. Others are still being charged 20% VAT when their usage qualifies for the 5% low-usage rate, or are billed for energy used more than 12 months ago, which Ofgem's back-billing protection for microbusinesses says suppliers cannot do.

MeterClaim lets an owner photograph or upload their bills, going back as far as they have them. The tool reads the meter numbers, contract dates, unit rates and VAT rates off each bill, and checks them against the supplier's own published tariff history and Ofgem's Standards of Conduct for microbusiness customers. Where it finds a breach, it calculates exactly how much has been overcharged and produces a formal complaint letter, citing the specific rule broken, ready to send to the supplier. If the supplier does not resolve it within eight weeks, the tool builds the case file for the free Energy Ombudsman service, in the format that service expects.

This works because nobody else is set up to look for these errors on the customer's behalf. Ofgem writes the rules but has no mandate to audit an individual business's bill. Energy brokers are paid commission by suppliers for signing new contracts, so digging up a supplier's past billing mistakes is directly against their own income. And the Energy Ombudsman only investigates once a complaint with a specific claimed amount has already been raised, so somebody still has to do the sum.

The business owner pays a flat fee per site audited, because they are the one holding the unpaid bills and the one who receives the refund if the claim succeeds. A shop with one meter pays a small one-off fee to run its bill history through the tool; a business with several sites, such as a small chain of cafes or a haulage yard with multiple depots, pays per meter and gets a claim built for each one.

Since the 2022 to 2023 wholesale energy price spike, a large number of small businesses were moved onto deemed and out-of-contract rates and many have simply stayed there, still paying the emergency-level price years later because nobody went back to check the paperwork. Ofgem's Standards of Conduct and back-billing protection for microbusinesses have been in force since 2018, but enforcement depends entirely on the customer noticing and complaining, and most small business owners have never read the rule that would let them claim the money back.
