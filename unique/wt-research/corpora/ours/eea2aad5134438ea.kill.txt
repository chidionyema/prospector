CHC Retrospect — The NHS Continuing Healthcare Retroactive Claim Broker.

A no-win-no-fee transaction broker for UK primary_carers — prepares and prosecutes retrospective NHS Continuing Healthcare (CHC) claims against the ICB for care-home fees the family should not have paid, taking 18–22% of the statutory cash recovered.

First-principles rebuild: the National Framework for NHS Continuing Healthcare makes the ICB liable for the full cost of care when a person's 'primary need' is a health need — and the family can recover past fees they wrongly paid. The ICB's own process is structurally conflicted (it pays from its own budget) so ~50% of first-time applications are denied, and a multi-year appeal route exists (MDT → panel → Local Resolution → Independent Review → Parliamentary Ombudsman) that primary_carers cannot prosecute alone. A solo broker owning a proprietary ICB-by-ICB interpretation matrix and a clinical-need structuring process converts a denied application into a paid retrospective claim in 60–80% of cases. Average recovered £60,000–£280,000 per case; broker take 18–22%.

Specifically nameable: the primary_carer (adult child or spouse) of a UK care-home resident whose relative has a primary health need — typically dementia with behavioural need, advanced Parkinson's, MS, motor neurone disease, or post-stroke frailty. They have paid £40,000–£150,000+ in fees over the past 1–5 years without realising CHC existed. Often time-poor, often in arrears with the LA, often unaware of the statutory route. Concrete: the primary_carer of a 78-year-old mother with vascular dementia admitted to a nursing home in 2022 who has paid £78,000 in self-funded fees.

Three converging signals: (1) the 2022 revised National Framework tightened the 'primary health need' interpretation, producing more wrongful denials; (2) ICB budgets are squeezed post-2022, increasing denial rates of borderline cases; (3) post-2022 media coverage (ITV, Channel 4) raised public awareness and several ICBs are now actively inviting retrospective applications. Estimated 30–50% of self-funders in UK care homes have a primary health need but have never been assessed — a large latent market being made newly visible.

Multiple passages confirm retrospective CHC claims are a real, structured statutory mechanism allowing recovery of up to 6 years of wrongly-paid care fees, with a formal review process and active specialist/solicitor market — indicating the value is genuine, durable, and not yet fully commoditised.
