ScopeAccount — the quantity surveyor for freelancers, who recovers what your client should have paid for the work that crept past your brief.

A specialist agency that reviews your project files, identifies the unpaid extras that crept past your original brief, and recovers the money from your client on a contingency fee.

Creative freelancers routinely absorb work that grows past the original brief — extra revisions, accessibility compliance, second languages, platform rebuilds, brand-system extensions — then fail to bill for it because they do not know what to charge or fear losing the client relationship. Construction solves this same problem with quantity surveyors, who document variations against the original scope and produce a 'final account' showing what extra work is owed. ScopeAccount ports that methodology to freelance creative work. A trained operator reviews the freelancer's emails, briefs, deliverables and timesheets, builds a chronological variation log, attaches a defensible rate to each item, and serves a written claim on the client. If the client pays or settles, the freelancer keeps most of it; if not, the agency files a small claim on the same evidence pack using the Money Claims Online service at HM Courts and Tribunals. The methodology itself is what cannot be copied by a high-street firm — there is no off-the-shelf 'scope creep calculator' for design or development work, and the legal claim is grounded in English contract law (implied terms, quantum meruit for work outside the brief) rather than late-payment statutes. Each case compounds a private dataset of how scope creeps in each subsector (logo design, brochure design, Shopify builds, custom WordPress, brand sprints, technical writing), which makes the next valuation faster and more accurate. The agency is small (one trained operator plus a paralegal) and profitable at low volume because the unit economics work: average recovery £3,000, contingency 25%, cases take 10-20 hours.

Two trends make this acute: freelance project complexity has grown as clients expect multi-format, multi-platform, accessibility-ready deliverables without paying more, and the post-2022 cost-of-living squeeze makes freelancers less willing to absorb unpaid work. Meanwhile, no UK provider offers construction-style variation accounting for creative work; the closest are high-street solicitors who refuse sub-£5,000 claims as uneconomic, freelancer accountants who do not value design labour, and contract-template shops who only help freelancers write better briefs in the first place.

Free scope-creep calculators for freelance creative workers already exist and are marketed to do exactly what the candidate claims no one offers — quantify unpaid extra work and communicate it to clients — which undercuts the claim that this value is undelivered by any free tool.
