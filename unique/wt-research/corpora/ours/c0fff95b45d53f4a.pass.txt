Booking calendar and owner updates for solo pet sitters.

A web app that pulls pet-sitting bookings from Rover, Cat in a Flat and direct messages into one calendar, auto‑sends photos to owners, and chases reviews — for the gig worker juggling multiple pet-care apps.

Solo pet‑sitters on gig platforms like Rover and Cat in a Flat spend too many unpaid hours on admin: they toggle between apps to see their schedule, copy‑paste the same ‘your dog is fine’ update across WhatsApp and email, and forget to ask for reviews that would win them more work. This borrows the channel‑manager model that big holiday‑let operators use to sync bookings across Airbnb, Booking.com and their own site, and puts it into the hands of a twenty‑something earning extra cash from pet‑sitting. The software plugs into each platform’s calendar feed, displays everything on a single clean dashboard, and lets the sitter tap one button to send a photo‑and‑comment update to the owner on the channel that owner expects. It also remembers each pet’s quirks — ‘only feed at 6’, ‘allergic to chicken’ — so the sitter looks professional from the first visit. The tool earns a £6 monthly subscription; once a sitter has ten repeat clients and fifty reviews stored inside it, ripping it out means losing that history and having to rebuild trust manually. That switching cost is what keeps a solo operator in business, even when bigger rivals appear.

Gen‑Z gig pet‑sitters — typically 20‑ to 27‑year‑olds who walk dogs, board cats or house‑sit through apps, and who want to look more professional than the next sitter so they can charge higher rates and get repeat bookings. They pay the £6 monthly fee themselves.

Pet‑sitting platforms have exploded in the UK since 2020, creating a market of micro‑sellers who work across two or three apps to fill their calendar. Meanwhile, none of those platforms offer a cross‑platform dashboard or automated photo‑sharing that works when the booking came from a different app. The tools the platforms do provide are designed to serve the platform’s brand, not the sitter’s reputation. Sitters are crying out for a unified way to manage their little business.
