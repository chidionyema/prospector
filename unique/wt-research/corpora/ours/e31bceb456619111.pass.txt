Hand repair kit for NHS nurses with hand-rub damage.

A post-shift hand repair kit sold to NHS nurses whose hands are wrecked by hospital hand-rub, proven to work with the sanitiser so skin and safety do not have to be traded off.

NHS nurses are told to sanitise their hands dozens of times a shift with alcohol‑based hand‑rub. The result is a pandemic of hand dermatitis, with 40% of clinical staff in some surveys reporting a skin problem. Many nurses fear hand creams will interfere with the sanitiser’s killing power, so they use nothing and worsen the damage.
This is a direct‑to‑consumer ecommerce brand selling a dermatologist‑formulated kit: an overnight repair cream, a silicone‑free barrier cream for work, and cotton glove liners. Every product is independently tested against three common NHS‑procured hand‑rubs (Purell Advanced, Cutan, and Clinell) to prove it leaves a stable antimicrobial film intact, using an EN 1500‑based method adapted for residual effect. Nurses get a personal kit from a website built to feel like a professional tool, with clear evidence they can show their infection‑control lead.
NHS infection‑control teams often tell staff ‘use hand cream at home’ but cannot recommend a brand. That gap is an exclusive channel we build by working with staff‑side health and wellbeing reps in trusts, who are free to pass on a product their colleagues trust. The rep receives a discount code unique to their trust; the brand tracks that channel. Over time, repeat purchase data from thousands of nurses lets us refine the formula further and patent the specific combination of humectants that remains non‑interfering, creating a technical moat that generic cosmetic brands cannot copy quickly.

A nurse, midwife or healthcare assistant working in an NHS trust, paying £18‑£24 out of their own pocket for a monthly kit subscription. They buy it because the constant pain and cracking makes every shift harder, and a one‑off hand cream from Boots didn’t fix it.

Two things align. First, NHS hand‑hygiene compliance audits are stricter than ever, and trusts switched to high‑performance rubs that damage skin faster. Second, the post‑pandemic ‘skin barrier’ conversation has educated buyers that a damaged skin barrier needs more than a moisturiser — it needs repair lipids and prebiotics, and consumers now understand that language.
