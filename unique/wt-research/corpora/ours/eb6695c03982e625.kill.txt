LockerKey Rounds — the mobile key-replacement service that NHS staff use when they lose their padlock key at work.

A mobile locksmith visits NHS hospitals weekly, cuts lost padlock keys and records your key code so next time you never pay full price.

NHS staff bring their own padlocks for personal lockers, but when the key is lost the only option is to call a locksmith at ad‑hoc rates or borrow bolt cutters from the estates team. LockerKey Rounds visits sites on a known schedule, offering a cheaper on‑the‑spot cut‑and‑replace service and a key‑code registration for £5 a year that gives a 50% discount on future replacements.

The solo operator compiles a proprietary dataset of which padlock models and keyways are used across different hospital sites, reducing the time to identify the correct key blank. This saves the staff member time and money. The business is bootstrapped with a van, a key‑cutting machine, and a stock of common padlocks.

The wedge is switching cost. Once a staff member registers their key code, they are tied to the service because they get a discount and avoid the hassle of finding a new locksmith. The regular visit schedule and the internal nickname ("the key person") make it the default choice.

Individual NHS employees — nurses, healthcare assistants, porters — who lose their locker key. They pay £25 for a lock cut and replacement, and £5 for the annual key‑code registration.

The NHS workforce is under extreme pressure, turnover of locker keys is high, and staff are time‑poor. The service saves them a trip to a hardware store or a wait for an estates team that serves the hospital, not the individual.
