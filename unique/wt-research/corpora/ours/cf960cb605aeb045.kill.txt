CouncilTaxBand Anomaly Atlas – The Inherited Property Downband Index.

A postcode-level UK data product that flags properties whose VOA council tax band is statistically incompatible with actual sold prices in the same street, weighted toward properties held 20+ years or inherited by retirees, with hit-rate scoring on past successful downband challenges.

Friction: many UK retirees sit in council tax bands set in 1991 that bear no relation to current property values, especially in London, Surrey, Bristol and parts of Scotland. A widowed retiree in a 1991 Band E London flat now worth £650k is paying hundreds a year more than a comparable Band D neighbour who moved in 2019. The 2023 revaluation was postponed indefinitely, meaning the VOA will not fix these errors — the resident must appeal. Invert: the VOA is the duty-imposer for accurate bands; the MORE it defers revaluation, the larger the anomaly backlog grows, and the more valuable an independent anomaly-detection dataset becomes. The VOA structurally cannot publish an 'appeal this postcode' tool, because that would be a regulator actively encouraging appeals against itself. Form: a proprietary_data intelligence product with monthly updated hit-rate scoring per postcode, sold to retiree home occupiers and their adult children.

UK retirees (65-80) in properties owned 20+ years or inherited from a spouse, particularly in metropolitan and South East postcodes where 1991 bands are most detached from current values. Many will be paying for the adult child (45-60) to run the check on their behalf.

2023 England council tax revaluation postponed indefinitely; 2026 Scottish revaluation ongoing but creating new anomaly patterns; rising council tax bills against frozen incomes is a top retiree cost-of-living pressure; VOA's 'Check Your Band' service is a black-box estimator that does not score challenge probability.
