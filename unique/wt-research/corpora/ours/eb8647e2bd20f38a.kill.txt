SplitCare Rebate — The Primary Carer's Council-Tax Second-Adult & Care-Occupancy Reclassification Broker.

A fixed-fee transaction broker that reconstructs a caring household's occupancy history and executes the specific council-tax reclassification claims (second-adult rebate, carer disregard, annexe/dependent-relative exemption, banding-effect adaptations) with the billing authority, charging per successful reclassification plus a slice of the backdated refund.

First-principles lens: the raw pain is not 'I don't know my discounts' but 'my house changed shape and who lives in it changed, and nobody re-priced the bill'. When a carer moves a parent in, converts a garage to a wet-room, or has a disabled adult child turn 18, four separate statutory levers exist across three different council teams (revenues, adult social care, planning) and each is claimed on a different form with different evidence and different backdating rules. Rebuilt from scratch, the job is a per-claim brokerage: take the household facts once, decide which levers fire, assemble each authority's idiosyncratic evidence bundle, and execute. Monetisation is per-executed-claim and per-refund-recovered, so the operator only wins when money moves. The compounding asset is a per-billing-authority (≈380 UK councils) dataset of what each revenues team actually accepts as proof of care, how far each backdates in practice, which reject annexe exemptions on planning grounds, and which named escalation route reverses a refusal — data obtainable only by executing claims and only readable across many authorities.

Primary carers in multi-generational UK households — specifically carers who have moved an elderly parent in, carers of a disabled adult child who has just aged past 18, and carers occupying a self-contained annexe — paying £145 per reclassification executed plus 20% of any backdated refund recovered, capped. Secondary payer: independent care-agency owners and small home-adaptation contractors who refer clients and take a flat referral credit.

Council-tax bills have risen at the referendum-capped maximum for consecutive years while adult-social-care precepts have grown as a separate line, so the absolute value of any single disregard or exemption is materially larger than it was five years ago. At the same time councils under section-114 pressure have cut face-to-face revenues counters and pushed claims to self-service portals, which raises the evidence burden on the claimant exactly when the prize grew. Multi-generational living has increased, and the post-pandemic wave of garage/annexe conversions created a large cohort of households whose occupancy status on the valuation and billing records is now stale.

A May 2026 Age UK factsheet still lists Second Adult Rebate alongside appeals and changes of circumstances, and current council pages still administer council-tax discounts and exemptions, so the statutory levers the broker monetises exist now [3138d1675e36a5a8][9504acb675a30998][71dfd3b4ab01244c]. The only erosion signal is a 2013-era proposal by some councils to abolish the separate 25% single-adult discount, which does not remove the carer/second-adult reliefs at issue [97f527d40a3f496b].

The only operators the passages evidence are no-win-no-fee band-challenge firms — Ingram Toft and Abbington Blake — whose stated specialism is properties in the wrong valuation band [e49e592566da7261][924b8449251e9aea][d3af5ff670389372], and the official routes described are VOA banding appeals [9930f5239fc605f0][9919bc7d31a10115]; none of these is a dominant incumbent, and none addresses carer disregards, second-adult rebate, or annexe/occupancy reclassification.
