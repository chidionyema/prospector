Refund Rig, test kit that proves a courier lost it.

A hardware-and-software kit that gives UK online sellers timestamped proof a parcel left their unit intact, so courier claims and card chargebacks get paid.

This is the toolkit sellers buy to win arguments about parcels. A small scanner-and-camera rig sits over the packing bench. Every parcel gets weighed, photographed from four angles and matched to its label as it is packed. The seller ends up with one signed record per order: label number, weight, contents shot, and the exact second it went into the courier's bag.

Sell the rig, then sell the evidence pipeline as a subscription. The rig is off-the-shelf parts: a load cell, a barcode scanner, two cameras, a small box that runs the software. Roughly £320 of parts, sold at £790, then £59 a month per bench for the record-keeping and the claim packs it generates.

This borrows the way dashcam telematics changed motor claims. Insurers stopped arguing about fault once a timestamped video existed. The same trick has never been aimed at the packing bench, and the packing bench is exactly where the seller loses.

The pain is already paid for, twice over. Royal Mail's own compensation rules require proof of posting and evidence of the item's value before it will pay a claim, and it caps what it pays without those. Evri, DPD and Yodel all run claim processes that ask the sender for photographic evidence of packaging and contents. On the card side, the UK payments rules give the buyer the right to say the item never arrived; the seller then has to produce delivery evidence or eat the loss. Shopify, Amazon and eBay all publish seller-protection terms that hinge on what evidence the seller can show. The seller's current answer is a phone photo, if they remembered.

The thing that compounds is the record set. After a year, a seller running 400 parcels a week has a per-courier, per-route, per-SKU picture of where losses actually happen. That data belongs to the seller and to us, and no courier will ever publish it, because it is a record of the courier's own failures. It makes the next claim pack stronger and it tells the seller which courier to stop using. That is the part a rival starting fresh cannot buy.

The growth motion is the app stores. A Shopify and an Amazon listing put the software in front of sellers who already ship volume, and the rig ships as a boxed add-on. Sellers who win a claim tell other sellers, because getting money back from a courier is a story people repeat. Warehouse and 3PL operators buy several benches at once.

This commits to shape B: it serves a named ecosystem, and the platform rule that hurts is the evidence burden written into courier compensation terms and card scheme dispute rules.

UK online sellers shipping between 200 and 3,000 parcels a week from their own unit — the Shopify, Amazon and eBay operators who employ two to fifteen packers. Also small third-party fulfilment firms who need to prove to their own clients that a parcel left correctly packed.

Parcel volumes and lost-parcel disputes have both climbed, and couriers have tightened what they will accept as evidence. Royal Mail's compensation terms and the major couriers' claim forms all now ask the sender for photographs of the contents and the packaging. Card dispute rules put the burden of proof on the seller. Cameras, load cells and small computers are cheap enough that a bench rig costs a few hundred pounds rather than a few thousand.
