LicenceRoute, wins export licences before deals die.

A licensing service that gets UK deep-tech startups export approval before a US or Israeli enterprise deal falls through.

LicenceRoute is a small team of former UK export control caseworkers who classify and file dual-use export licence applications for deep-tech startups, so a live sales contract doesn't stall in the paperwork.

Here's the problem it solves. A UK startup building things like AI training chips, quantum sensors, drone autonomy software or cyber security tools often needs a licence before it can legally send its product, or even a technical briefing, to a buyer outside the UK. This comes from the Export Control Order 2008 and the UK Strategic Export Control Lists, which cover far more ordinary tech than most founders realise — sending source code or a spec sheet to an engineer in the US or Israel can count as a 'deemed export' of controlled technology. Founders usually only discover this mid-negotiation, once a defence, quantum or AI safety customer's own compliance team asks for proof of licence. At that point the deal is stuck until the Export Control Joint Unit (ECJU) processes an application through its SPIRE system, which can take weeks if you don't already know which licence you need.

Why now: UK deep-tech has had a wave of funding into exactly the categories that trigger this — AI chips, quantum computing, autonomous drones, cyber tooling — much of it aimed at US and Israeli defence and enterprise buyers, and post-Brexit the UK has tightened export enforcement and stepped up ECJU audits. More of these startups are hitting the licence question for the first time, later than they should, with a live contract on the table.

LicenceRoute charges a flat fee per classification plus a fee per licence application handled, paid by the founder who needs the deal to close, not by a compliance department that doesn't exist yet. What makes it hold: every classification the team does builds a private record of how ECJU has actually ruled on specific product features against the control list and Wassenaar Arrangement categories, and how long each type of ruling took. ECJU's own free classification tool answers general questions but won't give a fast, binding read on a novel product like a new AI model, and it structurally can't take a side in a founder's deal or rush it. The trade compliance law firms who could give a fast, confident answer bill defence primes at rates no seed-stage founder can justify for a single deal. LicenceRoute sits in the gap: cheaper and faster than a law firm, because its growing case record does in seconds what a lawyer bills hours for, and more decisive than ECJU's own tool, because it's built to get one specific deal over the line.
