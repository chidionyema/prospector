AfterInk — the tattooist's client file with the age check and healing photos your council wants.

A pocket app for UK tattooists: photo-ID age check before every tattoo, then 1-week, 1-month and 3-month healing pings so you book the touch-up in time.

The pain every UK tattooist feels now: an underage tattoo is a criminal offence under the Tattooing of Minors Act 1969, and a single missed age check can trigger studio licence revocation under the Local Government (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1982. Local councils have stepped up enforcement — Manchester shut a studio for six months in 2024, and Trading Standards ran a specific operation against suspected underage work in 2023. Without a tamper-evident record of every ID check, the tattooist has no defence when a parent complains, and the clipboard-and-paper consent form gives them nothing. Add a second, daily pain: clients often claim a tattoo "didn't take" weeks after the appointment and demand a free touch-up the artist has no way to verify. Standard tools offer no help — a paper form is unsigned, a generic booking tool (Square Appointments, Treatwell, Fresha) treats tattooing as a one-off service with no healing cycle.

The solution borrows the controlled-drug register pattern from pharmacy and the structured re-assessment cadence from physiotherapy, and applies both to every skin breach event. Before tattooing, the artist photographs the client's photo ID; the app reads the date of birth, blocks anyone under 18 (or routes through a parent-consent workflow), and stores a tamper-evident, geo-tagged timestamp of the age check. After the tattoo, the app automatically schedules an SMS or WhatsApp follow-up at 1 week, 1 month and 3 months asking the client to send a healing photo. Photos are indexed by client, body part, ink colours and placement. The artist gets a portfolio of healing outcomes they can revisit before any touch-up conversation, and an inspection-ready record of every age check for any council visit or Trading Standards inquiry.

Why it holds: the dataset compounds. After a few hundred tattoos, the artist sees which ink colours scar on which skin types, which placements (fingers, ribs, feet) lose pigment fastest, and which clients historically demand free touch-ups versus those with genuine healing issues. That proprietary healing-outcome record informs every quote and aftercare conversation. Switching costs grow with every client logged — after a year, migrating means losing the entire portfolio of consent records, ID verifications and healing photos. A regulator cannot ship this (it would not take a commercial side or hold client health data on a tattooist's behalf); general booking tools structurally cannot speak the multi-month healing cycle; and insurance underwriters (Hiscox, Allianz) ask for documented age verification at quote stage but won't build or recommend a single tool, and won't indemnify a paper form.
