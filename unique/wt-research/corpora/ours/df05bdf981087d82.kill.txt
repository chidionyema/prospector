CloseWatch, know a UK startup is closing before it's news.

A subscription briefing that flags UK startups quietly running out of money, weeks before it's public, so founders can hire the team or buy the assets first.

This borrows the way deal-intelligence services like Mergermarket break unannounced M&A deals early, using a network of small, scattered signals nobody else bothers to stitch together, and points that same mechanism at UK startup deaths instead of UK startup deals.

A solo operator runs a paid weekly briefing, plus a searchable archive, that tracks which UK startups are quietly failing before it ever becomes a headline. The raw ingredients are all public but scattered: Companies House filing changes (dormant status, director resignations, charges registered against assets), London Gazette winding-up and strike-off notices, domain and hosting expiry, sudden hiring-freeze patterns on job boards, and clusters of 'Open to Work' badges appearing on a company's LinkedIn staff list. None of these alone means much. Correlated and scored over time, with a track record of which combinations actually preceded a real closure, they become a genuinely early warning.

The reason this is worth money to a startup founder-operator is not curiosity, it's opportunity. A founder who hears three weeks early that a nearby seed-stage company is dying can message its best engineer before the layoff is public, approach its customers before a receiver does, or buy its unused SaaS credits and hardware cheap. That's a real, recurring reason to pay that has nothing to do with schadenfreude.

Why it holds: The London Gazette and Companies House only publish once a company has formally filed for dissolution or insolvency, which is weeks to months behind the real moment a team stops paying rent and starts job-hunting, and neither register has any mandate to interpret or predict, only to record. Trade press like Sifted, TechCrunch or UKTN only has a commercial reason to cover the twenty or so highest-profile shutdowns a year, not the hundreds of quieter seed-stage closures that matter for hiring and asset-buying. What compounds here is the operator's own scored history of which signal combinations were right, built up briefing after briefing, plus direct human verification calls before anything is published, which a scraped or generic register can never claim.

UK startup founder-operators at pre-seed to Series A companies, typically running teams of 5 to 40 people, who pay a monthly subscription (around £49 to £99) because they are doing their own hiring and business development without a dedicated recruiter or corp-dev person.

UK startup closures have been running at multi-year highs through 2024 and 2025 as venture funding tightened and the runway from COVID-era SEIS and EIS-backed 2020-2021 rounds finally ran out. At the same time, Companies House's new identity verification rules under the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 are pushing a steady wave of struck-off and non-compliant companies through the public register, giving a live, machine-readable trickle of distress signals to correlate against.
