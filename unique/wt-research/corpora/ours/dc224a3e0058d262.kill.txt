Georgia state bid protest binder for GovTech agencies.

A ready-to-file bid protest evidence binder for GovTech agencies that lost a Georgia state procurement, sold at a fixed fee per protest.

Georgia's Department of Administrative Services (DOAS) runs a state-level procurement process with its own bid-protest procedure under O.C.G.A. § 50-5-60 et seq. — distinct from the federal GAO bid-protest process that GovTech agencies are familiar with. Vendors that lose a state bid have a tight window — often 10 days from award — to file a protest and need a binder that satisfies DOAS's evidentiary specificity. Most GovTech agencies reach for a law firm at $400 per hour or more, but for sub-$1 million bids the math does not work and the protest is not filed.

The service transplants the prosecutorial indictment-grade evidence binder model: every element of the protest is documented in a sealed, cite-checked binder with exhibits, citations to Georgia procurement code, and a procedural playbook that maps the case to the DOAS review officer's expectations. Sold at a fixed fee per protest — typically $4,500 — the pack ships as a filing-ready PDF and a digital evidence locker that supports ongoing DOAS engagement.

What compounds here is technical IP: the proprietary protest template library and procedural playbook, which compile dozens of Georgia DOAS protest decisions and surface the procedural patterns that win. A law firm at hourly rates is uneconomic for sub-$1M bids, which represent most of the Georgia GovTech pipeline; GAO protest templates target the federal process and miss Georgia's state-specific procedural quirks; no national firm has compiled a Georgia DOAS-specific precedent library at scale.
