PitchPerfect Pods — the mobile podcast recording booth that parks outside small business events across a UK city.

A soundproofed recording pod on a trailer that turns up at UK trade shows and business parks, so small firms walk out with a finished podcast episode the same day.

This is a local service run by one person with a van, a trailer-mounted acoustic booth, and two microphones. You park at business parks, chambers of commerce breakfasts, county shows and trade fairs. Small business owners book a 30-minute slot, walk in, and record. You hand them a mixed, captioned episode within 24 hours for £180 to £350 a slot.

The angle borrows from the mobile blood donation unit and the photo booth at a wedding. Both work because the thing people keep putting off becomes trivially easy when it physically arrives where they already are. Podcast recording has the same shape: thousands of UK small firms have wanted a podcast or a guest appearance for years and never booked a studio, because a studio means a separate journey, a separate day, and a decision.

Why a gig-economy person in their twenties can run this: no licence is needed, the kit is roughly £4,000 to £7,000 including a second-hand box trailer, and the editing is the part AI now does in minutes rather than hours. You are selling a room and a deadline, not audio engineering.

Distribution is owned, not bought. UK Chambers of Commerce, Federation of Small Businesses branches, and BID (Business Improvement District) teams run recurring member events and are constantly short of something to put on. You offer the booth free to the organiser and charge the attendees. That is a captive channel a rival cannot simply outbid you for, because the organiser only wants one booth and books the person they already trust.

The thing that holds is the calendar, not the kit. Once you have the recurring dates for a city's event circuit — the monthly FSB breakfast, the two county shows, the four business park open days — a copycat is bidding for slots that are already taken 12 months out. That is real switching cost for the organiser, who has to re-brief and re-risk a new supplier.

A sharper version: sell the slot as a recruitment tool rather than marketing. UK firms in trades, care and hospitality are struggling to hire, and a two-minute video of a real employee talking about the job outperforms a job advert. Same booth, same day, different buyer inside the business.

Owners of UK small businesses with roughly 3 to 50 staff who attend chamber and FSB events — accountancy practices, recruiters, commercial cleaners, IFAs, care agencies, building firms. They pay £180 to £350 per recording slot, on the day, by card. Event organisers pay nothing and get the booth as a free attraction.

Recording and editing costs collapsed. Automatic transcription, captioning and audio cleanup that used to need a paid engineer are now built into everyday tools, so a solo operator can turn a session round overnight. At the same time UK in-person business events came back hard after the pandemic years, and organisers are competing on what they can offer members. Second-hand acoustic booths and box trailers are cheap and plentiful.
