Compliance Fire Drill — the mock Home Office visit that keeps a startup's visa sponsor licence alive.

A team of ex-UKVI compliance officers runs unannounced, realistic sponsor licence audits on tech startups that hire overseas staff, before the Home Office does its own.

Any UK startup that sponsors Skilled Worker visas holds a sponsor licence from UK Visas and Immigration, and that licence can be suspended or revoked after a single bad compliance visit. Since 13 February 2024 the civil penalty for employing someone without the right to work has risen to £45,000 per worker for a first breach and £60,000 for a repeat one, and the Home Office has been running more of these visits, often unannounced, as it leans harder on sponsors after ending free movement. For a scaling startup that just recruited engineers from abroad, losing the licence does not just mean a fine — it can mean every sponsored employee's visa is at risk, and hiring internationally stops overnight. This service turns up at a client's office the way UKVI would: checking right-to-work files, HR records, and reporting duties on the Sponsor Management System, then hands over a fix list before the real visit happens. The government cannot build this itself, because the same body that enforces the rules cannot also coach companies on how to pass its own test — that would be a conflict of interest baked into the job. Generic immigration law firm retainers offer advice on paper, but they usually see one client at a time and rarely run the audit itself. This service is different because every mock audit adds to a private record of exactly which documents, worker categories, and reporting gaps UKVI flagged across dozens of clients that quarter — so as enforcement gets stricter and shifts focus (say, from care sector sponsors one year to tech sponsors the next), the checklist gets sharper before any public guidance catches up. The harder Home Office pushes, the more a founder needs to know what it will actually check next, and that pattern only exists inside a firm that has run the drill on enough companies to see it forming.
