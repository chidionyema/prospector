RideCheck – The PHV Driver’s MOT Downtime Income.

A parametric income protection product for taxi and private hire drivers that pays a daily cash benefit when their vehicle is off‑road due to an MOT failure, using telematics partnerships to select low‑risk drivers.

As the DVSA tightens MOT standards (especially on emissions), more cabs fail and sit idle, costing drivers their entire day’s income. A solo operator can structure a simple parametric payout (fixed sum per day, verified by a failed‑MOT certificate) and, crucially, partner with telematics providers to underprice the risk by only enrolling drivers whose vehicle health data predicts a low failure probability. Incumbent insurers cannot access this data at scale for this micro‑segment.

Self‑employed private hire and black‑cab drivers.

DVSA’s rolling MOT regime toughening (e.g. Euro 6 diesel checks) is increasing failure rates for older purpose‑built hackney carriages, while platforms like Uber offer no income safety net.

The passages only define what an MOT test is and how to check one, and separately show that gig-driver insurance products (Zego for Uber Eats) and Uber's own driver requirements exist, but none of them describe whether an MOT-failure income-protection payout is durable, already offered, or commoditised by any provider.

Zego is described as a tech-driven insurer using telematics to deliver tailored policies specifically for UK private hire and gig-economy drivers, and is trusted by over half a million UK drivers — a well-funded incumbent already using the same telematics-based approach on this exact driver segment. That undercuts the claim that this space is open and that only a new entrant could access telematics data to underwrite PHV drivers cheaply.
