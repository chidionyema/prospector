CutList Arbiter — The Independent Joinery & Kitchen-Fitting Shop's Sheet-Goods Yield & Off-Spec Board Reject Bench.

A vertical desktop tool that, before a single cut is made, nests a shop's job on the actual boards it received, measures each delivered pack's real thickness/moisture/bow against BS EN 14322 tolerance, and prints both the optimised cut list and a merchant-facing off-spec reject claim.

Lens: first_principles. Stripped to the raw pain, a 3-person joinery shop's margin is not lost on labour rates but on two invisible leaks: (a) it quotes sheet goods at a guessed 15-20% waste factor and nests by eye on the saw, and (b) it silently absorbs packs of MFC/MDF that arrive out of tolerance on thickness, bow or moisture, because rejecting a pack means proving the defect and losing the delivery slot. Both leaks happen in the same 20 minutes at goods-in. So build the bench that owns those 20 minutes: the operator logs each delivered pack with a cheap digital calliper and pin moisture meter into the tool; the tool nests that specific job's parts onto the boards actually in the rack (grain-direction locked, edge-banding allowance per profile, saw-kerf per blade), outputs a numbered cut sequence to the panel saw, AND flags every pack whose measured values sit outside declared tolerance, generating a dated, photo-anchored reject/credit note citing the specific clause. Each job logged deepens a shop-private dataset of measured yield-per-board-per-supplier and defect-rate-per-supplier-per-batch, which then rewrites the waste factor in the next quote from that shop's own history instead of a trade rule of thumb. Structurally a vertical tool, not a broker: no transaction is intermediated, monetisation is a per-seat annual licence, and the moat is the accumulating measured-yield record that lives inside one shop's workflow.

Owner-operators of UK independent joinery and bespoke kitchen/fitted-furniture workshops with 1-20 staff running a beam or vertical panel saw and buying 20+ sheets a week from a regional board merchant (e.g. Meyer, Hanson Plywood, local branch of a timber factor) — the person who both cuts and quotes. £45/month per seat, or £490/year, with an optional £150 setup where the operator's existing profile library and edge-banding stock are loaded in.

Panel product prices and their volatility stepped up hard through the post-2021 timber cycle and have not returned to the old flat baseline, so a mis-guessed waste factor now moves a £6k kitchen quote by hundreds rather than tens; at the same time merchants moved to thinner-margin, faster-turn stock and pack-level quality complaints rose. Cheap Bluetooth callipers and pin moisture meters are now under £60, and consumer-grade laptops can run 2D nesting optimisation locally that used to require a CNC vendor's licensed CAM seat — so measurement-at-goods-in became affordable to a shop with no CNC at exactly the moment guessing became expensive.
