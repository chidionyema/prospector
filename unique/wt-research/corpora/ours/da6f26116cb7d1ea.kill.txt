FlexCover — The Shift-Worker Carer's Micro-Sitter Broker.

A geographically-bounded transaction broker matching working primary carers (NHS band 2-5, gig drivers, hospitality shift workers) with personally-vetted micro-sitters for 2-6 hour ad-hoc childcare slots traditional childminders refuse, taking 22% from each booking arranged.

First-principles: the structural mismatch between nursery hours (9-3, fixed blocks, 4-week booking cycles) and shift-work schedules (irregular, 4-12 hour notice, single-shift patterns) creates a market failure that platforms structurally cannot fill. Generous micro-sitter supply exists — phased retirees (1.5m over-65s in work, up 2x since 2010), final-year uni students, secondary-earner parents — but the unit economics of a £10-12/hr × 3-hour booking cannot support 30% platform fees and 24/7 customer support. A solo broker who personally curates 30-50 micro-sitters within 2-3 postcodes via DBS/reference/first-aid vetting, manually matches to a steady cohort of 15-25 working-carer households, sits the first session to verify safety, and takes 22% of booking value reaches break-even at ~22 bookings/month with no external capital. The trust-density required for this transaction is structurally anti-platform.

Working primary carers on irregular hours — NHS band 2-5 staff, Deliveroo/Uber drivers, retail/hospitality shift workers, school-mealtime supervisors, zero-hours agency carers — earning £22,000-£38,000/year who cannot afford £130-180/week nursery placement and need cover at 6-48 hours' notice.

Nursery costs up ~25% in three years forcing more parents toward wraparound/irregular solutions; 27% of UK workers now on irregular hours post-pandemic; Carer's Allowance recipients needing supplementary income to escape the poverty-trap earnings threshold; Paediatric First Aid courses are now £45/half-day (mass-supply enabling a deep micro-sitter pool); UK phased-retirement trend reshaping local labour availability.
