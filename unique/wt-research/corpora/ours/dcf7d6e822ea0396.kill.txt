RateWatch Redress — proves your mortgage lender should have offered you a better deal, and builds the claim for you.

A self-serve tool for homeowners rolling off a cheap fixed mortgage that checks whether their lender broke its new duty to treat them fairly, then builds the compensation claim.

Millions of UK homeowners who fixed their mortgage rate in 2019-2021 are now rolling onto today's far higher rates. Many lenders quietly let existing customers drift onto the expensive standard variable rate instead of offering the cheaper deal they were giving other customers, including brand new ones, on that exact same day.

Since the FCA's Consumer Duty came into force, lenders have a specific duty to actively deliver fair value to existing customers, not just make better deals available if someone happens to ask. Most homeowners have no way to prove what deal they should have been offered on the day their fix ended, so the duty goes unenforced in practice.

RateWatch Redress asks the homeowner for their mortgage details and the date their fixed rate ended, then checks its own growing record of what each major lender was actually offering new and existing customers around that date. It shows the pound gap between what the customer got and what a fair deal that day would have cost, drafts a Consumer Duty complaint letter, and, if the lender doesn't pay within eight weeks, prepares the Financial Ombudsman Service referral with the evidence attached.

The record gets better with every customer: people upload screenshots of the actual rate-switching options their banking app showed them that week, building a per-lender, per-date archive that isn't published anywhere else. Neither the FCA nor the Financial Ombudsman Service can build this themselves. The FCA writes and polices the fair-value rule but, as the regulator, can't gather lender-specific evidence to help one customer's claim against a named firm. MoneyHelper gives free generic guidance but holds no product-level data and has no reason to build a case against a specific lender.

The squeezed-middle homeowner: a professional aged 35-55 coming off a fixed mortgage rate taken out before rates rose, feeling the jump in their monthly payment against a budget already stretched by school fees or childcare, often deciding jointly with a partner. They pay a small fixed fee for the report checking whether they have a case, then a percentage of whatever the lender or the Ombudsman actually pays out, so there's little cost upfront while money is tight.

The Bank of England base rate rose from 0.1% in December 2021 to a peak of 5.25% in 2023, so homeowners who fixed at 1-2% are rolling onto far higher rates right through 2024-2026 as their deals end. The FCA's Consumer Duty took effect for open products on 31 July 2023 and for closed products on 31 July 2024, creating for the first time an enforceable duty for lenders to deliver fair value to existing customers, and the FCA has since flagged mortgage loyalty-penalty practices as a supervisory priority.
