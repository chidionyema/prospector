FootCare Walk-In — the high-street foot care clinic for elderly and disabled people, booked by their primary carers.

A chain of high-street foot care clinics where primary carers book same-week 30-minute toenail and foot care appointments for the relative they look after.

Primary carers of elderly or disabled relatives often cannot find regular foot care for the person they support. NHS community chiropody is rationed and routinely has three to six month waits for non-diabetic patients. Private chiropodists are scattered, hard to find, and charge £50-80 per home visit with no guaranteed continuity. Yet foot care matters: untreated calluses, ingrown toenails and missed foot infections cause falls, avoidable hospital admissions and, in people with diabetes, amputation.

FootCare Walk-In is a chain of small high-street foot care clinics (around 600 sq ft each, in suburban town centres near bus routes and pharmacies) that offer a fixed 30-minute toenail cut, callus file, foot soak and moisturise for £45 per visit, with no clinical assessment, no GP referral, and same-week appointments. Each shop has two trained foot-care assistants and a part-time supervisor, taking six to eight clients an hour across four treatment stations. A membership card at £20 a month includes one appointment and 10% off additional visits.

The mechanism borrows from the high-street nail-bar chain: the same fixed transparent pricing, quick 30-minute turnaround, branded boutique interior, monthly subscription loyalty, and suburban high-street sites that brands like Margaret Dabbs, Townhouse and Cowshed have used to dominate the cosmetic nail market. That model has not been applied to older and disabled clients, even though their need is more clinical and more urgent. Once a shop has 400 monthly members, the recurring local density and the personal relationship between client and assistant create a defensible base that a new entrant on the same high street cannot easily replicate.

The service is personal grooming, not a regulated activity, so no CQC or HCPC registration is required. Public liability insurance, basic DBS checks, infection-control training and single-use instruments handle the risk profile.

Primary carers (mostly women aged 45-70) looking after elderly or diabetic relatives at home, household income £25-60k, who want predictable regular foot care for the relative without chasing NHS appointments or paying £80 for one-off private visits.

ONS Census 2021 records 5.7 million unpaid carers in England, with 1.4 million providing 50 or more hours a week. NHS chiropody waiting lists for non-diabetic patients commonly exceed three months, according to Healthwatch England's 2023 survey. Diabetes UK reports 4.3 million people in the UK living with diabetes, all needing regular foot checks to prevent ulcers and amputation. The nail-bar sector has matured with over 600 branded sites in the UK, proving the chain economics; foot care for older people is a clear gap no chain has filled.

The existence of a 25-year-old private chiropody business and demand infrastructure (NHS finder, Age UK services, self-referral forms) demonstrates that buyers are already paying and searching for this category of care.

Multiple passages describe UK unpaid carers as being in 'acute financial hardship' [10ceda9f6424916f], 'financially precarious situations' and 'less able to cope with additional costs' [e6934e95decceba2], and falling into 'financial misery' from the cost-of-living crisis [6fd7ac6dacde0ff2], with even Carer's Allowance at £67.60/week seen as inadequate. For a discretionary non-clinical service at £45 per visit or £20/month, this paints a target segment under severe financial strain, weighing against the ability and willingness to pay.
