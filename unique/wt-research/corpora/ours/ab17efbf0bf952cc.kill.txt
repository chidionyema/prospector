PayPace, know how fast a client pays before you invoice.

PayPace tells UK freelance creatives how fast a prospective client actually pays, before they take the job, using payment-speed data pooled from other freelancers who've invoiced that same client.

Freelance designers, writers, developers and consultants send an invoice and then just wait. If a client is going to pay in 9 days or 90, the freelancer usually only finds out after the work is done and they're stuck chasing it. IPSE, the body that represents the UK's self-employed, has repeatedly flagged late payment as one of the biggest financial stresses freelancers face.

PayPace flips the timing round. Before taking on a new client, a freelancer types the client's name in and sees how long that client has actually taken to pay other freelancers in the past — not a generic industry average, but a real number tied to that named business.

The data comes from freelancers themselves. When someone connects their invoicing tool (Xero, FreeAgent, QuickBooks, or a bank feed through Open Banking), PayPace reads their own paid and overdue invoices and adds the payment pattern for each client to a shared, anonymised pool. A freelancer only sees a client's score once at least three other freelancers have logged invoices to that same client, which pushes people to invite others who've worked with the same agency or brand so the record fills in faster. That invite step is the entire growth engine — no ads, no outbound sales.

This only works because it's a record built across many freelancers' clients, not one person's own accounts. Xero, FreeAgent and QuickBooks can already tell a freelancer that one invoice to one client is 40 days overdue. They can't tell a freelancer, before any work starts, that this same client has quietly taken 65 days on average to pay six other freelancers this year. Building that means pooling data across many competing customer accounts on the same platform, which cuts against the tenant-by-tenant isolation these tools are built and audited on — and it would mean showing one paying customer's payment behaviour to another paying customer who's about to become their supplier. None of the three has a reason to build that against itself.

PayPace is free to connect and log your own invoices; £12 a month unlocks live client scores, with a higher tier for small studios tracking cash flow across several freelancers. Distribution runs through the Xero App Store, the FreeAgent App Marketplace and the QuickBooks App Store, where freelance creatives already look for tools that plug into their invoicing.

Freelance creatives themselves — designers, copywriters, developers and consultants — who pay £12 to £25 a month once they want to see live client payment-speed scores rather than just contribute their own invoice data for free.

Open Banking rules now let a freelancer share their bank feed with one click instead of typing invoices in by hand, and the Xero, FreeAgent and QuickBooks app marketplaces have matured into places freelance creatives already browse for add-on tools — both are what make a pooled, cross-freelancer payment database practical for the first time.
