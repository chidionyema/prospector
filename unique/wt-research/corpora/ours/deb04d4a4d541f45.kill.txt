ShiftBay — a secure van yard by the fire station that has your trade job ready to go when your shift ends.

A staffed yard next to a fire station that stores a firefighter's trade van and tools, and restocks them with pre-ordered materials timed to the end of a night shift.

Most firefighters work a pattern of two days, two nights, then four off, and a large share run a trade — plumbing, electrics, building — in those days off. Fire and rescue authorities have been closing and consolidating stations for years, so fewer crews now have anywhere secure on site to keep a work van or a set of tools between shifts. The realistic options are driving the van home, which many stations discourage or ban for liveried vehicles, or losing an hour each changeover fetching kit before a job can start.

This is a small staffed yard within a few minutes of one or two specific stations: secure parking for the trade van, a lockable bay for tools and stock, and a simple ordering line so a firefighter can text or app in what materials they need for tomorrow's job before they go on their last night shift. The operator collects the order from a local builders' merchant overnight and has the van loaded and ready at the gate for 8am, when the shift ends. Add-ons — battery charging for power tools, a quick jet-wash of the van, secure key handover for a mate's tools — turn it into a habitual stop rather than a one-off rental.

The value doesn't come from the storage itself, which is a commodity. It comes from timing the whole handover to a fire shift rota that a generic storage chain has no reason to learn, and from the trust that builds inside a watch: firefighting crews are famously tight-knit, and one satisfied user on a watch tends to bring the rest of that watch and the next crew that rotates through the same station. That referral pattern, plus a firefighter's own routine hardening around a specific site over months of use, is what a copycat can't just rent space and replicate.

The individual firefighter, paying a monthly fee for the van and tool bay out of the extra income their trade already brings in, plus a small per-order charge for materials collection and prep. It's a personal spend against a personal side income, not something that needs employer or fire authority sign-off.

Firefighter pay has been squeezed through repeated real-terms pay disputes since 2022, pushing more crews to rely on trade income on their days off, at the same time as fire authorities have been shrinking station numbers and on-site space under budget pressure — so the storage that used to exist informally at the station is disappearing just as the need for it is growing.
