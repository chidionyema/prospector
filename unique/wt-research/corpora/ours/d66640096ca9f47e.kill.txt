Spine-Point Forensics — The Public-Sector Pay-Disc Audit.

A solo operator who visits NHS, council and school staff at home to audit 12–24 months of payslips against Agenda for Change, NJC and Teachers' Pay Scale spine points, building a per-employer error dossier used to recover systematic underpayments via grievance then tribunal.

Public-sector payroll drifts from the published spine-point/band frameworks through soft, recurring underpayments — missing high-cost area supplements, miscounted unsocial-hours enhancements, wrong pension banding, wrong overtime rates, omitted recruitment-and-retention premia. Each Trust and council has a recognisable error signature because the same shared-services payroll desk processes everyone. A solo operator can fingerprint that signature from a worker's payslips, file a grievance that HR rarely refuses once the line-by-line discrepancies are itemised against the published AfC/NJC/TPS pay circular, then escalate to ACAS and ultimately the Employment Tribunal. The home visit is the operational wedge: HR will dismiss a worker self-auditing, but a forensic dossier cross-referenced line-by-line against the public pay circular carries weight, and the operator's compounding per-employer signature dataset means each subsequent audit at the same employer takes hours instead of days.

Individual NHS Band 3–7 staff (nurses, AHPs, healthcare science, admin and clerical on AfC), teachers on the main and upper pay scales, and council NJC-graded employees typically earning £28k–£45k who suspect — or come to suspect after a colleague's audit — that they have been chronically underpaid. Pay structure: £250 fixed audit fee per worker plus 18–25% of identified recovery; tribunal-stage escalation carries a separate success fee.

Four converging 2025–2026 signals: (1) the post-NHS-strike and post-2024 teacher-pay-deal environment has normalised payslip self-scrutiny as a worker behaviour; (2) the AfC 2023–25 multi-year deal is still being applied inconsistently by many Trusts during the back-propagation phase; (3) consolidation into NHS Shared Business Services, Midlands & Lancashire CSU, and the local-government shared payroll hubs has stripped out the in-house payroll officer who used to catch errors before pay day, replacing them with a national helpdesk; (4) HMRC's real-time PAYE/APIT feeds now let a solo with a laptop reconcile every line of a payslip in minutes against the operator's own rules engine, where ten years ago this needed a chartered accountant on £300/hour.
