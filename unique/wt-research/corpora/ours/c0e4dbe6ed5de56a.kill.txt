Georgia film tax credit findings, for production software.

A feed of Georgia Department of Revenue audit findings on film tax credit claims, structured for production accounting software vendors so their customers avoid denied claims.

Georgia's film tax credit (O.C.G.A. § 48-7-40.26) gives a 30% transferable credit on qualified production spend. Georgia is the #1 US film production destination, with 400-plus productions spending $4B-plus annually. The Georgia Department of Revenue audits film tax credit claims heavily, and denied claims can result in five- to seven-figure loss per production.

Production accounting software vendors (Movie Magic Budgeting, Showbiz Planning, Greenslate, EP Budgeting) serve Georgia productions. Their customers ask them, before spending on a budget category, whether that spend will survive a DOR audit.

The product is a feed of DOR film tax credit audit findings, classified by spend category (above-the-line talent, below-the-line crew, equipment rental, post-production, location fees), with denial rates and the specific DOR language used to deny. Recurring trigger: each new DOR audit report (quarterly); each new spend category in a production budget. Public source: Georgia DOR Tax Credit and Incentive Reports, Georgia Department of Economic Development film office statistics, and Georgia Open Records Act responses on denied claims.

Built from the raw constraint, not the playbook: the real signal is that DOR has a specific audit playbook — it scrutinizes loan-out corporation arrangements, above-the-line payments to non-Georgia residents, and "qualified production spend" interpretations. The product surfaces that playbook from past denials; that parsed history is the durable advantage.

This is not a Georgia DOR filing tool (the DOR's free first-party portal processes claims). It is the intelligence layer that comes from parsing past audit denials — a dataset the DOR will never publish in structured form.
