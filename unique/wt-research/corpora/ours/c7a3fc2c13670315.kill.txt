AsbestosStop Pay — daily income when plumbers, electricians and fitters legally have to stop work after finding asbestos.

Pays plumbers, electricians, kitchen fitters and heating engineers a fixed amount for every working day they lose when an asbestos find forces a legal work stoppage.

Every working day a sole trader loses to a legal work stoppage is a day they don't bill. Under the Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012, when a plumber, electrician, kitchen fitter, bathroom fitter or heating engineer opens a wall, ceiling or floor in a pre-2000 home and finds suspected asbestos-containing material, the law says stop work immediately, isolate the area and bring in an HSE-licensed asbestos removal contractor. The stoppage usually runs three to fourteen days. The tradesperson cannot bill the client (the discovery is no one's fault), cannot easily switch to another site (their tools and kit are committed), and has no cover for it.

AsbestosStop Pay pays a fixed daily amount, around £120 to £180 a day for up to fourteen days, triggered the moment the tradesperson uploads two pieces of evidence in the app: a photo of the suspected material in its original position, and an invoice or quote from an HSE-licensed removal contractor for the same address. No claims assessor, no waiting period, no medical evidence. The legal duty to stop is the trigger itself, and the licensed contractor is the verifier.

The reason it works now is that the regulator is getting more aggressive, not less. HSE's asbestos enforcement has stepped up since Grenfell, the Building Safety Act 2022 created a new Building Safety Regulator with explicit duties around asbestos in higher-risk buildings, and a 2023 HSE-commissioned survey found that the majority of tradespeople still do not check for asbestos before opening up. As enforcement tightens, more stop-work events get properly reported, the network of licensed contractors grows, and the dataset of verifiable events gets sharper. This is what flips in the tradesperson's favour: the product's value compounds as the regulator gets harder.

What holds it in place is that the HSE cannot sell income protection to the people it regulates, and the big insurers (Hiscox, Direct Line, Aviva, NFU Mutual) cannot price this for a sole trader at a weekly premium of about ten pounds. Their underwriting cost is fixed at a level the unit economics of a micro-premium product cannot support, and their existing income-protection policies carry thirty to ninety day waiting periods and exclude work-stoppage events entirely. The licensed asbestos contractors are not incentivised to share stop-work data with incumbents either, so a specialist builds a proprietary dataset: every verified stoppage adds a row of (postcode, ACM type, contractor, duration, removal cost) that no incumbent can replicate. Switching cost builds quietly over time, because twelve months of paid-up cover with no claim is hard to walk away from.
