CalTrue, colour calibration rounds for freelance editors.

A small team of technicians who visit UK freelance video editors and colourists at home to calibrate their monitors, rooms and audio so their work passes broadcast delivery checks.

Freelance editors and colourists working from a spare room or garden studio are increasingly delivering finished work straight to broadcasters and streamers, not through a facility. BBC, ITV, Channel 4 and Netflix all set technical delivery rules through the Digital Production Partnership's AS-11 standard and colour specs like ITU-R BT.709 and BT.2020. If a freelancer's monitor is out of calibration, or their room has the wrong ambient light, their master can be rejected at quality control, costing them the invoice and the relationship.

A chain of local technicians, organised city by city (starting in London, Manchester, Bristol and Cardiff, where most UK indie production companies cluster), drives a set route of home studios. Each visit calibrates the screen with proper viewing-geometry and ambient-light checks, tests the audio monitoring against broadcast loudness standards, and issues a dated compliance certificate the editor can attach to a delivery or show a commissioning QC team before they even hire.

This is built first-principles: the freelancer's actual problem isn't 'I need a colorimeter', it's 'I need proof, today, that my suite will pass', which a self-service gadget can't give them. The chain keeps a calibration history per client — drift patterns, room changes, past certificates — so re-booking with the same team is faster and more trusted than starting over with anyone else, and over time the certificate itself becomes something production companies recognise by name.
