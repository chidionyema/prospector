Renovation Glitch Hunter.

A productized service that walks through a recently renovated home with a calibrated toolkit of non-destructive tests (thermal, acoustic, moisture) to find the specific defects that will become expensive warranty claims or insurance disputes within 18 months, then documents them in a legally-admissible report.

Home renovation is dominated by a power asymmetry: the contractor has a structural information advantage over the homeowner. After the final payment, the homeowner discovers defects they cannot prove. By analogically transferring the 'pre-purchase vehicle inspection' business model (a neutral third party finds latent defects before the buyer's payment clears) into renovation snagging, a solo operator can capture the acute pain of a homeowner who just paid $80k for a kitchen remodel and isn't sure the waterproofing was done right. The owner's own anxiety, plus the credible threat of a lawyer's letter if defects are found, forces the contractor to re-do the work without legal escalation.

Homeowners in the USA and UK who have just completed a renovation costing $30k+ and have not yet signed the final completion certificate or made the final payment. This is a solvent, motivated, one-time buyer with an acute pain and budget authority.

Post-COVID renovation boom means a huge cohort of homes were renovated by overbooked contractors with inconsistent quality. Simultaneously, social media has made homeowners hyper-aware of 'renovation nightmares' (the GFE, or 'Gut Feel Error', is replaced by documented fear). Insurance subrogation clauses are tightening, pushing liability back to the homeowner if latent defects aren't documented promptly.
