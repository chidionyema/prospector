ClaimBridge, cash cover when HMRC delays your R&D credit.

A fixed cash payment for early-stage UK startups whose R&D tax credit claim is stuck in an HMRC check past 8 weeks, paid with no argument about who's right.

Most seed and Series A startups plan their cash runway around an R&D tax credit landing on a certain date. Since HMRC tightened compliance in April 2023 (the mandatory Additional Information Form) and moved to the merged R&D scheme in April 2024, a much larger share of claims now get pulled into a compliance check before payment. The National Audit Office flagged an estimated £1.13bn of error and fraud in 2020-21 R&D claims alone, and HMRC's own response was to push enquiry rates up sharply from roughly 1% before the crackdown to over 20% in some claim categories. A startup that budgeted for the money in March can end up waiting until August with no clear reason and no say in the process.

ClaimBridge borrows the trigger-based payout used in flight-delay insurance, where you get paid the moment the plane is late, not after a dispute about whose fault it was, and applies the same idea to HMRC's own published claim-processing statistics. A startup buys a one-off contract at the time it files its claim. If HMRC's own average clearance-time data (which HMRC publishes) shows the claim has passed the normal processing window, the startup gets paid out automatically, funded by a UK-regulated insurance carrier acting as capacity behind the product, run day-to-day as a managing general agent under FCA appointed-representative arrangements. Nobody assesses whether the underlying claim is valid, so there's no argument, no loss adjuster and no waiting on HMRC's final decision.

What makes this hold rather than just being a bridge loan in a new coat: every policy sold adds a real, matched data point pairing a claim's characteristics (sector, claim size, first-time versus repeat claimant, which HMRC itself treats as a risk flag) against the actual delay or rejection outcome. That loss-experience dataset is not something HMRC publishes at the individual level and not something a lender needs to build, because a lender's business is repayment, not pricing a peril. As the book of policies grows, ClaimBridge's pricing on the riskiest claim types gets sharper than any new entrant could manage without years of the same claim-level data, and the FCA appointed-representative status needed to distribute an insurance product is itself a real gate a plain fintech tool doesn't have to clear.

Seed to Series A UK startups with an active R&D tax credit claim worth more than roughly £20,000, bought at filing time by the founder-operator or the fractional CFO who's already watching the cash runway, as a one-off premium worth a few percent of the expected claim.

HMRC's compliance crackdown on R&D claims, which started in earnest in 2022 after the NAO's fraud findings and hardened further with the mandatory pre-claim notification and Additional Information Form rules from August 2023, has pushed both the enquiry rate and the average time to payment up at exactly the moment more early-stage companies than ever are relying on the merged scheme introduced in April 2024.

HMRC's own 2024 statistics show 9,700 of 61,000 R&D claims were pulled into compliance checks, and HMRC states it has sharply increased scrutiny due to fraud concerns, confirming the underlying delay/review risk is real, current, and growing rather than fabricated or fading.
