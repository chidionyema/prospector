ReSellShield — the tool that checks a buyer's history across Vinted, Depop and eBay before a seller posts their address.

A subscription browser tool for Gen Z sellers on Vinted, Depop and eBay that checks a buyer's record before you ship, and flags scam patterns other sellers have already reported.

Gen Z sellers running a resale side hustle on Vinted, Depop and eBay lose money to buyers who claim an item never arrived, claim it wasn't as described, or open a chargeback after the seller has already posted it. Each platform only sees its own side of that buyer, so someone who scams sellers on Vinted can move straight to Depop under a new account with no record following them. ReSellShield is a browser extension and app a seller runs before confirming a sale: it checks the buyer's username, delivery address and payment details against a shared list of scam reports from other sellers, across all three platforms, and gives a plain 'safe / caution / high risk' flag before the seller pays for postage.

This borrows the way banks and card networks catch repeat fraudsters by pooling reports across many merchants, rather than each shop checking only its own till roll. No single resale platform sees fraud that hops between Vinted, Depop, eBay and Facebook Marketplace, but a pool of sellers reporting the same buyer's address or delivery pattern can. The more sellers use it, the faster a new scam account gets flagged, because the first few reports protect everyone who checks that buyer afterwards.

UK sellers now have real reason to treat this as a business, not a hobby. Since January 2024, HMRC's platform-reporting rules have required Vinted, Depop and eBay to report seller earnings and transaction counts to HMRC, with first reports issued in January 2025 — casual sellers who used to shrug off a scam now have an income figure on file with HMRC and a much stronger reason to protect it. Vinted also rolled out identity verification for higher-volume UK sellers through 2024, and consumer coverage from Which? and MoneySavingExpert has flagged a rise in fake-tracking and item-swap scams hitting resale sellers specifically, with no seller-side screening from the platforms themselves.

What keeps this defensible is the shared scam list itself. It only becomes useful once enough sellers are feeding it reports, and once a seller's own recent scam history sits inside the tool they open every time they post an item, they have little reason to switch to a rival that starts from zero reports. A new entrant has to convince a critical mass of sellers to hand over report data before their version is worth anything at all.
