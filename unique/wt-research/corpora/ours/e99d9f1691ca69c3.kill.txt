ChargeDelta, cuts the AI bill your investors question.

A fixed-fee monthly service for UK software companies that rebuilds each product feature's real AI model cost and returns a signed cut plan the finance lead can act on.

Start from the raw pain, not from how anyone solves it today. A UK software company ships twelve features that call a language model. The invoice from the model provider arrives as one number in US dollars. Nobody in the building can say which feature caused it.

This is a productized service, not software. Each month we take read-only access to the company's model provider usage export, its own request logs, and its billing data. A small team plus our own tooling reconstructs cost per feature, per customer, and per prompt version. We hand back one document: what each feature costs to serve, which customers are served at a loss, and a ranked list of specific changes with the pounds saved next to each.

This commits to shape C, sells-to-tech. The buyer is the VP Engineering or Head of Platform inside a UK software company of 30 to 300 staff. The recurring trigger is the monthly board or investor pack, where gross margin must be explained. The deliverable is the signed cut plan.

The public source that proves this is already paid for: UK company law requires every company to file accounts at Companies House, and mid-sized filers disclose cost of sales. Investor-backed software companies report gross margin to their boards monthly. Anthropic, OpenAI and Google all publish per-token prices publicly, so the input prices are knowable but the attribution is not.
