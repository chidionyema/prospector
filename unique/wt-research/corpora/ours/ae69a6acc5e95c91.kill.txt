QuarterlyFire Visit — the quarterly fire safety check that keeps your pub, takeaway or HMO inspection-ready, with photo evidence your fire officer accepts.

A mobile crew visits your small commercial premises every three months to check fire doors, alarms, extinguishers, emergency lighting and escape signage, and gives you a photo-tagged report you can hand to any inspector.

UK small commercial premises — pubs, takeaways, restaurants, small hotels, B&Bs, nurseries and small HMOs — face constant fire safety inspections. Fire doors need quarterly checks, alarms need regular testing, extinguishers need annual servicing, emergency lighting needs ongoing testing, and escape routes and signage need continuous attention. Most small operators juggle three or four separate suppliers: a fire risk assessor, an extinguisher company, an alarm engineer and sometimes an electrician. They pay each separately and stitch together their certificates.

A small operator can train two or three fire safety technicians — BM TRADA or equivalent certified, with extinguisher and alarm competency — to do a single quarterly visit per premises that covers all of these, photograph each item with a phone or tablet, and produce one time-stamped report. The customer pays a monthly subscription of £60 to £120 depending on premises size. Per visit, a technician can complete six to ten premises in a region, so a team of three can cover two hundred to three hundred premises, with modest seed capital for a van, ladders, a tablet and basic test gear.

The wedge is the photo-tagged evidence chain. Every visit produces a per-premises record — door photos with intumescent strip checks, alarm test results, extinguisher service dates, emergency lighting duration tests, escape route photos — that lives in a customer portal the fire officer, EHO or insurance assessor can be invited into. Once embedded, the technician knows the premises layout, the inspector's hot-buttons and the history of defects and fixes. Switching cost is real because leaving means losing that accumulated record and starting the audit trail from scratch.
