SecondChair — the completion-guarantee agency that lets a solo creative sign the contract only an agency used to be trusted with.

A small agency vets a freelance designer, writer or developer's project files and briefs, then guarantees a trained stand-in will finish the job on time if they can't, so corporate clients will hire them directly instead of insisting on a full agency.

This borrows the way film completion-bond firms work. Companies like Film Finances Inc. promise a studio's financiers that a movie will get finished even if the director quits or the money runs out, and they keep a bench of people who can step in. SecondChair applies the same promise to freelance creative work.

Here is the everyday friction. A freelance brand designer, copywriter or web developer wins a six-figure engagement from a mid-size company, but the client's legal or procurement team blocks it at the last step, because there's no backup if the freelancer gets ill, disappears, or simply can't finish. So the work goes to a full agency instead, at a much higher day rate, even though the freelancer was the better fit.

SecondChair fixes this without becoming an agency itself. For an annual membership fee, a small in-house team reviews how the freelancer actually works: their brief templates, file structure, asset handoffs, style guides, source control habits. For each large contract, the freelancer pays a one-off setup fee, and SecondChair turns that project into a 'continuity dossier' — enough documentation that one of the agency's vetted, cross-trained substitute freelancers could pick it up mid-project and finish it to spec. SecondChair also arranges (as a broker, not as an insurer) a modest commercial cover policy that pays the client's completion costs if a handover is triggered, so the guarantee is backed by real money as well as a real person.

The freelancer gets a signed letter and a short clause they can hand to the client's procurement or legal team: named backup, documented handover process, financial guarantee attached. That one clause is often what turns a stalled sign-off into a signature.

The wedge is the bench itself. A single vetted illustrator or React developer isn't a guarantee — it's a coin flip on availability and fit. The agency only becomes credible once it holds a wide enough panel that almost any skillset and software combination has two or three ready covers. Building that breadth first is what a thin, fast-following competitor can't match; a client won't trust a guarantee backed by three people when SecondChair can point to eighty.

The freelance creative pays: roughly £30–£60 a month for membership and access to the bench, plus a one-off dossier-setup fee (a few hundred pounds, scaled to contract size) each time they use the guarantee to win a project over about £8,000. The corporate client pays nothing directly — the guarantee is the freelancer's own sales tool.

Marketing and comms teams inside mid-size UK companies have been cutting headcount and moving spend to freelance and fractional creatives to save money, but their own procurement and legal teams still run supplier risk checks built for agencies, not sole traders — checks that increasingly ask about business continuity and key-person risk since public-sector procurement tightened supplier assessment under the Procurement Act 2023. A freelancer who can answer that question with a signed guarantee, rather than a shrug, is the one who gets past the sign-off stage.
