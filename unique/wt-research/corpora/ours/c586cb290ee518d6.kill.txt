NightStep — the platform that books a vetted, insured sleepover carer into your home, paid through your Direct Payment with the receipt your council needs.

A booking platform that matches primary carers with vetted, insured sleepover carers for an overnight shift in the carer's own home, taking a commission on every completed night.

Primary carers are chronically sleep-deprived. Caring for a parent with dementia or a child with complex needs means being on alert every night, and the impact on the carer's own health is well documented by Carers UK.

Councils increasingly recognise sleep deprivation as a carer crisis and will fund overnight respite through Direct Payments under Section 28 of the Care Act 2014, but the operational friction stops most families using the funding. The family has to find someone themselves, verify an enhanced DBS check, confirm public liability insurance, agree a price, produce an invoice, and file a monthly Direct Payment claim pack in the format the council will accept. Most give up. CQC-registered home care agencies charge £200+ per sleepover night, do not issue council-compliant invoices, and require minimum weekly bookings.

NightStep curates a small pool of vetted self-employed micro-providers — each DBS-checked at enhanced level, reference-checked, public liability insured, first-aid trained, and trained in moving and handling. The primary carer books through the platform, the sleepover carer arrives for the 10pm-7am shift, and on completion the platform auto-generates an invoice plus a DBS and insurance snapshot that fills the carer's Direct Payment claim pack in the format the council requires. Commission is £18 per completed night, taken from the carer's Direct Payment.

As CQC's new single assessment framework tightens expectations on micro-providers, and as ICBs commission more preventive respite to keep carers in their role, the platform's vetted pool becomes the only safe route for a primary carer to book privately. The compound data — repeat-booking rates, price sensitivity by region, where the gaps are — becomes a forecasting edge that ICBs will eventually pay for. The shape works the same way regulated marketplaces thrive when the regulator gets tougher: the harder CQC squeezes unregistered provision, the more families have to book through a vetted pool, and the platform's commission grows with enforcement intensity.
