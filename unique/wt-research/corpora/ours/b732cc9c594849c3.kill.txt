Marking Season Kit — the graded exercise book set that arrives at your school each term, so marking takes half the time.

A termly delivery of pre-printed, curriculum-matched exercise books and stampers that let UK teachers mark a full class set in about half the usual time.

This is a physical product brand, not software. Every term, a box arrives at the teacher's school or home with class sets of exercise books whose pages are already laid out for the marking they actually have to do.

The plain version: most exercise books sold to UK schools are blank commodity stationery, bought in bulk by a business manager from Rymans, YPO or Amazon. The teacher then spends hours writing the same feedback comments by hand, sticking in learning-objective labels, and evidencing progress for book scrutiny. The books do none of the work.

Our books do. Each right-hand page carries a pre-printed feedback strip matched to that subject's assessment focuses, a self-assessment box for the pupil, and a dated response space that satisfies the 'pupil acted on feedback' evidence a senior leader looks for in a book scrutiny. The teacher circles and initials instead of writing sentences. We ship it with a set of subject-specific stampers and a roll of learning-objective labels sized to the page.

This fuses two separate changes into one product. First, the Department for Education's own workload reduction toolkit (published on gov.uk, updated 2024) explicitly tells schools to cut written marking and use codes, stampers and whole-class feedback instead. Second, the Education Endowment Foundation's guidance on marking and feedback says the same thing on evidence grounds. Schools have the permission to change, but almost nobody has changed the paper. The advice exists; the stationery has not caught up.

Who we sell to is the teacher, not the school. A primary teacher or a secondary head of department buys one class set for around £95 a term on a card, or expenses it against a departmental capitation budget they already control. That is a price a teacher on the upper pay range will pay out of pocket to get two evenings back a week, and many do already for laminating, stickers and resources.

The hard core is the page design itself, and it compounds. Each term we ask buying teachers which feedback strips they actually used and which their school's marking policy rejected. That gives us a growing library of what real English marking policies demand, school type by school type, subject by subject. Within two years we can print a book that matches a named academy trust's own written policy, and we know that because their teachers told us. No stationery wholesaler collects that; they sell paper by weight.

Runs are small and outsourced. A UK litho printer will run 2,000 exercise books on a short run; we hold no press, no warehouse beyond a unit, and pick-and-pack goes to a third-party fulfilment house. Two people can run the brand: one on design and buyer research, one on operations and customer service.

Distribution is the part teachers make easy for us. Teacher recommendation runs on Facebook groups, X, and TES forums, and a class set is visible on thirty desks every day. Every book carries a small line on the back cover with the reorder link, so pupils' parents and the teacher next door both see it.

UK primary class teachers and secondary heads of department, aged 30 to 60, in state schools and academies. They pay £90 to £110 per class set per term, either on their own card or against a small departmental budget they already sign off. Secondary heads of department buying for a faculty of five teachers are the highest-value repeat buyers.

So the policy permission to change arrived, but school stationery is still blank paper bought on price.
