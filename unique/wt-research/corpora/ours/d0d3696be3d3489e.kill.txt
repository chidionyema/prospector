KeySafe Rounds — The Community Nurse & Home-Carer's Front-Door Key-Safe Forensic Re-Siting and Insurance-Voidance Round.

A mobile bench round that photographs, force-tests, re-sites and re-codes the police-rated key safes on the front walls of housebound patients' homes — turning a device the carer legally depends on but insurers structurally distrust into a dated, load-tested, insurer-quotable evidence record.

Lens: combine_signals — fuse the domiciliary-care access signal (key safes are now the default entry method for council-commissioned home care and NHS community nursing rounds) with the unrelated home-insurance signal (many insurers treat an unrated or LPS 1175-uncertified key safe as an unforced-entry exclusion, and locksmiths on YouTube routinely open the £20 supermarket units in under 30 seconds). The person who feels this daily is a public-sector community nurse or council home-carer aged 30-60 doing 12-18 calls a shift: the safe jams in the cold, the code has drifted after 400 openings, the unit is mounted at handle height in plain view, and if a theft occurs the carer's own name is on the last-access log. Neither the employer nor the insurer will fix it. The physical_ops form: one AI-leveraged operator runs a geographic round in a van with a torch, a cordless SDS, a torque/pull-test rig, a batch of LPS 1175 SR1/SR2-rated safes and a resin anchor kit. Per property: force-test the incumbent unit to a recorded load, photograph the failure or pass, relocate the replacement out of sightline (side return, porch reveal, below the DPC line), re-key to a rotating code, and issue a two-page dated Access Integrity Record naming the standard met, the substrate, the anchor torque and the sightline geometry. The compounding asset is not the safes — it is the per-postcode, per-substrate, per-model failure dataset: which brand of safe shears out of 1930s soft red brick at what load, which council care-agency codes are being reused across whole streets, and which local insurers' loss adjusters accepted or rejected which evidence pack. That dataset lets the operator quote fitting time and failure probability before arriving and lets the record be written in the exact language a specific adjuster has previously accepted. Sold to the carer or to the patient's adult child, not to any institution.

Two nameable payers, both individuals with household budget authority: (1) NHS community nurses, council home-carers and their team leads aged 30-60 buying a £45 'my rounds' subscription that covers force-testing and re-siting the safes on their own named caseload — bought out of pocket because the employer's risk register does not cover the carer's personal exposure on the access log; (2) the adult child of a housebound parent, £180-£240 one-off per property for the rated unit, resin-anchored fit, sightline re-siting and the dated Access Integrity Record. Distribution is owned and free: the operator works one town's district-nursing patch at a time, seeding through the carers themselves (a nurse who has had her round done tells the other eleven on the rota), through parish and street WhatsApp groups, and by publishing the anonymised per-model shear-load results locally.

Domiciliary and community-nursing caseloads have shifted decisively to key-safe entry as the standard access method, so the installed base of cheap unrated safes on residential walls is now enormous and ageing; simultaneously insurers have hardened on unforced-entry and 'reasonable security' exclusions, and locksmith-influencer content has made the sub-standard units publicly known as trivially defeatable. The two facts sit in different worlds and nobody has joined them at the doorstep.
