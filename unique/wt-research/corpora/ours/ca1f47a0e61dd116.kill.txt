The Cold-Store Amnesty Round — The Trust Nutrition Team's Bariatric-Weight Load-Rated Freezer & Fridge Fleet Recertification Round.

A mobile physical round that visits NHS trust ward kitchens, school kitchens and council day-centres to load-test, re-shelve and formally re-rate the shelving inside their existing cold-storage units so heavy pre-plated bariatric meal trays can be legally stacked — turning a fleet the estates team was told to condemn into a certified-capacity asset, priced per cabinet.

Lens: broaden — widen from the single obvious job (fix/replace one broken fridge) to the whole cold-chain value chain the buyer actually owns, and take physical custody of the load-bearing failure nobody has measured. The specific daily friction: NHS ward catering leads, school kitchen managers and council day-centre supervisors are now plating heavier meals (bariatric portions, fortified/high-calorie ONS meals, allergen-segregated duplicate trays) into cold cabinets whose wire shelves and runners were spec'd decades ago for lighter loads. Shelves bow, trays tip, HACCP temperature probes drift because airflow is blocked by over-stacking, and the ward-level answer is always 'stop using that cabinet' or 'raise a capital request for a new one' — a 9-12 month estates queue. Nobody physically measures what the cabinet can actually hold; they either condemn it or overload it. The physical ops business: one operator, a van, calibrated load cells, a set of dial gauges, replacement stainless runners/shelf inserts in standard cabinet widths, an airflow anemometer and a datalogger. The round is: deflect-test every shelf and runner to a rated load, replace the failed runners on the spot from van stock, re-map the cabinet's true safe stacking pattern with the anemometer so temperature uniformity is preserved, and leave a signed per-cabinet load-and-airflow rating card with a photo set and a datalogger trace. Solo runs it as a geographic circuit: one trust or one council's whole site list in a week, then move on. The compounding asset is the cabinet-level dataset: after a few hundred cabinets the operator knows, per make/model/age of cabinet, exactly which runner fails first, at what load, and which stacking pattern preserves 3°C uniformity — meaning quotes get faster, van stock gets exactly right, and the round becomes a re-visit cycle (annual re-rate) rather than one-off jobs. That is a switching cost: once a site's whole cold fleet carries this operator's rating cards and re-rate dates, the EHO-facing paper trail and the estates asset register are built around them, and a rival starts with zero history on those specific cabinets. Not a middleman: the operator physically performs the load test and the metal replacement — there is no market being brokered.

NHS acute-trust catering/nutrition leads and estates facilities managers with delegated non-capital spend (typically the £500-£5k band they can authorise without a business case), plus local-authority day-centre and school-kitchen managers holding site budgets — the named persona buys it because a condemned cabinet directly creates her own extra workload and a failed EHO visit lands on her.

Bariatric and fortified-meal provision has risen sharply across UK institutional catering while capital replacement budgets for catering equipment are frozen under NHS and local-authority revenue pressure, so sites are being told to make ageing cold storage last; simultaneously allergen-segregation duties (Natasha's Law aftermath) force duplicate trays into the same cabinets, pushing loads past original spec at exactly the moment nobody can afford new units.
