RateCheck — the small business tool that hunts down business rates overcharges and builds your appeal pack.

Shows any independent shop, café or office if they’re paying too much in business rates, using real Valuation Office data, and creates the challenge paperwork for a fixed fee.

This borrows the way US property-tax appeal tools like Ownwell scan every home for a protest candidate, and repurposes it for the UK’s business rates — a cost most small occupiers suspect is wrong but never challenge because the process feels opaque.  

The tool asks for your property address.  It pulls the rateable value from the Valuation Office Agency’s open data and instantly compares it against similar premises in your postcode that have a lower assessment.  It flags any that share your use-class, floor area band and location, then builds a side‑by‑side evidence pack you can submit through the VOA’s own ‘Check, Challenge, Appeal’ portal.  

The engine gets smarter as it processes more challenges: every outcome feeds a dataset of what arguments worked, so the next person gets an even sharper recommendation.  Rivals would need the same volume of resolved cases to catch up — and because most small firms never appeal, that dataset is not lying around.  A flat fee of £129 per challenge pack keeps the cost far below a surveyor’s fee while returning hundreds of pounds in the first year if the rates come down.  

The VOA’s online register is free, but it is a neutral listing — it won’t go prospecting for unfair valuations, won’t collate the comparative evidence for you, and won’t draft the formal challenge.  That gap is where a product‑led tool can sit, monetised one appeal at a time.

Independent shopkeepers, café and takeaway owners, small office tenants — anyone leasing a single business unit with a rateable value under £15,000, who sees business rates as one of their biggest fixed costs and suspects they’re being overcharged but has no time to fight it.

The 2023 revaluation pushed up many small-business bills just as energy, wages and input costs spiked.  The VOA’s formal challenge window is open, yet most small occupiers don’t act because the DIY route feels like legal paperwork.  Meanwhile, the VOA has moved its register online and exposed more comparable data — the raw material is finally easy for a software tool to scrape and structure, so the product is buildable today.
