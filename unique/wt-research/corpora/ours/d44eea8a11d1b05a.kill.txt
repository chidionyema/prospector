PlanningUmpire – the resident's per-application objection argument builder.

A web tool that shows you exactly which planning arguments have worked in your council area, so your objection or support letter has maximum impact.

Submitting a planning objection or support letter is a black box: you guess which points might sway the committee, but without real data on past decisions you are often ignored. This tool rebuilds the process from first principles. It scrapes every publicly available planning-committee minutes and decision notice for a given local authority, extracts the arguments raised and the outcome, and then maps which types of argument (overlooking, traffic, character, precedent etc.) correlate with approvals or refusals. When a user enters a new application reference, the tool analyses the proposal against that historical pattern and produces a personalised bullet‑point script – not a legal template – that the resident can submit verbatim in the council's online portal.

Because the data is specific to each council’s voting behaviour, no generic guide can match it. The tool is built and operated by one person: the core technology is web scraping, NLP clustering and a simple report generator. Distribution is entirely organic – via local Facebook groups, Nextdoor, community associations and word of mouth – which costs nothing and reaches the exact audience of squeezed‑middle homeowners facing a neighbour’s extension or a new development next door.

The durable wedge is proprietary data that compounds over time: as more authorities are ingested and more outcomes observed, the predictive precision grows and no late-comer can replicate the historical record. The strongest free alternative is the council’s public‑comment system, but that offers zero intelligence about what actually works. Planning consultants charge hundreds and do not deliver this kind of evidence-based argument selection; they typically rely on generic professional judgement, which this tool empirically outperforms.
