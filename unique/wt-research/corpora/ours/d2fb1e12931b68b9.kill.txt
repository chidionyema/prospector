ThawPoint — The Independent Ice-Cream Van & Mobile Freezer Operator's Per-Route Product-Integrity & Insurance-Defence Bench.

A vertical tool that turns a cheap logger in the freezer into a per-load, per-stop soft-serve mix and hard-scoop integrity record — telling the owner-operator which stops are quietly cooking their stock, and producing the temperature-chain exhibit that wins a spoilage or food-complaint dispute.

Lens: analogical — transplant the pharmaceutical cold-chain 'excursion audit' model (where every temperature deviation is scored against a product's cumulative mean-kinetic-temperature budget, not a pass/fail line) into mobile catering, a sector that only ever asks 'is it below -18°C right now?'. The specific daily friction: a single-van ice-cream or mobile-frozen-food operator opens the hatch 200+ times a shift, runs the compressor off engine or a leisure battery, and loses product quality invisibly — mix that has partly thawed and refrozen goes gritty, sells badly, gets complained about, and gets binned at the end of a hot week with no idea which route or which generator behaviour caused it. Existing telematics/fridge alarms are threshold alarms: they fire at -15°C and stay silent through the exact repeated shallow warm-cold cycling that destroys texture and creates listeria/complaint exposure. The tool logs door-open events, ambient, coil and product-probe temperature per stop, and models cumulative thermal damage per batch, then does two things a threshold alarm structurally cannot: (a) tells the operator 'stop 14 on the Thursday pitch costs you 9 minutes of hatch-open in 28°C ambient — reorder the round or pre-chill' with a per-round pound figure of stock saved, and (b) on a customer complaint or an EHO visit, generates a signed, tamper-evident chain-of-custody exhibit for that exact tub's history from depot to sale. Value capture is a compounding proprietary dataset: after a season the tool holds thousands of van-specific thermal signatures mapped to pitch, ambient, van make, compressor type and product SKU, which lets it predict — for a new subscriber's specific van — which of their pitches will cause loss before they run them. That dataset cannot be assembled by anyone who is not inside hundreds of small operators' freezers.

UK single-van and 2-4 van mobile frozen-food operators — ice-cream van owners on council-licensed pitches, mobile gelato and dessert trailers on event circuits, and small frozen-doorstep-delivery rounds (the ~£12-18k/yr-stock owner-operator who is also the driver). £29/month per van plus £180 for the hardware kit; £250 flat for a complaint/EHO exhibit pack.

Retained EU food law still requires documented temperature control under Regulation (EC) 852/2004 Annex II and the FSA's Safer Food Better Business mobile pack, but enforcement moved to documentary evidence at inspection rather than a spot probe reading; simultaneously sub-£40 BLE dataloggers with 2-year battery life and phone-gateway sync have collapsed the hardware cost of continuous logging, and UK summer ambient extremes plus event-circuit consolidation have pushed spoilage from a nuisance to a margin-defining line item for single-van operators.
