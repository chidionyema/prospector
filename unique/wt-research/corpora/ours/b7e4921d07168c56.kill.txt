CareCancel Desk — The Primary Carer's Travel-Crisis Refund Broker.

A no-win-no-fee per-claim transaction broker that recovers holiday booking costs and travel insurance payouts for primary carers whose trips are cancelled by a care crisis (cared-for hospitalisation, carer incapacitation, emergency respite collapse), built on a proprietary dataset of per-insurer acceptance rates and FOS decision patterns for care-context claims.

UK primary carers (~1.4M providing 20+ hrs/week) experience 'care crises' — cared-for hospitalisation, sudden care-home emergency admission, carer themselves incapacitated — that force last-minute holiday cancellation. Travel insurers routinely reject these claims because the medical event fails their internal acute-emergency threshold, or the care situation is deemed 'foreseeable.' Citizens Advice and Which? hand out template complaint letters, but the consumer still has to (a) diagnose which evidence the SPECIFIC insurer needs, (b) hit the FOS 6-month final-response deadline, and (c) frame a care crisis as a covered medical event. Most abandon at first rejection. A solo broker with a proprietary, compounding database of per-insurer acceptance rates, panel-physician evidence requirements, and FOS decision patterns for care-context claims can take 20–25% of recovered funds. Lens: BROADEN — widen the value chain from one-off claim to a systematic insurer-behaviour data layer that turns the broker into the only UK desk that knows which insurer to push, with what packet, and at what escalation stage.

UK primary carers — verified via Carer's Allowance award letter, Attendance Allowance award, or local-authority carer register — with a cancelled or curtailed holiday in the last 12 months triggered by a documented care crisis. Typical recoverable claim £1,200–£6,500 (booking cost + insurance payout).

2024–25 post-pandemic travel surge collided with NHS waiting list growth (+200k+ since 2023) and a care-sector staffing crisis, materially increasing care-crisis frequency. The 2024 Aviva Supreme Court ruling widened the 'medical emergency' definition in travel insurance, and the FCA's 2024 consultation CP24/6 on fair claims handling is reshaping insurer behaviour. The FOS is now materially more receptive to care-context claims, but consumers don't know how to use the route effectively — and most never get past the first insurer rejection.
