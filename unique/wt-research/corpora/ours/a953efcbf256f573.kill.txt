GA campaign tech compliance binder for AI vendors.

A per-deployment compliance binder for AI vendors selling into Georgia political campaigns, covering SB 361 AI election restrictions, campaign finance reporting, and lobbyist disclosure.

Shape: Sells-to-Tech (C). Georgia passed SB 361 in 2024, which restricts AI-generated and AI-manipulated content in election communications under O.C.G.A. § 21-2-417 and gives the Secretary of State enforcement power over vendors, not just candidates. National campaign AI vendors — synthetic voice platforms, robocall tools, deepfake detectors, automated outreach — face a state-specific enforcement exposure in any Georgia deployment, and Georgia is a perennial battleground with a 2026 governor's race already shaping up.

Today, vendors handle this with a generic AI policy page and a lawyer on retainer; no one publishes a Georgia-specific binder. This tool maps each vendor product feature to the relevant SB 361 subsection, the Secretary of State enforcement bulletin history, the Georgia Government Transparency and Campaign Finance Commission disclosure rules, and the Georgia lobbying disclosure statute, and produces a per-deployment compliance pack the vendor attaches to a Georgia customer contract. The buyer is the Compliance Lead or General Counsel at a campaign AI vendor with active or prospective Georgia customers; the deliverable is a per-deployment PDF plus a quarterly update when the Secretary of State issues new guidance. The wedge is the proprietary catalog of SOS enforcement letters, AG opinions, and candidate complaints that the state publishes but never indexes — a body of work that grows every cycle. Public source: O.C.G.A. § 21-2-417 as amended by SB 361 (2024), Georgia Secretary of State election bulletins, Georgia Government Transparency and Campaign Finance Commission.
