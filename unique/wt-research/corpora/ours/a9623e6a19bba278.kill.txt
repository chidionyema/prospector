Probate Property Buyout Broker.

A solo operator brokers a direct cash sale of a deceased person's property to a pre-vetted 'probate buyer' (a cash-rich investor or developer) at a discount to market value, taking a fixed fee from the buyer, solving the executor's urgent need for liquidity without a protracted estate sale.

Invert: When a person dies, their executor (often a grieving family member, not a professional) inherits a property that is illiquid and costly to maintain (council tax, insurance, upkeep). The executor's pain is acute: they want cash quickly to distribute to beneficiaries or pay inheritance tax, but a full estate agent listing takes 6-12 months, costs 1-3% in fees, and requires them to manage viewings and negotiations. A solo operator can act as a transaction broker connecting the executor to a network of cash buyers who will pay 80-85% of market value in 2-4 weeks, taking a 2-3% fee from the buyer (motivated by the discount). The solo operator's moat is a proprietary list of vetted, cash-ready 'probate buyers' and a repeatable process to identify probate registrations (publicly listed weekly) and reach executors before estate agents. Why Now: UK probate registrations exceeded 300k in 2023, with average property value of £280k; many executors are overwhelmed and time-poor. The solo operator can operate entirely by phone and email, with automated probate registration scraping and email outreach, and a simple CRM to match properties to buyers.

The cash buyer (investor or developer) pays a 2-3% finder's fee on completion, covering the solo operator's margin for brokering the off-market deal.

High UK inheritance tax thresholds (£325k nil-rate band) mean many estates face tax bills within 6 months of death, forcing rapid sale. Rising interest rates have slowed the mainstream market, making cash buyers more dominant and desperate for off-market deals. Probate registrations are publicly searchable and update weekly on gov.uk, providing a free, unscreened lead source.
