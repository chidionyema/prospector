CoMove, lands your partner a job before your Whitehall move.

A fixed-fee coaching sprint that gets the partner of a relocating civil servant a job in their new hub city before the move happens.

CoMove works with civil servants who've been told their role is moving from London to a regional hub — Leeds, Darlington, Cardiff, Glasgow, Salford — under the government's Places for Growth programme, which has already shifted more than 24,000 civil service roles out of London and is still running new waves through 2030. The person being relocated usually has a job waiting. The partner they live with usually doesn't. CoMove is a two-to-six week sprint — CV rework, targeted outreach to named employers in the destination city, mock interviews pitched at that local market — aimed at getting that partner interviews and, ideally, an offer before the removal van is booked.

This works because of a real, specific gap. The Civil Service relocation policy pays for the move itself — legal fees, removals, a few nights in a hotel — but it is explicit that it reimburses the employee's own costs, not employment support for someone else in the household. That gap is often the actual reason someone turns down the move, or takes redundancy instead of relocating. CoMove is bought directly by the relocating civil servant out of household budget, priced as a fixed fee of roughly £450-£900 depending on how far the search has to run, because it is solving their household's affordability problem, not the department's HR problem.

What compounds is the map of who is actually hiring in each hub city as it fills up with newly arrived civil service households: Darlington's growing DWP and HMRC campus, Leeds' UK Infrastructure Bank and financial-services cluster, Glasgow's public-body concentration. A small team builds direct relationships with local recruiters, chambers of commerce and mid-sized employers in each hub who want a steady, low-effort feed of skilled candidates who are moving to their city for reasons that have nothing to do with the local job market — reasons that make them likely to stay for years. That referral relationship, and the growing record of which local employers actually convert an accompanying partner into a hire, is not something a national relocation contractor bothers to build, because a single household is too small a deal for their account model.

The civil servant whose post is being relocated under Places for Growth, paying out of household budget — sometimes topped up from their relocation lump sum — because their partner's income is what makes the move actually affordable.

Places for Growth has already moved more than 24,000 civil service roles out of London, and the Cabinet Office is still running new waves through 2030 as hubs like Darlington Economic Campus keep growing. Every new wave creates another batch of households facing exactly this choice this year, not as a one-off event.
