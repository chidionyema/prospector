PitStop Ledger — The Gig Driver's Per-Vehicle Warranty & Recall Claim Reconstruction Service.

A fixed-fee productized service that reconstructs a high-mileage gig driver's fragmented service history into a manufacturer-grade warranty/goodwill claim pack, recovering repair costs the dealer has already refused.

Lens: broaden — instead of drilling into one repair dispute, widen to the whole ownership lifecycle of the one asset a gig driver cannot lose. A 22-27 year old rideshare/delivery driver puts 40-60k miles/year on a used car bought at 3-5 years old, often with a used-approved or aftermarket warranty. When a DPF, turbo, DSG mechatronic unit or hybrid battery module fails at £1,800-£4,500, the dealer refuses on 'lack of service history' or 'commercial use' — and the driver, who has serviced the car at four different independent garages paying cash, has no coherent evidence and no idea that (a) manufacturer goodwill schemes run well outside warranty, (b) EU/UK type-approval emissions durability obligations and open technical service bulletins/recall campaigns often cover the exact failure, and (c) 'commercial use' exclusions are frequently unenforceable against a private-plate owner under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 s.9-s.19. The productized service is a fixed £180-£340 per-claim reconstruction: pull the DVLA MOT history API (free, complete, mileage-verified), sequence it against garage invoices, VIN-decode the build spec, cross-reference the failure against a compounding internal library of manufacturer TSBs, goodwill precedents and DVSA recall campaigns, then output a signed evidence dossier plus a sequenced escalation letter chain (dealer principal → manufacturer customer care → Motor Ombudsman → s.75/chargeback). Delivered as a service, not software, because the value is the operator's judgment about WHICH argument wins on THIS engine family. Every claim outcome feeds a proprietary win/loss table by manufacturer × component × mileage band × argument-used, which no incumbent can accumulate because dealers see only their own franchise and ombudsmen publish nothing granular.

UK-based full-time rideshare and multi-app delivery drivers aged 18-27 running a private-plate used car or PHEV at 40k+ miles/year, who have just been handed a refused warranty claim or a £2k+ repair quote — sourced free via driver WhatsApp/Telegram groups, r/UberDriversUK, and the private-hire licensing office queues where they already congregate.

Three changes converge: the 2022-2025 used-car price spike pushed gig drivers into older, more complex hybrid/DPF-equipped vehicles just as their emissions-critical components hit the 80-120k mile failure cliff; DVLA's MOT history API went fully open and now carries verified mileage readings that make historic-usage reconstruction possible for free for the first time; and the Motor Ombudsman's caseload has risen sharply while manufacturers quietly widened out-of-warranty goodwill budgets to protect ULEZ-era emissions-component reputations — creating a discretionary pot that is only released to a well-argued file.
