CarerCredit Catch — the carer's tool that finds the State Pension years you should have claimed for caring, and tells you which to top up for the biggest boost.

Pull your National Insurance record, find every year you should have claimed Carer's Credit for caring, and top up only the years that give the biggest pension boost.

CarerCredit Catch is a small team tool that helps a primary carer find every year of National Insurance credits they should have claimed for caring, and tells them which years to top up with Class 3 voluntary NICs for the biggest boost to their State Pension.

It works because Carer's Credit, created under the Carer's Credit Regulations 2010 (SI 2010/2530), gives a carer up to 35 years of NI credits for caring 20+ hours a week for someone on Attendance Allowance, PIP (daily living), DLA (middle or higher care) or Constant Attendance Allowance. The gov.uk NI forecast page does not flag this entitlement, the DWP does not warn you when you stop claiming Carer's Allowance, and Carers Trust guidance is generic. Filling those gaps often unlocks £2,000 to £12,000 of extra State Pension over a carer's retirement, for a top-up cost of about £880 per year, and the maths is per-person, depending on how many more years they keep caring, how long they live, and any State Pension uprating under the Pensions Act 2011 triple lock.

The thing that holds this together is the algorithm that combines four pieces of data: the carer's actual NI record (pulled from the HMRC portal), the qualifying benefits of the person they cared for, the eligibility rules for Carer's Credit, and the buy-back maths. None of the first-party tools put these together. The gov.uk NI checker shows you the record but not the credit. The DWP can tell you about Carer's Credit but not how to apply it to your specific years. Citizens Advice and the Carers Trust signpost you but will not build a personalised pack. A two-person team can hold this because the rules are stable and the personalised data is collected once per user.

We rebuild the carer's situation from the raw thing they lose — years of pension built up — instead of starting from the benefits they can claim today. That is how a small team can build a real product here: nobody else has stitched the rules together. The price is £79 for the gap report, with an optional £149 that includes the backdated Carer's Credit claim forms and the Class 3 top-up application. Roughly 1.5 million UK carers aged 50-67 gave up work to care full-time, and most have no idea what they have lost.

Why now: the State Pension rose to £221.20 a week in April 2024 and the Pension Service has been writing to over-50s about their forecast. Many of them are seeing 5 to 10 year gaps they cannot explain, and the rules about filling those gaps are not well publicised. The DWP has not run a coordinated Carer's Credit awareness campaign, and the devolved governments are not pushing it either.
