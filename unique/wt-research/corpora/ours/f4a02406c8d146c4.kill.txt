PreCert, FDA Pre-Sub kit for AI medical-device startups.

A tool for AI health startup founders that drafts the FDA Pre-Submission package for their software medical device, so the FDA meeting ends with a clear pathway.

PreCert is a vertical tool for early-stage AI health startup founders in California who have built a Software-as-a-Medical-Device and need to engage FDA before they file a 510(k). Today, these founders either pay a regulatory affairs consultant $300 to $500 an hour to draft the Pre-Submission, or they try to write it themselves and get bounced back by FDA reviewers asking for clearer framing. The tool drafts the Statement of Indications for Use, the Q-Sub questions, and the device description in the framing that FDA's Digital Health Center of Excellence reviewers actually expect for AI/ML devices.

The buyer is the founder-operator wearing the regulatory hat because they cannot yet afford a full-time RA hire. The trigger is the moment the founder commits to a Pre-Submission, typically six to nine months before a planned 510(k) or De Novo filing. The deliverable is a Q-Sub package formatted per FDA's Pre-Sub Program guidance (FDA-2014-N-0738) and ready to upload to the CDRH Portal.

The wedge is proprietary data. The tool compounds a structured database of FDA Q-Sub response letters, which are public through FOIA but arrive as scanned PDFs across thousands of pages, mapped to AI/ML device categories and to the specific questions FDA tends to push back on. Every new FDA AI/ML clearance, and every new Q-Sub run through the tool, makes the mapping sharper.

The analogical lens borrows from how pharmaceutical contract research organizations built structured Pre-IND question banks for biotechs entering the FDA process, and applies that same prep discipline to AI/ML SaMD Pre-Subs. Incumbents like Greenlight Guru and Qualio fill QMS records, not FDA Q-Sub packages, and lack AI/ML-specific framing. FDA's free Pre-Sub guidance is general, not AI/ML-specific. Regulatory affairs consultants charge $300 to $500 an hour, do not scale across many Pre-Subs, and keep their knowledge in individual heads rather than structured data.
