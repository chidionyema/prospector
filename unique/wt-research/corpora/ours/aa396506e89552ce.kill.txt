PrepRoom Sweep — The County Science Department Chemical Amnesty Round.

A solo-run annual physical service that inventories, photographs and hazard-classifies the decades-old chemical stock in secondary-school science prep rooms, then consolidates 15-20 schools onto a single licensed-contractor disposal day so each department pays a fraction of the haz-waste minimum call-out — sold as a fixed-fee 'amnesty round' to heads of science.

Lens: cross_sector — this transplants the agricultural 'obsolete pesticide amnesty collection' route model (group a county of farms onto one contractor day to defeat minimum call-out economics) into school science departments. The daily friction: senior science technicians and heads of science sit on prep-room shelves of time-expired, sometimes shock-sensitive chemicals (dried picric acid, old ethers, unlabeled Winchester bottles) that they know are a COSHH and inspection liability, but a single-school hazardous-waste quote carries a £400-800 minimum call-out plus per-item identification charges their £500-2,000 capitation budget cannot absorb, so the problem is deferred year after year. The solo operator does the physical work incumbents refuse: walks the prep room, photographs and identifies every container (AI-assisted classification against CLEAPSS hazard data), produces the consignment-ready inventory, books one licensed ADR contractor for a whole-county route day, and splits that fixed cost across all participating schools. Each year's round regenerates the school's chemical inventory ledger, which becomes the department's living COSHH record — the switching cost that makes the renewal automatic. Physical_ops native: revenue is per-school fixed fee for the on-site round plus a per-kilo pass-through margin on the consolidated disposal day; the operator never carries hazardous waste personally, so no licence gates the solo.

Heads of science and senior science technicians at the ~60-90 state secondary schools in one named county (e.g. Kent or Essex), spending from departmental capitation budgets of £500-2,000/year, typically £250-450 per school per round versus a £600+ solo disposal quote.
