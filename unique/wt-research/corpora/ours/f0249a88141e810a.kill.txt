The Dead Man's Lease Extractor.

A solo operator buys out the remaining months of a deceased tenant's lease from their estate, then sublets the property at a profit to a pre-vetted replacement tenant, solving the problem of lease liability for grieving families and empty property costs for landlords.

When a tenant dies, their estate is legally liable for the remaining lease term, but grieving families are financially and emotionally unprepared to pay months of rent for an empty property. Landlords face lost income and re-letting costs. A solo broker can act as the 'middleman'—taking over the lease from the estate for a lump sum (e.g. 50% of remaining rent), then subletting at market rate to a new tenant sourced from a pre-built waiting list, pocketing the delta. This is a transaction brokerage between two motivated parties: the estate wants to exit cheaply, the landlord wants a paying tenant fast, and the solo operator captures the spread. The durable wedge is proprietary data: a curated list of pre-qualified tenants seeking short-term leases in high-turnover areas (near universities, hospitals, or corporate relocation hubs).

The primary payer is the deceased tenant's estate (executor or next of kin) for the lease buyout fee. The secondary payer is the new tenant for the full market rent. Both transactions are facilitated by the solo operator.

Rising housing costs and rental demand in UK cities (e.g. Manchester, Birmingham) mean lease liabilities are larger and harder to offload. Simultaneously, the population is aging, and the number of single-person households is increasing, making tenant death more common. Landlords are more motivated than ever to avoid void periods. No existing service specializes in this exact gap—estate lawyers handle probate, not property subletting, and letting agents charge full fees without addressing the unique legal and emotional burden of a deceased tenant.
