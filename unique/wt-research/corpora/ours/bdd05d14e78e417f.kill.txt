ChimneyHold Bond — The Sole-Trader Chimney Sweep's Stove-Fire Compensation-Claim Income Micro-Bond.

A per-sweep-visit parametric income bond (£7 per sweep lodged into a segregated ledger) that pays the sole-trader chimney sweep £500/week for up to 16 weeks when a customer's chimney fire within 120 days of sweeping opens a Professional Indemnity claim against the sweep and freezes their booking pipeline.

UK chimney sweeps face a specific and mostly invisible income crisis (narrow lens, drilled to one micro-niche): when a customer's chimney catches fire within weeks or months of a sweep, the customer's insurer opens a recovery claim against the sweep's PI policy on the working theory that the sweep certified a defective flue. The sweep's PI insurer then either non-renews cover, hikes the premium beyond reach, or imposes a 'claims-cooperation' clause that effectively prevents taking new bookings while the file is open — typically 4-9 months. Mainstream income-protection insurance excludes this trigger (it requires the underlying claim to settle first, sometimes years later), and no specialist parametric product exists because the trigger is too narrow for generalist insurers to model. By aggregating per-sweep contributions into a per-sweep segregated client ledger, and pricing each sweep against a proprietary fire-attribution timeline dataset the operator accumulates from sweep self-reports, FOI'd Fire & Rescue Service incident logs and per-appliance class outcome data, a one-person operator can underwrite a parametric bond that pays out automatically on a single verifiable event (the PI insurer's claim-opening letter naming a chimney-fire incident) — exactly matching the cash-flow gap mainstream products do not.

Self-employed chimney sweeps registered with NACS (National Association of Chimney Sweeps) or the Guild of Master Chimney Sweeps — roughly 5,000-8,000 UK sole-trader sweeps earning £60-150 per sweep at 8-12 sweeps per week, most operating without employer cover.

Three converging shifts since 2022: (1) UK log-burner installations up ~40% (HETAS / Stove Industry Alliance data) materially increase per-sweep fire exposure; (2) post-Grenfell insurer pressure on trades' Professional Indemnity cover has lengthened claims investigations — sweeps report 6-9 month windows where renewal cover is declined and they cannot legally undertake regulated work; (3) recent well-publicised chimney fires (Whitby 2024, Bristol 2023) have emboldened insurer recovery teams to chase sweeps even where attribution is weak, making the cash-bridge gap more frequent and more acute.
