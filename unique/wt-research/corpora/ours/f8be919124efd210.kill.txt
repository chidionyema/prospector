The Divorce Financial Forensics Engine.

A web app that flags hidden assets and drafting errors in Form E financial disclosures by cross-referencing the user's submission against a proprietary database of judicial findings from published divorce cases.

first_principles: Form E is a dense, high-stakes document; self-reppers and even some lawyers miss subtle misrepresentations. By mining the public record of financial remedy judgments for common concealment patterns and valuation disputes, the engine provides a specific, fact-based 'red flag' report and tailored amendment language that generic guides cannot.

Squeezed middle individuals going through a divorce (typically age 40–55, mortgages, children) who cannot afford a full forensic accountant but fear the other party is hiding assets or income.

Rising cost of legal services is pushing more couples toward self-representation or unbundled advice; recent Family Court transparency pilots are releasing richer judgment data, making pattern extraction more viable.
