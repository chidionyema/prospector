LeakGuard, finds the revenue your Stripe billing quietly drops.

A self-serve tool that plugs into a startup's Stripe account and finds revenue lost to billing bugs, before investors find it first.

LeakGuard is a small tool a startup founder plugs into their Stripe account. It compares what Stripe actually charged against what the product's own logs say customers used or were entitled to, and flags every gap — a webhook that failed silently, a discount code that never expired, a usage tier that never triggered a price bump.

This borrows the way phone companies run a 'revenue assurance' team that reconciles billed minutes against the network's own call logs, catching leakage that never shows up in a billing report because the billing system only knows what it billed, not what it should have billed. UK SaaS founders building on Stripe never get that second check — right up until a Series A term sheet stalls over a data-room question they can't answer.

Diligence teams increasingly pull raw Stripe exports and re-derive MRR themselves rather than trust a founder's dashboard, and Stripe's own billing has grown more complex — usage tiers, proration, dunning retries — which is exactly where leakage hides.

Every gap LeakGuard finds and confirms becomes a labelled leakage pattern — a specific webhook failure signature, a specific proration bug tied to a specific pricing-model shape. That library compounds across every company it runs on, so the tool gets faster at spotting new leaks with every install, in a way a single founder's own reconciliation script never could.
