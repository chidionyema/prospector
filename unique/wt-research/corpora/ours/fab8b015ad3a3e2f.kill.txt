SolderFloor — The Squeezed-Middle Parent's Board-Level Repair Bench for Dead School Chromebooks and Tablets.

A component-level toolkit-and-training operation that sells the exact jig, donor-board map and failure-code playbook that lets a parent or a school IT lead resurrect the specific dead Chromebook/iPad models UK schools issue to pupils — sold as a physical kit plus a per-model bench manual, not a repair service.

Lens: analogical — transplant the automotive aftermarket's 'Haynes manual + model-specific special tool' business into the sealed-consumer-electronics category, where the pain now lands on the household. A 35-55 parent with two secondary-age children on a mortgage is now the de-facto owner of £600-£1200 of school-mandated devices (1:1 device schemes, DfE Get Help With Tech legacy fleets, BYOD sixth-form policies), and when a charge port lifts or a board shorts, the OEM/reseller quote is a flat replacement — a predictable multi-hundred-pound hit at exactly the moment the household is cash-poor. Repair shops won't touch board level on low-value devices because the labour doesn't clear; parents and school IT leads will, because their alternative is buying a new unit. The picks-and-shovels play is to arm them: a per-model bench kit (correct-pitch preheat jig, donor-part harvest map for the two or three boards these fleets share, known-good voltage rail sheets, failure-code-to-component tables built from every board that passes the bench) plus a laminated model manual and a consumable stream of the four connectors that actually fail. The moat is the failure corpus: each kit registration returns a structured board-fault log, so the per-model rail sheets and fault-frequency tables get sharper with every unit anyone in the network fixes — a dataset that no OEM will publish (it undercuts replacement revenue) and no shop will pool (it's their margin). Sold to parents' WhatsApp/Facebook year-group networks and to school IT technician forums — both free, owned distribution. Solo-operable: kits are assembled from off-the-shelf tooling with one custom jig, manuals are generated from the corpus, and the operator never touches a customer's device.

UK parents aged 35-55 with 2+ children on school 1:1 device schemes facing a £250-£600 out-of-warranty replacement invoice; plus single-technician IT leads at multi-academy trust primary schools managing 200-600 pupil devices on a flat annual budget.

The 2020-22 DfE Get Help With Tech and trust-funded 1:1 fleets are now 4-6 years old and simultaneously exiting both warranty and Google's ChromeOS Auto Update Expiration window, so a huge installed base has hit the point where every fault is a full-price replacement decision made by a household, not an institution — while right-to-repair pressure has finally made schematics, donor boards and hot-air tooling cheap and legal to source.
