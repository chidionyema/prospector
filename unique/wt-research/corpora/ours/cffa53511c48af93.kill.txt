AppealEdge – The School Admission Tribunal Intelligence Letter.

A subscription data service that uses FOI requests to predict school admission appeal outcomes, giving parents a strategic edge.

School admission appeals are quasi‑legal battles fought blind. By running a systematic FOI pipeline across all English local authorities—capturing panel‑level win rates, school‑specific patterns, and board‑member tendencies—a solo operator can build a predictive case‑strength model. Paying parents get a monthly intelligence letter and premium access to a searchable database that lets them tailor their appeal to exploit hidden biases. The FOI‑powered dataset becomes a proprietary asset that no free forum or official body can reproduce.

Squeezed‑middle parents in London and the South East who have just been refused a primary or secondary school place and are preparing an appeal.

The 2023 Schools Bill reinforced parental choice rhetoric but delivered no transparency. With 90,000 annual appeals and a 25% national success rate, desperation is high. FOI request platforms now make large‑scale data collection viable, and parental mistrust of admission authorities is at a peak, creating a window for an adversarial intelligence product.

The entire thesis rests on one claim: a FOI-driven, panel-level dataset becomes a self-reinforcing proprietary moat 'no free forum or official body can reproduce.' The gathered evidence affirmatively contradicts that claim on two independent grounds. (1) Non-exclusivity: the ICO guidance confirms FOIA 2000/EIR 2004 require state schools to answer requests from 'anyone... researchers, journalists, commercial organisations, members of the public' and to 'treat them all in the same way' [75219770eec5dd7e]. The collection mechanism is a public statutory right open to every competitor, volunteer, or Mumsnet user at zero cost. There is no exclusive access, so no durable wedge. (2) The specific granular data the moat depends on (panel-member tendencies, school-level win rates) is exactly the data authorities lawfully withhold and that takes years to extract: FOI decision notices show councils refusing raw 11+/admissions data under DPA s.40(2) personal-data and s.43(2) commercial-interest exemptions [1bdb92ce4dbb93c5, 3db1f725c30fe0ba], and the Coombs tribunal shows a single such dataset request lodged Oct 2020 was not resolved until July 2024 [3e7b7e909ac9634e]. A solo operator cannot run a 'systematic FOI pipeline across all English local authorities' for litigated, multi-year-per-dataset, frequently-refused granular records. Meanwhile the official APAD statistics that ARE freely published cover outcomes only as aggregate totals [fb3f0bfae39868ff, 7f6a69ec44dcf6dd] — the coarse, free, non-predictive layer the product claims to transcend. So the product is squeezed between free aggregate official data and granular data that is legally withheld/slow. The proprietary-data hypothesis, which is the only stated source of durability, is dead.
