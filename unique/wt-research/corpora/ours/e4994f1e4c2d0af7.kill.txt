CrewCap — The Under-27 Film & TV Runner's Unpaid-Overtime Reconstruction & Recoupment Pack.

A fixed-fee productized service that rebuilds a young freelance film/TV crew member's actual worked hours from call sheets, unit-base WhatsApp traffic and location GPS, then issues a BECTU/PACT-grade-cited overtime, turnaround-breach and meal-penalty demand pack their production accountant can pay without a lawyer.

Lens: broaden — instead of drilling into one claim type, widen to the WHOLE bottom-of-the-call-sheet value chain: runners, trainees, daily art-department and camera juniors on non-union daily deal memos who work 13–16 hour days but are paid a flat 'day rate' that silently absorbs overtime, broken turnaround and missed meal breaks. The friction is daily: a 22-year-old runner is texted a 05:30 unit call, wrapped at 21:40, driven back to base at 22:30, and re-called at 06:00 the next morning — a turnaround breach worth a full extra day under most PACT/BECTU-referenced deal memos — but they have no record, no confidence, and a rational fear of never being re-booked. The structural form is a productized service, not software: a fixed £180 reconstruction fee (plus £45 per additional production) where the operator ingests the client's phone (call sheets PDF'd to them, production WhatsApp group exports, Google Timeline/location history, petrol receipts, unit-base parking texts) and outputs a single artefact — a day-by-day reconstructed hours ledger, each line cross-cited to the specific deal-memo or agreement clause and the specific evidence exhibit, plus a payable-now schedule addressed to the production accountant with a 14-day pre-action date. Crucially the pack is written to be SETTLED, not litigated: production accountants have an existing 'crew payment adjustment' line and settle quietly rather than let a turnaround-breach precedent circulate. Every pack completed writes into a proprietary asset no incumbent holds: a per-production-company, per-line-producer, per-payroll-bureau ledger of which deal-memo wordings were used, which breaches were conceded without argument, which were fought, what percentage settled and in how many days. By pack 200 the operator can tell a runner joining a specific production, before they sign, exactly what that line producer historically concedes — and price the pack against expected recovery. Distribution is owned and free: the runner/trainee Facebook groups, the r/UKfilmcrew and CrewStore-adjacent Discords, and the trainee cohorts of the ScreenSkills and NFTS short-course pipelines, where a single anonymised 'here is what my £600 week was actually worth' teardown post is native content, not advertising. Not a claims portal, not an HR SaaS, not an employment-law marketplace: it is one operator producing one high-craft document per client, priced fixed, with an AI-assisted evidence-reconstruction pipeline doing the heavy lifting.

Under-27 freelance UK film/TV crew on non-union daily or weekly deal memos — specifically runners, trainees, floor/art-department/camera juniors and production assistants on scripted drama and high-end TV (HETV) productions in the London, Leeds, Cardiff, Belfast and Glasgow hubs — who have just wrapped a block and are owed 3–6 weeks of absorbed overtime. Secondary named payer: junior crew in the same bracket on commercials and branded-content shoots booked through production service companies. They pay £180 for the first production reconstructed and £45 per additional production in the same sweep, out of a recovery they typically value at £600–£2,400.

Three changes stack. First, HETV production volume in the UK rebounded hard after the 2023 strike freeze and the 2024 Independent Film Tax Credit, pulling a very large, very young, largely non-union intake onto sets on daily deal memos — the cohort with the least idea what their memo entitles them to. Second, the Employment Rights Act 2025 package and the associated push on the Fair Work Agency has moved holiday pay, worked-hours records and the employment-status question from background noise to live, quotable pressure, making a production accountant materially more inclined to settle a well-evidenced hours claim than to argue it. Third, and this is what makes reconstruction actually possible now: the evidence a 22-year-old carries is far richer than it was five years ago — call sheets arrive as PDFs, unit moves and wrap times are timestamped in WhatsApp groups, and Google Timeline/iOS location history places them at a location and unit base to the minute — but assembling that into a citable ledger was, until AI-assisted document and chat-log extraction, days of unaffordable human work. The gap between 'the evidence exists on their phone' and 'nobody can afford to assemble it' is the whole business, and it opened recently.
