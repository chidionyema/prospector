Tax deduction status appeals for building subcontractors.

A fixed-fee appeal pack and First-tier Tax Tribunal representation service for UK construction subcontractors hit by an HMRC CIS gross payment refusal, recovering full invoice payment.

Small UK construction subcontractors earn the right to be paid gross by their contractor under the Construction Industry Scheme (CIS). HMRC grants Gross Payment Status (GPS) when it is satisfied the subcontractor meets the tests set out in HMRC's CIS430 guidance: business in the UK, turnover above the CIS minimum, and a clean tax-compliance record.

If HMRC refuses GPS, the subcontractor loses roughly 20% (if registered for VAT) or 30% (if not) of every invoice to the contractor, who must deduct it and pay it to HMRC. For a sole trader earning £200 a day on the tools, that is £40 to £60 a day withheld until the next tax year. Losing GPS is a cash-flow killer for a small subcontractor.

HMRC's stated criteria are public. What the small subcontractor lacks is the specific evidence pack that wins at HMRC internal review, and the experience to take a refusal to the First-tier Tax Tribunal (the court that hears tax disputes) if the review fails.

GrossStick is a fixed-fee service for the small subcontractor who has just lost GPS, or who needs to defend it at a compliance review. The fee (from £950 fixed) covers an evidence pack built around HMRC's published tests, a formal review request, and representation at the First-tier Tax Tribunal if the review fails.

The fixed-fee process is built on a working library of tribunal precedents and HMRC officer guidance that HMRC publishes, but that no small subcontractor has the time to assemble. The technical asset compounds with each appeal: every new case adds to the precedent library and refines the evidence templates.

This works because the small subcontractor is highly motivated (cash flow under immediate threat), the fee is small compared to the cost of losing GPS (£950 fixed fee versus £8,000 to £15,000 a year in withheld tax for a typical sole trader), and the route to the customer is direct via CIS contractor forums, IR35 communities, and accountant referrals.

It holds because HMRC is the body that decides the internal review, so its free guidance tells the subcontractor what HMRC wants them to meet, not how to win against HMRC's refusal. HMRC will not represent the applicant against itself. That structural gap is what GrossStick fills, for a fixed fee.
