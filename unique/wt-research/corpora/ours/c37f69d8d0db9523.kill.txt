OverpaidCare — the fixed-fee check that gets a carer's overpaid Carer's Allowance debt written off.

For family carers hit with a Carer's Allowance overpayment demand, this is a fixed-fee service that builds the official-error case and asks the DWP to write the debt off.

Carer's Allowance in the UK has a hard earnings limit. Earn a pound over it in a week and you lose the whole week's payment. The DWP often does not notice for years. Then a letter arrives demanding thousands of pounds back, sometimes tens of thousands.

This service is one person, working from home, doing one narrow job well. A carer sends their overpayment letter, their bank statements and their payslips. Within five working days they get back a finished pack: a mandatory reconsideration request, a written argument that the DWP's own delay caused the debt to grow, and a formal request to waive recovery.

The argument has a specific shape. The DWP has run automated earnings alerts since 2019 through its link to HMRC's Real Time Information. If the department was told a carer's earnings had risen and sat on that alert for three years, the carer can argue the department caused the loss. The National Audit Office reported in 2019 that DWP was not acting on most of these alerts. A House of Commons Work and Pensions Committee inquiry reported in 2024 on exactly this failure. Those published findings are the backbone of every pack.

Most of the work here is the invert. The usual move is to help someone comply with the rules. This does the opposite: it takes the fact that the DWP is getting more aggressive at recovering debt, and turns that aggression into the reason the service exists. The harder the department chases, the more people need someone to argue back. The DWP cannot build this. It cannot write the argument against itself.

The money is a flat £180 per pack, paid up front. No percentage, no regulated claims-management activity, no debt advice — the pack is a document the carer signs and sends themselves. That keeps it outside the FCA's claims-management permissions, which cover regulated claims only, and outside the debt-adjusting rules, because the operator never negotiates on the carer's behalf.

What compounds is the outcome file. Every pack that comes back with a decision — written off, reduced, refused — is logged against which argument was used, which DWP debt centre replied, and how long it took. After two hundred packs the operator knows which wording works at which office. That is a record nobody else holds, because no charity publishes decision-level outcomes and no carer ever sees more than their own letter.

Distribution is free and already assembled. Carers UK forums, the Benefits and Work community, the r/DWPhelp subreddit and several large Facebook carer groups have the same thread every week: 'DWP says I owe £8,000, what do I do?' Answering those threads honestly, in public, with real detail, is the whole marketing plan.

One person can run this at around thirty packs a month once the argument templates are built and an AI assistant does the first pass on the bank statements.
