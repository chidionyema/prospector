TaxQuarter Planner — the printed diary built around HMRC's new quarterly filing dates for freelancers.

A printed year planner that maps the UK's new quarterly tax filing dates for freelancers, sold through the maker's own online shop.

From 6 April 2026, HM Revenue & Customs began requiring self-employed people earning more than £50,000 a year to keep digital records and send a tax update every quarter, under the Making Tax Digital for Income Tax rules. A second wave, covering anyone earning over £30,000, joins from April 2027. For a freelance designer, writer, developer or consultant used to doing one tax return a year, this is a real shift: four new deadlines a year instead of one, landing on odd dates — 5 July, 5 October, 5 January, 5 April — that don't map onto a normal calendar or diary.

This borrows the idea of a subject-specific planner, the kind gardeners, students or new parents already buy because a general one doesn't fit their year, and applies it to the newly complicated freelance tax year. Each section leading up to a filing date carries pre-printed prompts: which receipts to gather, which income category to check, and a countdown to the deadline. It's a physical, printed product, sold through the maker's own Shopify or Etsy shop, made through print-on-demand or a local UK printer, and posted out with no factory, warehouse or stock risk.

It doesn't compete with accounting software. Xero, QuickBooks and HMRC's own free tools already handle the actual filing. What it sells is the thing none of those give a freelancer: a physical object on the desk that turns four new, oddly-dated deadlines into something they can see and touch, the way a paper diary still outsells a phone reminder for people who plan that way. Reprinted each April with that year's exact dates, it's the kind of thing a freelancer might just re-buy out of habit.
