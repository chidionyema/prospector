Respite care grant applications for Texas family carers.

A Texas flat-fee service that handles paperwork so primary carers can access scattered free or low-cost respite care grants and voucher programs, charged per application.

The raw pain is that millions of family caregivers are burning out, but they lack the time and energy to research and apply for respite funding. In Texas, respite care resources are fragmented: the Texas Health and Human Services Commission runs the 'Texas Respite Support Program' (TRSP) offering vouchers for Alzheimer's-related respite, but each region's Area Agency on Aging (AAA) has different application processes and eligibility criteria. There are also local nonprofits and faith-based grants. A primary carer cannot navigate this alone.

This service rebuilds the process from the carer's perspective, not the funder's. Instead of the carer chasing help, a solo operator with AI-assisted research compiles a proprietary database of every active respite funding opportunity in each Texas AAA region. For a fixed fee of $149 per application set, the operator fills out all required forms, attaches the needed documentation, and submits them on behalf of the carer. The carer provides basic information once, and the service does the rest.

This borrows the model of college application coaches, but applies it to respite care. The operator's database of grant sources, success rates, and application tips becomes a durable wedge — proprietary data that nobody else has because it compounds with each client. Distribution is through targeted outreach in Texas caregiver Facebook groups and partnerships with local aging organizations.

The primary carer (typically an adult child managing an aging parent's care) pays the flat fee out of pocket, because the value of a funded break far exceeds the cost.

Texas has recently expanded the Texas Respite Support Program (TRSP) and other non-Medicaid respite funds, but uptake remains low because each grant requires a separate application and most carers don't have the bandwidth to find and complete them. Federal recovery funds from the American Rescue Plan Act also provided a temporary boost to home- and community-based services, creating a window where more money is available than is being claimed.
