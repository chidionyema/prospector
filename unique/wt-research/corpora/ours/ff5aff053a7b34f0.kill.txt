Bench Boss, court booking software for one-court clubs.

Booking, coaching and pay-as-you-go software built for the volunteer who runs a single-court UK community sports club from their kitchen table.

A teaching assistant or NHS nurse ends up as honorary secretary of the local tennis, bowls or squash club. They run 300 members off a WhatsApp group, a paper key list and a shoebox of £4 subs. Clubmark and Sport England funding forms then ask for membership records, safeguarding records and court usage they cannot produce.

Bench Boss is software sold per club, £39 a month, that runs the whole single-site club: court slots with smart-lock codes, junior coaching registers, DBS expiry dates, and card payments that land in the club account. It borrows the way restaurant table-booking software made its money — not from the booking, but from owning the seating map and the no-show data. Here the equivalent asset is the court occupancy record.

That record compounds. After two seasons a club has proof of usage per hour, per age group, per court. Sport England's Movement Fund and most local authority asset-transfer negotiations both ask for exactly that evidence. A club with three years of Bench Boss data writes a stronger funding bid than one with a paper diary, and it cannot get that history anywhere else after the fact. That is the reason a volunteer secretary never switches away, and the reason the next secretary inherits the software rather than replacing it.

Growth comes through county associations. There are county tennis, bowls and badminton bodies that each hold lists of 80 to 400 affiliated clubs and send them newsletters already. One partnership deal seeds hundreds of clubs, and the volunteer handover each September brings a fresh person into the product without any selling.
