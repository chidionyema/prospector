IncomeTrace, proof packs that raise your child maintenance.

For a separated parent whose self-employed ex under-reports income, a fixed-fee evidence pack that gets the Child Maintenance Service to use the real number.

Every year thousands of separated parents ask the Child Maintenance Service (CMS) to work out what their ex-partner should pay, and CMS bases that figure entirely on whatever income the ex-partner declared to HMRC on their Self Assessment return. If the real number is higher because a business bank account, a rental property, or cash work is kept off the books, CMS has no way to find that out itself — it is built to run a formula on a declared figure, not to go digging into anyone's finances.

This is a fixed-fee investigation and report service for the parent left short. Working from Companies House filings, Land Registry title data, and lifestyle evidence that is already public, the operator builds the kind of pack that persuades CMS to open a variation under the Child Support Maintenance Calculation Regulations 2012 — the grounds that let a caseworker look past the tax return: assets over £31,250 not producing income, a lifestyle inconsistent with declared income, unearned income, or income deliberately diverted to reduce the assessment. Where CMS turns the request down, the same pack is rebuilt as a submission for the First-tier Tribunal (Social Entitlement Chamber).

The Child Support Collection (Domestic Abuse) Act 2023 gave CMS much stronger enforcement from 2024-2025: it can now take money straight out of a paying parent's bank account, including a joint or business account, without a court order first, and can ask for a passport or driving licence to be suspended for persistent non-payers. That makes getting the starting number right worth far more than it used to, because collecting the money is no longer the weak link — getting CMS to use the true income is. Every successful variation, with the tribunal reference and the evidence pattern that won it, feeds a private library the operator reuses and sharpens for the next parent, the same compounding-precedent approach that works for services built on summarising contract case law.

The parent receiving maintenance — commonly someone aged 35-55 raising children largely on one income, holding a mortgage and covering school and childcare costs, who believes their self-employed ex-partner is under-declaring income to keep the CMS payment low. They pay one fixed fee, roughly £250 to £600 depending on how much digging the case needs, because a successful variation raises their maintenance for years, not just once.

The Child Support Collection (Domestic Abuse) Act 2023 enforcement powers, including deductions straight from bank and business accounts without a court order, came into force through 2024 and 2025, so CMS collection is now far more reliable once the correct income is on the file. At the same time, HMRC's move toward Making Tax Digital is putting more self-employed financial activity into digital, cross-referenceable form, which makes a mismatch between someone's lifestyle and their declared income easier to evidence from public and semi-public records.
