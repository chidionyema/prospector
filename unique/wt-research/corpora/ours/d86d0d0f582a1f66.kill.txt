BakeMark — the batch-and-allergen sticker roll built for UK home bakers under Natasha's Law.

Pre-printed sticker rolls for UK home bakers that record batch number, the 14 allergens and best-before in one place, so every cake you hand over carries a label that survives a Trading Standards visit.

Each roll is a strip of 250 sequential, pre-formatted stickers a home baker peels and sticks on every cake box, loaf-tin bag and biscuit jar before it leaves the kitchen. The layout is engineered specifically for the Food Information (Amendment) (England) Regulations 2019 — Natasha's Law — which forces any small food business selling pre-packed-for-direct-sale items to carry a full ingredient list plus the 14 EU allergens on every unit. Most home bakers either handwrite their labels, which is slow and easy to miss an allergen, or buy generic kitchen label stickers from Etsy that aren't designed for this regulation. The product borrows the visual discipline of pharmaceutical batch labels — where every pill bottle carries a traceable batch number, manufacture date and recall pathway — and rebuilds it for a tray bake. Each sticker carries a sequential batch code, the bake date, a best-before, the 14 named allergens as tick boxes, an optional 'may contain' line, a customer or order reference, and a place for the baker's signature. Sold at £14.99 per roll on Shopify, Etsy and Amazon, it costs the home baker less than six pence per cake, and saves the panic of re-labelling after a Trading Standards visit or an allergic-customer complaint. The product is run by a solo UK printer who sources the rolls from a domestic label manufacturer, lists them on Amazon Handmade and Etsy, and runs the storefront through Shopify. It is defensible because the layout itself is engineered for one specific UK statutory instrument — Natasha's Law — that no generic kitchen label maker targets, and because the design absorbs future regulation updates as new versions rather than competing on price across thousands of unrelated kitchen uses.
