AidCare Drop-In — the hearing aid aftercare shop where any make or model gets cleaned, retuned and fitted with new tubes in 30 minutes.

A chain of high-street shops that clean, retune and replace parts on any hearing aid in 30 minutes, charging £35 a visit or £18 a month for unlimited care.

Around 18 million hearing aids are worn in the UK, mostly by people aged 65 and over, and almost every one of them needs its tubes, filters and domes swapped every three to six months for it to work properly. NHS audiology departments now routinely book six to twelve months ahead for routine maintenance, and the big private retailers like Specsavers and Boots only service aids they fitted themselves. The result is a fast-growing service gap: every hearing aid sold online by Hearing Direct, Jabra Enhance, Eargo or a high-street independents lands in a customer's hand with no aftercare route. People stop wearing the aid, hearing drops again, and the £800 to £3,000 they spent quietly dies in a drawer.

AidCare Drop-In plugs that gap with a small high-street shop that any hearing aid user can walk into, on any brand, NHS or private. The 30-minute service is run by a trained Hearing Aid Aftercare Practitioner who cleans the device, swaps tubes and filters, retests output against manufacturer settings with a portable hearing aid analyser, and checks the ear mould fit. The £35 walk-in fee covers labour and basic consumables; the £18-a-month plan covers unlimited visits, free tubes and filters, a free annual mould check, and 10 per cent off accessories such as drying kits and dehumidifiers. Earwax inspection is offered with a micro-camera only, so it stays outside the regulated clinical activity and the shop does not need an HCPC-registered dispenser on the rota.

The unit economics work because each shop is small (300 to 500 square feet, £1,200 to £1,800 a month rent on a secondary high street), takes only two staff (one practitioner, one front-of-house), and turns over eight to twelve appointments a day at an average ticket of £35, plus subscription recurring revenue of around £3,500 a month once the local subscriber base passes 200. At five shops inside a single region, the chain clears £20,000 to £35,000 a month operating profit, with the founder and a single operations manager running it from a small head office. The wedge is the subscription: once an older customer is on the monthly plan with their tubes, filters and annual check included, switching away means losing the only place in town that services their aid on a walk-in basis, and most will not bother to find out whether the new shop exists.

Private hearing aid buyers who purchased online or from an independent dispenser and have no aftercare route; NHS hearing aid users who face six to twelve month hospital waits for tube changes; and adult children buying the £18 monthly plan for an elderly parent as a Christmas or birthday present, which is a pattern already proven by hearing aid subscriptions sold direct to families.

The UK population aged 70 and over is projected by the ONS to grow from roughly 8.4 million today to over 12 million by 2040, and hearing aid prevalence rises sharply with age. Online hearing aid sales have grown rapidly since 2022 as brands like Jabra Enhance and Eargo launched in the UK, leaving a fast-growing pool of aids with no local service. NHS audiology departments have not recovered their post-Covid backlog, with routine follow-ups still running six to twelve months in many Integrated Care Boards. The cost-of-living squeeze is also pushing people to keep their existing aids for longer rather than upgrade, which makes ongoing maintenance more valuable, not less.

The passages show manufacturers and third parties already teach and sell the exact fix the shop charges for — dome and tube changes described as something to do 'at home' or 'yourself', with step-by-step guides and direct-to-consumer replacement tubes available online. That undercuts the durability of paying £35 a visit for the recurring task the hypothesis cites as the core pain, since a free self-service route already exists for it.
