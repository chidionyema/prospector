FDA event report pack for AI medical device makers.

A fixed-fee service that drafts the FDA incident report and model update plan a SaMD company must file within 30 days when its AI medical device underperforms in the field.

The firm sells a fixed-fee report pack to a small Software as a Medical Device maker (typically 5 to 30 people, based in California, Massachusetts or Minnesota) that has an FDA-cleared AI medical device on the US market: a radiology triage tool, a cardiac arrhythmia detector, a pathology classifier, something similar. The buyer is the head of regulatory affairs or the VP of quality. The trigger is a field event: a hospital reports the model missed a cancer, a customer complains the model drifted in production, an internal test catches a new bias.

When that happens, federal law (21 CFR Part 803) requires the manufacturer to file a Medical Device Report with FDA within 30 calendar days. The 2024 FDA guidance on AI-enabled medical devices also requires the manufacturer to log the event in its Predetermined Change Control Plan, the file that documents how the manufacturer will monitor and update the model, and to update its quality system under 21 CFR Part 820. The pack contains the drafted MDR narrative, the Predetermined Change Control Plan entry, the customer notification letter, and a complaint handling log entry, which together run 40 to 80 hours of regulatory writing work for a one-person regulatory function that now has 30 days to finish it.

For a Series A or B SaMD company, the regulatory affairs head is often one person wearing many hats, and the work is compressible. Most of the MDR narrative is template-driven (what happened, what the device did, what the patient outcome was, what corrective action is planned), and most of the PCCP entry is structured (model version, monitoring metric, threshold breach, planned retraining). An LLM can draft the bulk of the pack from the company's model monitoring logs, the field complaint, and the prior MDR history. A fixed fee per pack sits below the regulatory affairs contractor rate of roughly $300 per hour and below the cost of a 30-day FDA warning letter if the deadline is missed. The addressable market is the population of FDA-cleared AI/ML medical devices on the FDA's official AI/ML SaMD device list, concentrated in a few hundred small SaMD companies in California, Massachusetts and Minnesota.

The durable value lives in a per-product dataset that grows with each engagement: every MDR filed, every PCCP entry, every FDA Form 483 observation letter, every closeout letter from a 510(k) review. A new entrant without that history starts from zero and has to learn FDA's narrative style and PCCP structure on each new pack. FDA's own eMDR system is a free filing form, not a generator. It cannot draft the clinical narrative from the manufacturer's model logs, cannot update the manufacturer's PCCP, cannot write the customer notification letter, and cannot maintain a per-product event history across many manufacturers. FDA is structurally barred from drafting on behalf of a regulated party, from arbitrating the manufacturer's commercial relationship with its hospital customers, and from carrying the manufacturer's confidential complaint handling data across product lines.

This borrows the workflow pharmaceutical companies already use to process adverse drug event reports: a narrative case report, a label update, a prescriber notification letter, all kept in a per-product safety database. The shape of the work is the same. The regulator and the form numbers change.
