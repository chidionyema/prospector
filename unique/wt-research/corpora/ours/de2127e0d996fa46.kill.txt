PitchLoop Studio — the small band's tour merch run, printed and shipped to the venue the morning of the show.

For UK gig-working musicians on small tours, we print your merch in small batches and deliver each box to the venue on show day, so you sell without carrying stock.

A band on a 12-date UK tour makes most of its money at the merch table, not from streaming. The problem is stock. You must guess sizes and quantities weeks ahead, pay for the whole run up front, then carry unsold boxes in a van for a month. Musicians in their early twenties, driving a hired Transit and sleeping on floors, do not have £900 of dead cash to gamble on shirt sizes.

This is a printing and delivery service, not an app. You send us your tour dates from your booking sheet. We print small quantities and courier a box to each venue's load-in address to arrive the morning of the show. What you don't sell, we collect and re-route to the next city. You pay per item printed, not for a minimum run.

The angle borrows from how bakeries handle bread. A bakery does not bake a month of loaves on the first day. It bakes daily, close to the counter, and accepts a slightly higher cost per loaf in exchange for never eating the waste. Merch has been sold the opposite way for forty years, because screen printing rewarded huge runs. Direct-to-garment printing changed that economics and nobody rebuilt the service around it.

What holds it is the venue relationship, not the printer. Getting a box accepted at a venue before doors is genuinely hard. Venues do not take deliveries at random hours, load-in doors open at fixed times, and a courier who turns up at 10am to a locked pub gets a failed delivery. Building the contact list of duty managers and load-in windows at the roughly 400 UK grassroots venues on the Music Venue Trust's register takes a year of phone calls, and it compounds — every failed delivery teaches us a real constraint about that room. A national print firm cannot run that; a band cannot either.

Pricing: £14 per shirt printed and delivered, versus roughly £6 wholesale on a 200-run the band must fund up front and store. The band's cash risk drops from £900 to nearly nothing. Our margin is roughly £5 a unit after print, blanks and courier, on typical runs of 30-60 items a show.

We run it with two people and a printer. Everything else is a courier account and a phone.

Self-releasing UK bands and solo artists aged 18-27 playing 10-30 date tours of grassroots venues — the ones booking themselves through agents like ATC Live's smaller roster or direct with promoters. They pay per item, at the point of print. Also artist managers running two or three small acts, who currently front the merch cash themselves.

Grassroots touring costs have risen sharply and the Music Venue Trust reported venue closures through 2023-2024, pushing more artists onto shorter, tighter tours where carrying stock is impossible. Meanwhile direct-to-garment printing has made runs of 20 items economic, where a decade ago screen printing needed 100+ to make sense. And since Brexit, merch stock crossing to EU dates now carries customs paperwork, so printing locally per city removes a headache artists complain about constantly.
