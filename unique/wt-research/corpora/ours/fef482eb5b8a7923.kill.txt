TMHP manual updates turned into PA templates for vendors.

For Texas digital health founders, a model builds TMHP-compliant prior authorization packets per the Texas Medicaid manual, so your customers stop losing reimbursement to bad paperwork.

The Texas Medicaid & Healthcare Partnership (TMHP) administers Texas Medicaid's prior authorization (PA) process and publishes the Texas Medicaid Provider Procedures Manual - more than 4,000 pages specifying exactly what clinical and administrative documentation must accompany each PA request by procedure code. A Texas digital health vendor selling PA automation to Texas providers and managed care organizations (MCOs) must keep its templates in sync with the manual, or its customers see denied claims and lost reimbursement.

Today this is a 2 to 3 FTE compliance team's job, reading quarterly manual updates and rebuilding per-code templates. The tool ingests each TMHP manual update, generates per-code PA packet templates with required clinical elements and attachments, and ships them as structured content the vendor's software can use. Pricing is a monthly subscription keyed to procedure code coverage, starting at $3,000 a month. It broadens TMHP manual compliance from a 2 to 3 FTE compliance team to a vendor-side tool any digital health startup can buy.

What compounds over time is the proprietary data: a structured corpus of TMHP manual revisions matched to historical denial reasons (TMHP publishes denial codes as public data) - a corpus no generic vendor can replicate for Texas specifically. TMHP publishes the manual but cannot help vendors monetize it; the agency's mandate is policy, not vendor tooling. Multi-state PA vendors (Cohere Health, Olive) treat Texas as one of many markets and lack the Texas-specific denial-code corpus that compounds with each new manual revision.
