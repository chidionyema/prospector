Business rates challenge service for UK shop owners.

A web app that checks whether business rates are excessive and prepares a ready-to-submit appeal for a fixed fee.

You run a cafe, a corner shop or a small workshop. Business rates are one of your biggest fixed costs — often over £10,000 a year. The 2023 revaluation may have overvalued your property, but appealing it takes evidence and expertise you don’t have. RateRebase uses publicly available rental evidence and machine learning to compare your property with similar ones, spotting grounds for a reduction. It then generates a complete appeal pack with a valuation argument, ready to send to the Valuation Office Agency. This borrows the model of automated US property tax appeal services and applies it to the UK’s business rates system. The tool charges a fixed fee of £149 for the assessment and, if you choose to proceed, a success fee of 25% of the first year’s saving. Over time, it accumulates a proprietary dataset of what evidence the VOA accepts, making its predictions stronger and creating a data moat rivals cannot easily copy.

Independent retail, hospitality and service business owners in the UK with a physical storefront, typically managing 1–5 locations, who feel business rates are eating their margins but lack the time or know-how to challenge them.

The 2023 business rates revaluation in England and Wales took effect in April 2023, and the window to appeal against the 2023 rating list ends on 31 March 2026. Many small businesses were hit by large increases just as wider costs surged. The VOA’s own check-and-challenge system is intimidating, and most ratepayers won’t use it effectively — creating a narrow window of acute need and a large, solvent market.

VOA figures show 57% of firms that challenge their business rates bill secure a reduction, so the saving from a successful challenge is real and still available.
