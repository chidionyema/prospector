PayFloor — the statutory pay audit that recovers what your platform should have paid.

For Gen Z couriers who never see holiday pay, a no-win, no-fee audit that finds what the platform owes and files the claim to recover it.

Most Gen Z delivery riders and platform workers are engaged as 'self-employed contractors' by their platform — but many should legally be classified as 'workers' under the Supreme Court's Uber BV v Aslam (2021) UKSC 5 ruling, which entitles them to the National Minimum Wage and 5.6 weeks of paid annual leave under the Working Time Regulations 1998. This service audits 24 months of platform work, calculates what the platform should have paid, and files the tribunal claim to recover it.

The audit works by ingesting every payslip, fee statement and shift export from the rider's apps, computing the gap between actual pay and the statutory minimum, then triggering ACAS early conciliation and (if needed) filing an employment tribunal claim under Regulation 30 of the Working Time Regulations 1998. Typical recoveries run £1,500-£4,500 per worker, against a contingency fee of £500 paid only on success — accessible to gen_z_workers with no savings buffer. Distribution is direct via TikTok and Instagram to gen_z_worker audiences, plus referral partnerships with the IWGB, GMB and Community unions, whose members are exactly the people who don't realise they have these rights.

The moat is the proprietary audit templates, fee calculation engine, and tribunal bundle structure built up over hundreds of claims (technical IP that takes years to assemble). ACAS and Citizens Advice provide general guidance but do not calculate the exact back-pay owed, prepare the tribunal bundle, or represent at the hearing; employment lawyers charge £250-£450/hour with no fixed-fee product for gen_z_workers. A copycat would need to replicate 24 months of WTR 1998 and NMW 1998 case-law analysis, the Section 23 ERA 1996 filing logic, and the relationships with the unions that refer claimants — none of which is a regulator's free first-party tool.
