SwapSeat, sells your unused school fee term back.

A broker that finds a family to take over your child's paid-but-unused private school term, so you get most of your money back instead of nothing.

UK private schools almost universally require a full term's notice in writing before a child leaves. Miss it and you owe a whole term's fees — often £5,000 to £15,000 — for a seat your child will never sit in. Meanwhile the same school has a waiting list of families who would start in January if a place opened.

SwapSeat brokers the handover. When a parent decides mid-term to move their child — a job relocation, a divorce, a bursary that fell through, or the VAT rise pushing the budget past breaking — we find a replacement family already on that school's waiting list and negotiate a fee novation with the bursar. The leaving parent recovers a large share of the term they've already committed to. The arriving family gets a place months early. We take a percentage of the sum recovered, paid only on completion.

The angle here is to build the thing that gets MORE valuable the harder the school enforces its notice clause. A school cannot run this itself and never will: it has no incentive to help a departing parent recover money it is contractually owed, and doing so for one family invites every family to ask. That conflict is structural, not a gap in their software. We sit on the parent's side of the table, which the school cannot occupy.

What compounds is the bursar relationship and the waiting-list intelligence. After a few dozen completions we know which schools have genuine demand behind which year groups, which bursars will novate and which won't, and what discount actually clears. A new entrant has neither the bursar's phone number nor the record of what cleared last term.

The legal ground is ordinary contract law: parent contracts are between the school and the family, and a school may agree to release one family and admit another. Nothing here requires a licence. We are not giving financial advice and we do not hold client money — the school refunds the parent directly and invoices us for our fee.

Parents aged 35-55 paying private day school fees of £15,000-£25,000 a year who have decided mid-term to withdraw a child. The fee comes out of money they recover, so the decision is easy for a couple deliberating together. Second payer: the incoming family, who pay a smaller placement fee for jumping a waiting list.

From 1 January 2025 VAT at 20% applies to private school fees in the UK, and the Independent Schools Council reported pupil numbers falling in the year that followed. Mid-year withdrawals are running well above their historic level, and every one of them collides with a full-term notice clause. At the same time, oversubscribed schools in London and the South East still hold real waiting lists, so a leaver and a joiner exist in the same building at the same moment.
