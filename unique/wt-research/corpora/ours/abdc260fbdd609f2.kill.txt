Chain Rescue Boxes — the pre-cut, exact-fit chain and sprocket kit for one specific motorbike, posted to a courier rider's door.

A UK online shop selling one thing well: a boxed, correctly-sized chain and sprocket set for the handful of small motorbikes food-delivery riders actually use, delivered next day.

This is a small online shop, run by one person, selling a single physical product line: a sealed box containing a drive chain cut to the exact link count, a front sprocket, a rear sprocket, a split link, a chain breaker loan card, and a one-page fitting sheet with photos.

It only covers the bikes that dominate UK food delivery: the Honda PCX125, the Yamaha NMAX 125, the Lexmoto Echo, the Honda CB125F and the Keeway RKS 125. Nothing else. A rider types their number plate, the shop returns one box, and that is the whole choice.

The idea borrows the way replacement water filter cartridges are sold for fridges. Nobody browses a catalogue of filters. You give the fridge model, you get the one cartridge that fits, and the seller wins on being certain rather than on being cheap. Motorbike chains are sold the opposite way today, as loose parts in a wall of pitch sizes and link counts, which is why riders buy the wrong thing.

The rider pain is sharp and weekly. A courier doing 200 miles a week on a chain-drive 125 wears a chain out in three to five months, far faster than a commuter. When it snaps or jumps, the bike is off the road, and a Deliveroo or Just Eat rider loses every hour it is in a shop. Independent motorbike garages in London and Manchester routinely quote a week's wait for a chain and sprocket job in summer.

The durable part is not the parts, which anyone can buy. It is the fitment table. Every order records the plate, the DVLA vehicle record, the actual chain pitch found on the bike, the link count that worked, and whether the rider reported a mismatch. After a few hundred orders that table knows things no supplier catalogue does, because manufacturers changed sprocket teeth mid-model-year and grey-import Lexmoto and Keeway machines differ from their own published specs. That data is generated only by shipping boxes and hearing back, so a rival starting today cannot buy it.

One person runs this. Stock is five to eight box variants, chains cut with a bench tool at home, packed in a padded envelope under 2kg for Royal Mail 48. No premises, no licence needed to sell motorbike parts to consumers, though the Consumer Rights Act 2015 and the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 mean a 14-day return right must be honoured and stated.

Distribution is owned and free. Rider WhatsApp and Telegram groups for each London zone, the r/deliveroos and r/UberEatsDrivers subreddits, and the car parks outside McDonald's branches where riders queue are where these buyers already gather. A short video of a chain being cut to the right length for a named bike is the entire marketing asset.

Pricing sits around £75 to £95 a box against a garage bill of £180 to £250 including labour, so the margin is real on parts bought at trade from UK distributors.

At the same time independent motorbike garages have thinned out, so the wait for a routine chain job has stretched. Grey-import Chinese 125 brands such as Lexmoto and Keeway now make up a large share of new registrations in the class, and their parts are exactly the ones mainstream chain suppliers describe badly or not at all.
