The Dead Stock Buyer for Independent Pharmacies.

A solo operator purchases expiring or slow-moving over-the-counter inventory from independent pharmacies at a steep discount, then repackages and sells it to veterinary clinics and nursing homes at a margin, solving the pharmacist's cash-flow and waste problem.

Cross-sector borrowing of the 'dead stock buyer' model from retail fashion (where liquidators buy end-of-season inventory) applied to pharmacy: independent pharmacies (with 1-20 staff) regularly write off OTC stock nearing expiry because they lack volume to sell through it. A solo operator can acquire this inventory for pennies, perform basic relabeling and lot-checking, and resell it to veterinary clinics and care homes—buyers who are price-sensitive but legally can use human-grade stock within expiry dates. The pharmacist gets immediate cash for a zero-value asset; the solo operator captures a 40-60% margin on each deal. Distribution is owned via direct outreach to pharmacy chains and vet practices; the moat is proprietary knowledge of which SKUs cross-apply and the logistical flow to move fast on expiry windows.

Independent community pharmacists in the UK (pharmacy owners with 1-20 employees, handling cash-flow stress and margin pressure from NHS reimbursement cuts).

NHS pharmacy funding cuts (2024-25) have squeezed margins; independent pharmacists are more willing to monetise dead stock. Meanwhile, veterinary costs have risen 30%+, pushing vets to seek cheaper alternatives, and care home regulations allow human-grade OTC stock if properly labelled. The gap between pharmacy waste and vet/care home demand is widening and still unbrokered.
