CrashProof Kit — test a used child car seat for hidden structural damage before you buy.

A home-use load test kit that helps parents check a second-hand car seat's crash-worthiness, so they can buy used without the worry.

Parents keen to save on childcare essentials buy second-hand car seats on Facebook Marketplace and Gumtree but have no way to tell if a seat’s crash‑absorbing structure is intact after an unseen impact. The CrashProof Kit fills that gap. It includes a calibrated load cell that clips onto the seat’s harness, a smartphone app that guides the user through a standard pull test, and a database of new‑seat resistance benchmarks built from manufacturer‑published specifications and anonymised test results. The app compares the live reading with the expected range and gives a clear pass or investigate‑further result. It works with the two UK car seat regulations parents meet every day: the older UN ECE Reg 44.04 and the newer R129 (i‑Size). Because every test feeds back into the dataset, the analysis sharpens with each use, making it harder for a copycat to offer the same accuracy without an equivalent crowd‑sourced history. The kit is sold directly online, and a solo operator can run everything — from order fulfilment to app updates and customer queries — with ordinary e‑commerce tools and an AI assistant that interprets the sensor data and automates support.

Budget‑conscious parents who work in public‑sector jobs — nurses, teachers, council staff — and need to keep childcare costs down without gambling on safety. They buy the kit as a one‑time purchase to use whenever a second‑hand seat catches their eye.

The cost‑of‑living squeeze has sent more families to second‑hand markets, at the same time as media reports of counterfeit and damaged car seats appearing online have made parents nervous. Fusing the circular‑economy instinct with a physical safety check gives parents a way to buy used with confidence — something neither the manufacturer nor a regulator’s static advice sheet can provide.
