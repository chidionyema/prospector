CareContract Locker – the child caregiver’s platform for getting paid by Mum and Dad, with a contract that stops the state taking the house.

A digital platform where an elderly parent and their adult child caregiver sign a binding care agreement, log hours, and process payments, creating a paper trail that shields the family home from Medi‑Cal recovery.

In California, Medi‑Cal can place a lien on a deceased recipient’s home to recover long‑term care costs, but an adult child who provided care that kept the parent out of a nursing home can claim an exemption or offset. To succeed, they need a written personal care agreement that spells out services and payment, signed before the care began. Most families never set one up and face a battle with the state when the parent dies. CareContract Locker is a transactional platform that walks the carer through building a legally sound agreement, tracking hours, and processing actual payments from parent to caregiver. Each family uses the platform to transact privately — it is not a benefits claim. The platform collects a monthly subscription of $39 from the caregiver or a small fee of 2% per payment. Because it formalises a genuine transaction between family members, it holds up under state scrutiny. Over time the platform amasses a proprietary dataset of what documentation Medi‑Cal auditors accept and reject, creating a moat that rivals cannot copy. As Medi‑Cal estate recovery becomes more aggressive, the value of having a bulletproof contract grows. The state cannot offer a tool that helps families shield assets from its own claims; the platform is inherently adversarial to the recovery arm of the Department of Health Care Services.

The adult child caregiver pays a monthly subscription of $39 to use the platform, or a 2% fee on each payment they receive from the parent. In some cases the elderly parent’s estate ultimately benefits, but the caregiver is the one who feels the acute pain of losing the family home and will seek out and buy the solution.

California’s rapidly aging population is producing a surge in informal caregiving. Medi‑Cal estate recovery is already aggressive, and recent legislation has tightened the rules around what counts as a valid pre‑paid care contract. Families now need watertight documentation from day one.
