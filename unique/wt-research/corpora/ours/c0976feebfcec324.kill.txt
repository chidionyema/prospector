PGCB vendor approvals, for PA sportsbook AI.

Generates PA Gaming Control Board vendor approval packets for sports betting AI vendors selling to PA sportsbook operators under Act 42 of 2017.

This is a sells-to-tech tool for founders and Heads of Compliance at PA-based sports betting AI vendors (oddsmakers, fraud detection, KYC, player tracking, marketing compliance) seeking PA Gaming Control Board approval to sell to PA sportsbooks. The PA Sports Wagering Act (4 Pa.C.S. § 1201 et seq., enacted as Act 42 of 2017) requires PGCB vendor approval before any third-party software touches a licensed PA sportsbook platform, and the approval is at PGCB's discretion based on internal scoring criteria that are not published in detail.

This borrows the way FDA medical device clearance uses internal reviewer scoring against an unpublished rubric, and applies it to PA PGCB vendor approvals which use the same discretionary scoring model. PA currently has 14 licensed online sportsbooks (FanDuel PA, DraftKings PA, BetMGM PA, and 11 smaller operators), each onboarding multiple AI vendors as the platforms rebuild odds, fraud, and marketing stacks. The vendor approval takes 60 to 180 days and the vendor's launch schedule slips. The tool replaces the 30 to 60 hour gaming regulatory lawyer engagement at a $5,000 fixed fee per vendor approval.

It works because the PGCB publishes vendor approval requirements at a high level, but the discretionary scoring is internal, and the vendor needs to anticipate what PGCB will ask. The wedge is the proprietary database of past PGCB vendor approval decisions and the discretionary scoring patterns observed at PGCB public meetings. The strongest free alternative is the PGCB's published vendor approval requirements page; the PGCB structurally cannot publish its internal scoring without compromising its discretion, and a gaming regulatory lawyer cannot scale to a $5,000 fixed fee per approval.
