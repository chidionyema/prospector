ShiftFresh — overnight scrub laundering for NHS shift workers, timed to your rota.

A doorstep laundry round that collects NHS staff scrubs and uniforms after a shift and returns them washed to the right standard before the next one starts.

Most NHS staff on rotating or night shifts are expected to launder their own uniform at home. NHS Employers' 2010 uniform and workwear guidance, still the reference point most trusts point staff to, says washing should be at 60°C, or a lower temperature with a laundry disinfectant, and that uniform should not be worn travelling to or from work where avoidable. Very few community and ward staff have access to an on-site hospital laundry any more, because many trusts have centralised or outsourced their linen services and stopped offering a staff laundering service, so the guidance quietly becomes the individual worker's problem.

The daily friction is timing, not willingness. A nurse finishing a 9pm-to-7am run does not want to come home and put on a hot wash before sleeping, then be woken by the machine, then find the load still damp for the next shift twelve hours later. Running two or three hot washes a week also costs more with today's electricity prices than most people notice until they add it up.

This takes the model of an overnight hotel linen or nappy laundering round — collect dirty, return clean on a fixed cycle, no ironing-app browsing required — and points it at one specific, recurring collection window: a hospital site's shift handover times. A solo operator collects scrub sets from a car park pickup point or staff locker at shift end, washes to the 60°C/disinfectant standard the guidance describes, and has them back before the worker's next shift, on a simple weekly subscription. The pitch to the trust's staff wellbeing or occupational health team is that it is one way for shift staff to actually meet the uniform guidance without cutting sleep, which is the kind of thing that gets a flyer on the staff intranet rather than a contract — no procurement process needed for a worker paying out of their own pocket.

Individual NHS shift workers — ward nurses, healthcare assistants, paramedics and community nurses on rotating or night shifts — who pay a weekly or monthly fee directly, the way they'd pay for a gym or parking permit; some trusts already pay a small annual home-laundering allowance that can offset it.

Hospital laundries have kept consolidating onto fewer regional sites, so staff at outlying wards and community teams increasingly have no on-site laundering option at all. Energy prices have made home hot washes noticeably more expensive since 2022, and the NHS long-term workforce plan keeps pushing more staff onto rotating and night shifts to cover gaps, so the group with no time to launder properly keeps growing.
