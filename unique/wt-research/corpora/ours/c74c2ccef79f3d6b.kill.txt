Vendor Cull, we cancel the AI tools your staff bought.

A fixed-fee service that finds every AI subscription your agency's staff expensed, cancels the dead ones, and hands you signed proof each quarter.

This sells to technology buyers: the owner of a 15-to-60-person marketing, design or development agency in the UK. It commits to shape (C), selling to a named budget-holding role about a recurring chore anchored to public rules.

Start from the raw pain rather than how software solves it today. A designer expenses Midjourney on a personal card. A developer puts Cursor on the company Amex. An account manager signs up for an AI meeting notetaker that quietly joins client calls. Nobody cancels anything. The agency owner sees one line in Xero called "software" and has no idea what is inside it.

The service is deliberately human-first and boring. Each quarter we take read-only access to the agency's card statements and accounting ledger, plus the Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 admin list of third-party apps staff have connected. We produce three things: a list of every AI tool in use with who bought it and what it costs, a set of cancellations we execute on the owner's behalf, and a signed record of which tools were allowed to keep processing client data.

That third deliverable is the part an agency cannot get anywhere else, and it is why the work recurs. Under UK data protection law, an agency handling a client's customer data is a processor, and Article 28(2) of the UK GDPR says a processor must not bring in another processor without the client's written permission. An AI notetaker sitting in a client call is exactly that. Agencies routinely sign master service agreements with enterprise clients that require a current list of sub-processors on request. Today most agency owners answer that question from memory.

We answer it from a ledger. Every quarter's cull adds another dated snapshot of what that specific agency ran, on whose card, touching which client. After four quarters the owner has a defensible history no new supplier can reconstruct, because the raw material is the agency's own past statements and app-connection logs. Ripping us out means starting the record from zero on the day a client's procurement team asks for it.

Pricing is a fixed quarterly fee of around £900 for agencies under 30 staff, with a smaller ongoing fee for the record itself. Distribution runs through the accountants and bookkeepers who already serve creative agencies, and through the professional indemnity brokers who ask agencies about AI use at renewal.

The work automates well. Statement parsing, app-connection pulls and the drafting of cancellation emails are mechanical. What stays human is the judgement call on which tools touch client data, and that judgement is what the fee actually buys.
