GigRight — The Gen Z Gig Worker's Worker-Status Entitlement Claim Pack.

A fixed-fee service that turns your gig-work answers into a tailored legal claim for holiday pay, minimum wage and sick pay, using worker-status law and platform-specific data.

Many UK gig workers are legally 'workers' rather than self‑employed, entitling them to holiday pay, sick pay, the minimum wage and pension contributions.  Platforms almost never tell them this, and asserting those rights feels impossible without a solicitor.  GigRight compresses the process into a fixed‑fee web app.  The worker answers questions about their work pattern, control, substitution rights and the platform's terms.  The engine cross‑references those answers with a proprietary database of past Employment Tribunal findings, ACAS early conciliation outcomes and platform‑specific arguments.  It then generates a pre‑action letter and a claim pack ready for ACAS and, if needed, the tribunal — with a plain‑English explanation of why the worker status test is met.  This borrows the way dispute‑resolution services in construction use a fast, pre‑scripted route to force a decision, transplanting that model into the gig economy.  The moat is a compounding proprietary dataset: every claim settled or decided feeds back into the engine, improving success rates and making the service harder for a late‑comer to replicate without that data.

Gen Z gig workers (rideshare, delivery, freelance platforms) who suspect they are underpaid on holiday or basic pay. They pay a fixed fee of £99, either upfront or via a Pay in 3 from a provider like Klarna – a price that is quickly recouped from a successful claim.

Since the 2021 Uber BV v Aslam Supreme Court ruling, HMRC and the Department for Business & Trade have intensified enforcement of worker status.  Hundreds of gig workers now bring successful claims for back‑dated holiday pay and minimum wage, but the process is still a maze.  Platforms rarely volunteer the rights, and ACAS conciliation is neutral rather than strategic.  This creates an immediate window for a productised service that equips individuals to enforce their entitlements without a lawyer.
