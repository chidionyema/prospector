CareDelayBack — recovers money you spent on care while your council dragged its feet.

Recovers privately-paid care costs for unpaid carers when the local authority fails to assess needs on time, for a cut of what's won.

Unpaid carers who pay for home care out of their own pocket while waiting for a local authority needs assessment can reclaim those costs when the council's delay breaches its duty under the Care Act 2014. The service pieces together the timeline, links it to the council's published assessment delays, and packages a claim that cites Ombudsman decisions and statutory guidance—all for a percentage of the sum recovered. This borrows from the way holiday-claims firms bundled flight-delay compensation into a factory process, but applies it to a forgotten market where nothing existed until the backlog became undeniable. The Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman has repeatedly found councils at fault for these delays, and the financial pressure on carers is acute. Because the broker collects a dossier of payment receipts, care logs, and council correspondence, it builds a proprietary dataset of what arguments succeed with which authorities—a pattern library that gets sharper with every case and is unrealistic for a one-off claimant or generalist adviser to replicate. A solo operator running a document-intake engine and an AI-driven letter generator can handle a caseload that a high-street solicitor would never touch at the same price point.

The primary carer pays nothing upfront; the broker takes a 25% success fee from the recovered amount. Carers find the service via carer forums, local Age UK partnerships, or word of mouth in condition-specific support groups.

Council care-assessment backlogs have ballooned since 2020, with some authorities taking over six months for an assessment that the Care Act expects within weeks. The LGSCO has upheld dozens of complaints about delays causing financial loss, and media scrutiny is sharpening. Yet no product exists to turn those findings into a low-friction refund mechanism for the carer who has already spent the money.
