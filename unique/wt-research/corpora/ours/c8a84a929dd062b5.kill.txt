The Solo Tenant's Snagging Warrant.

A productized service where a solo surveyor performs a legally-admissible pre-move-out inspection of a rented flat in the UK, identifying all deposit-deductible damages with photographic evidence and a fixed-price repair quote, enabling the tenant to pre-empt the landlord's claim.

By analogizing the 'snagging' process from new-build home purchases (where buyers hire an inspector to find defects before the developer's warranty expires) to the rental deposit dispute process, a solo operator can serve tenants who face opaque, often inflated deposit deductions. Landlords have structural incentives to make claims; tenants lack the expertise to counter them. The service provides a documented, timestamped baseline that arbitration bodies (like TDS) accept, shifting the power balance. The solo operator uses a drone camera and a proprietary checklist to document every room in 30 minutes, generating a report and a pre-negotiated repair service via local handymen.

Private renters in the UK (particularly students and young professionals in London, Manchester, Birmingham) who are about to vacate a property and have a deposit of £500–£3,000 at risk. They pay a fixed fee (£80–£150) per inspection.

The UK rental market saw a record 1.7 million deposit disputes in 2023, with an average deduction of £350. Tenants are increasingly aware of their rights through social media (e.g., TikTok #depositdispute) but lack a tangible, affordable tool. The Tenant Fees Act 2019 banned many landlord fees, making deposit deductions a primary profit lever for landlords, creating a growing adversarial market.
