TicketCut – the delivery rider’s PCN appeal service that uses your shift data to prove you were working.

A fixed-fee service that challenges parking tickets for delivery riders by gathering platform evidence to claim the loading exemption.

This borrows the way specialist parking appeal firms work, but applies it exclusively to food delivery riders by integrating real‑time shift data from apps like Deliveroo and Uber Eats. When a rider gets a Penalty Charge Notice for stopping on a yellow line or restricted street, the service pulls the delivery timestamp and location from the platform’s digital record – proving the stop was for loading or unloading. A custom appeal is then generated citing the statutory exemption under the Traffic Signs Regulations and General Directions 2016, and submitted to the council.

Why it works: most riders pay the £65–£130 penalty because they lack the time or know‑how to fight; councils count on this. The service turns their own phone data into irrefutable evidence, offering a streamlined, repeatable process for a flat fee of £18 per ticket – less than a quarter of the penalty.

Why it holds: free online templates exist (Pepipoo, MoneySavingExpert), but they don’t automatically harvest personalised shift logs or tailor arguments to a specific local authority’s adjudication patterns. The solo operator compounds a proprietary dataset of which argument phrasing succeeds at which tribunal, encoded into generation prompts that improve with every case – a data moat no forum or free tool can match.

The delivery rider themselves, typically spending £18 per appeal out of a potential £65+ saving. They have budget authority over their own fines and are acutely time‑pressed, preferring a low‑cost, done‑for‑you fix.

London boroughs and Transport for London are expanding camera enforcement on red routes and clearways, issuing a rising volume of PCNs to delivery riders who briefly stop. At the same time, the gig platforms’ shift logs now capture precise stop locations and timestamps, creating a new evidence trail that was previously unavailable to riders – a combination that makes a data‑driven appeal service both newly possible and urgently needed.
