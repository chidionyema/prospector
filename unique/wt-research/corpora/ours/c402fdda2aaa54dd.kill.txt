ListReady — one product record that fills out every supermarket's range-review form.

A self-serve tool for small UK food and drink producers that keeps one master product record and auto-fills each supermarket's own range-review paperwork, so one ingredient change updates every retailer's form at once.

Independent food and drink brands stocked in UK supermarkets go through a range review with each retailer every 6 to 18 months. Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Morrisons, Co-op and Ocado each run their own portal and their own spreadsheet layout, covering the nutrition panel, the allergen matrix required under Natasha's Law, pack dimensions, BRCGS or Red Tractor certificate numbers, and GS1 UK barcode data. A small brand selling into four or five of these retailers ends up keeping four or five different spreadsheets by hand, and a single recipe change or new supplier means updating every one of them separately, under deadline, with delisting as the penalty for getting it wrong.

ListReady lets the founder or the one person doing ops enter the product data once. The tool then maps that single record into the exact columns, units and code lists each retailer's range-review system expects, because Tesco's layout is not Sainsbury's layout, which is not Asda's. When the next range review comes round, it re-exports automatically instead of starting from scratch.

This rebuilds the problem from the ground up rather than treating it as another compliance checklist: the real cost is not knowing the rules, it's re-typing the same facts into five incompatible boxes, again and again, under a countdown. Once a brand's SKUs, certificates and retailer-specific mappings live inside the tool, pulling them back out and rebuilding that history by hand under deadline pressure is expensive enough that switching away is a real decision, not a click. And because many small suppliers who all sell into the same handful of major retailers use the same tool, it builds up a working map of exactly which fields, formats and quirks each retailer's buying team actually enforces — detail the retailers themselves never publish cleanly, and that no single supplier's spreadsheet ever accumulates on its own.

Growth is self-serve: sign up free for one retailer and one product, pay per extra retailer or extra SKU as the range grows. The people who spread it are co-packers, brokers and new-product-development consultants who work across dozens of small brands at once and hit the same range-review pain with every new client.

The owner or the one person running commercial and ops at a small UK food or drink producer, roughly 1 to 20 staff, already supplying two or more national supermarkets. Right now they lose days each range-review cycle retyping the same facts into different retailer templates, and missing a deadline risks losing a listing worth tens of thousands of pounds a year in sales.

Retailers keep digitising and tightening what a range review demands — wider allergen data fields since Natasha's Law took effect in October 2021, ongoing updates to Tesco's supplier product-data portal, and GS1 UK's continuing push for standardised product data across grocery. Small producers are still running this process on spreadsheets and email, so the gap between what a retailer's system now expects digitally and what a small back office can turn around by hand keeps widening.
