RosterWatch — the agency that finds every stolen model photo online and gets your agency paid or the image pulled down.

A specialist takedown and recovery agency for UK modelling and talent agencies, hunting down unauthorised and AI-faked use of their roster's photos across the whole internet and turning each one into a removal or a paid licence.

A UK modelling or talent agency signs the model, shoots the photos, and then loses control of them completely. The same face turns up on a dropshipping site selling knock-off skincare, in a Facebook ad for a product the model never touched, cropped into a deepfake video, or resold to a print catalogue nobody licensed. Each one is a breach of the agency's contract with the photographer or client, and often of the model's own image rights, but the agency has no one whose job it is to go looking, and no time to chase it once someone spots it by chance.

This is a small agency of investigators and an IP paralegal, not a platform. We build and run a face-matching and reverse-image crawler tuned specifically for modelling and headshot photography, so it catches cropped, filtered, colour-graded and AI face-swapped reuses that a plain Google or TinEye search misses. Every match becomes a case file: where it was found, who is running the ad or site, and which contract or copyright claim applies. We send the takedown notice or the invoice for a backdated usage licence, and we take a cut of anything we actually recover, plus a small monthly retainer to keep the roster under watch.

Why this holds: the value is the continuously-improving matching model and the growing case history of who tends to steal modelling images and how (the same ad networks, the same catalogue resellers, the same deepfake sites recur), which a firm doing this ad hoc for one client at a time never builds. A free reverse-image search finds an exact pixel match; it does not detect a face-swapped composite, does not draft an enforceable notice under the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 or the agency's own model contracts, and does not chase the infringer for a settlement. The agency owner is not equipped to run that chase themselves, and no incumbent gives it away, because doing it properly needs both the detection tooling and the follow-through.

The owner or head of bookings at a small to mid-sized UK modelling or talent agency — typically representing 20 to 300 signed models or influencers — who pays a monthly roster-monitoring retainer plus a percentage of any takedown fee or backdated licence fee actually recovered, because every unlicensed reuse dilutes their models' exclusivity deals and their own commission on legitimate bookings.

The Online Safety Act 2023 made sharing a non-consensual deepfake intimate image a criminal offence from January 2024, and the Advertising Standards Authority has been increasingly cracking down on AI face-swapped product ads on Meta and TikTok, giving agencies a live legal and reputational reason to act rather than shrug it off. At the same time, cheap AI face-swap and image-generation tools have made it trivial for dropshipping sellers and counterfeit ad networks to lift a model's face onto a product they never endorsed, multiplying the number of infringements any one agency's roster now attracts each month.

A photography agency actually sued over unauthorised image use and sought damages, showing agencies do act (and spend) to enforce image rights when misuse occurs, and three separate reverse-image-search tools compete for exactly this 'find where my image is used' search demand, evidencing the underlying buyer behaviour even though it is currently served free.
