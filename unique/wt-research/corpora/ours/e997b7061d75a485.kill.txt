DriveSheet — the self-employed PHV driver's lease-aware shift planner.

A SaaS for self-employed UK PHV drivers that joins their three ride-hail platforms and their EV lease into one daily shift decision, so they don't lose money to the lease.

DriveSheet is a SaaS for self-employed private-hire vehicle drivers in the UK, built for the Gen Z cohort now running EV-only leases across Uber, Bolt, FreeNow and Addison Lee from a single driver seat. The product joins the driver's platform payouts, weekly lease obligation, insurance premium and mileage into one daily decision: today's best shift starts where, runs on which platform, and ends with what bankable return after the lease payment leaves the account. The driver plugs their lease terms in once (an Onto, Drive-Electric, Smart Lease or LeasePlan contract is the common case) and the product maps each day's three or four platforms onto a single shift score, with the EV's real-world range penalty and the TfL licence renewal calendar baked in.

The lasting advantage sits in technical IP built around the few PHV-specific inputs that genuinely compound over a driver's career: the lease, which is rigid and unforgiving across 12 to 24 months and triggers excess-mileage penalties at contract end; and the cross-platform earnings record, which gets richer with every shift. After nine months the product holds a year-end lease-return pack (driven mileage, city mix, top-paid zones, insurance no-claims-discount ladder) that a spreadsheet cannot reconstruct cleanly, alongside a platform-switch recommendation the driver uses when Uber or Bolt each dropped pay rates through 2024 and 2025.

Acquisition uses three UK-specific repeatable motions: a self-serve web app that PHV driver trainers (The PHV Course, Drive 24/7, Uber's partner schools) point new multi-platform drivers at; an exclusive deal with one EV lease operator for warm leads a competitor cannot reach; and social-led growth inside active UK PHV-driver Discord communities, the r/uberdriversuk subreddit, and YouTube PHV creator channels. Self-serve keeps unit economics positive at £9.99 a month or £59.50 a quarter, and the trial triggers off a real fear: 'I lose £80 if I take the wrong shift tonight', so the trial-to-paid window is short.

Self-employed PHV drivers in London and other TfL-licensed cities, many in their late twenties or early thirties, often in their first year or two after moving from Deliveroo or a salaried role, who have signed multi-year EV leases (most often with Onto or Drive-Electric) and run Uber, Bolt and FreeNow accounts in parallel. They have a solvent motivation: each shift carries a £200 to £400 gross earnings outcome, and the right shift can be £80 net better than the wrong one. They already spend £9 to £15 a month on tools they actually use.

Two UK-specific changes create the buying moment. First, EV-only PHV drivers exploded in 2024 and 2025 because TfL reissued PHV licences to new operators and the cost-of-living squeeze pushed drivers into vehicles they don't own, so Onto, Drive-Electric and Smart Lease tripled their PHV driver counts and the lease-overage penalties at contract end now catch many drivers out. Second, Uber and Bolt both stripped in-app demand detail after an OFSI review on cross-platform payment data in 2024, leaving drivers to combine screenshots from two or three apps each evening, while TfL's cumulative-impact consultation raised the cost of getting PHV licensing wrong.

The passages confirm excess-mileage penalties are a real, recurring cost of leasing, but they also show mileage-tracking is already a solved, free/cheap commodity: Everlance already automates mileage and expense tracking for self-employed workers, and Capterra lists numerous free-to-list driver-management/GPS/mileage-tracking tools, so the specific data-capture piece of the claimed value is not open or undefended.
