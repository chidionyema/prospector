Slatematch — the heritage slater's per-repair source, spec and evidence brief.

A pocket tool that tells a heritage slate roofer which slate to source, how to fix it and what evidence to keep, repair by repair.

Heritage slate roofing is a specialist trade concentrated in the Cotswolds, the Lake District, Snowdonia, Edinburgh and a handful of other places. A sole-trader slate roofer working on a Grade II cottage or a Victorian chapel has three jobs on every repair that take real time and require real expertise.

First, identify the existing slate: its type, its original quarry, its weathering profile, its size and gauge. A Welsh slate from Penrhyn is a different colour, grain and weathering pattern from a Westmorland green or a Ballachulish blue-black; matching the patina of a 150-year-old slate to a new piece requires careful work.

Second, source a matching replacement. The pool of UK heritage quarries still active is small — Burlington in the Lake District, Penrhyn and Ffestiniog in North Wales, Ballachulish in the Highlands, Westmorland in the Lakes, Delabole in Cornwall — and each has its own stock levels, prices, lead times and minimum order rules. Reclaimed slate sits with a different set of salvage yards, each regional.

Third, produce the specification and the evidence. BS 5534:2014 + A2:2018 (the UK slating and tiling code) dictates the fixing method, nail gauge, flashing and mortar specification. Heritage England's Traditional Roofing guidance, updated in 2023, asks the conservation officer to verify the work is repair rather than alteration — increasingly requiring photographic evidence the existing slate was beyond repair before replacement. Building control (Approved Document L and the heritage version of Part B) wants its own sign-off pack. Insurers on heritage properties increasingly want pre-work evidence too.

Slatematch is a phone tool the slate roofer uses at the property. They point the camera at the damaged tile; the tool identifies the slate type against the curated database of UK heritage slate varieties and returns a shortlist of available matching stock across the integrated supplier network, with lead times and prices. They pick the match; the tool generates the BS 5534-compliant repair specification tailored to the local exposure zone. They photograph the existing tile before removal; the tool auto-builds the conservation officer's evidence pack with timestamps and GPS. Finally, it generates a Building Control notification where required and a customer-facing repair quote with the sourced materials and the heritage-grade labour cost.

Why it holds, in two parts. First, the aggregated intelligence: across every roofer on the platform, Slatematch builds a region-by-region map of which slates were installed where and when — data no single rival can accumulate on its own. Second, the supplier network: as the quarries integrate their stock feeds, Slatematch becomes the easiest channel for a heritage roofer to source matching material, and the easiest channel for a quarry to reach its best customers. The roofer's own repair portfolio becomes hard to leave, because losing it means losing years of documented evidence.

BS 5534 was substantially tightened in 2014 and updated in 2018; many heritage slate roofers are still working to older habits. Listed Building Consent enforcement has tightened post-Grenfell. The ECO4 retrofit scheme, running from 2023 to 2026, is driving a wave of insulation work on heritage properties, much of which requires the slate roof to be lifted and relaid — generating a surge in slate-matching demand. The trade is small, concentrated and findable: SPAB courses, the Listed Property Show, Roofing Today magazine, and the handful of UK heritage quarries themselves are natural channel partners. A £30 monthly subscription or a £6 per-pack fee is trivial against the £400 to £2,000 cost of a single slate-matching mistake.
