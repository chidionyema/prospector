SurgeLine — the out-of-hours answering service for tradespeople that also tells them what to charge tonight.

A live, out-of-hours phone service for plumbers, electricians and locksmiths that books the emergency job and recommends the price to quote, based on real call volume from other trades in the same postcode that night.

When a boiler bursts at 11pm, the homeowner calls three or four plumbers off Checkatrade within minutes and books whichever one answers first with a sensible price. The plumber who picks up is guessing what to charge — too high and they lose the job to the next name on the list, too low and they've just worked through the night for less than it was worth. Airlines and hotels solved exactly this problem decades ago by watching how many rooms or seats similar customers were booking at that moment and adjusting price in real time; nobody has brought that trick to a tradesperson's phone.

SurgeLine answers a tradesperson's overflow and out-of-hours calls, the way any answering service does, but every call is logged with postcode, trade, and time. Because SurgeLine's operators also answer for dozens of other plumbers, electricians and locksmiths across the same towns, the system can see, in real time, how many burst-pipe calls have come into that postcode in the last hour, and what price other SurgeLine clients just won or lost a job at. It reads that back to the tradesperson as one line before they call the customer: 'Three other burst-pipe calls in your postcode tonight, last two wins were at £180 and £210 call-out — quote £195.' The tradesperson still makes the call and does the job; SurgeLine just hands them a number a solo operator could never work out alone.

Why it holds: a generic national answering service (Moneypenny, AnswerConnect, and a hundred others) sells the same seat to a dentist, a locksmith and a solicitor, and has no reason to build trade-specific, cross-client, real-time demand data — their business model is seats sold, not vertical intelligence. Building the equivalent means specialising in home-emergency trades and running enough concurrent local clients to see real overlapping demand — exactly what a 2-10 person operator can do across a handful of towns, and exactly what a national call centre serving thousands of unrelated businesses structurally has no reason to bother building for one niche.

Self-employed and small (2-10 person) plumbing, electrical, locksmith and boiler-repair firms who already pay for a generic out-of-hours answering service or a Checkatrade/Rated People lead package. They pay a monthly seat fee plus a small percentage of the value of jobs booked through the recommended price, because it replaces guesswork with a number backed by real, tonight-in-your-postcode demand.

Cost-of-living pressure means homeowners now shop three or four quotes on aggregator apps within minutes for any emergency job, so the trade that answers fastest with the right price wins the job and the rest lose it outright. At the same time, Met Office cold-weather alerts and the well-documented seasonal spike in burst pipes each winter create predictable local demand surges that a single small trade firm has no way to see happening at other firms in real time — until an operator like SurgeLine is aggregating the calls across many of them.
