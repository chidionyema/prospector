PitchSim, rehearse your raise against real UK VC partners.

A rehearsal tool for early-stage UK founders that plays specific VC partners in a mock pitch, using a live-updated library of what each one actually asks.

A founder about to pitch Balderton or LocalGlobe can Google the fund's thesis, but nobody can tell them what that specific partner is likely to grill them on this month. This borrows the way flight simulators and golf-swing analysers work: you rehearse against a realistic, data-driven opponent before the real thing, and you get a score that improves with repetition. The tool builds a live-updated corpus of named UK VC partners, drawn from their podcast appearances (20VC, Seedcamp, EF), recent tweets, LinkedIn posts and portfolio moves, and turns that into a voice roleplay that grills the founder the way that partner actually grills people, with the objections they're currently known for raising. Sessions are recorded and scored, so a founder can watch their answer to a hard traction question get sharper over three rehearsals. It works because pitch prep today means asking ChatGPT to 'pretend to be a VC', which produces generic, dated grilling with no connection to the specific person in the room. It holds because the value is the maintained data layer, not the roleplay mechanic: someone has to keep re-scraping and re-labelling what each partner has said in the last quarter, which is an ongoing data-operations job, not a one-off prompt. A generic assistant has no standing reason to build or maintain that per-partner corpus. Distribution rides the accelerator cohort cycle: Entrepreneur First, Techstars London and Seedcamp all run pitch-day sprints on fixed calendars, so a bulk cohort licence sold once reaches 20-40 founders at a predictable moment of maximum acute pain.

Pre-seed and seed-stage founder-operators in the UK preparing for a raise, paying roughly £29-49 a month for solo access, or accelerator programmes buying a cohort licence to hand every founder a seat in the run-up to demo day.
