PitStop Ledger — the software that tells a mobile car valeter which jobs to take, and proves it to the finance company.

Job software for young self-employed car valeters and mobile detailers that scores every booking on real net pay per hour, and turns a year of that record into proof of income lenders accept.

A 23-year-old running a mobile valeting round drives to jobs, buys water, chemicals and fuel, and gets paid in cash or bank transfer. They have no idea which postcodes, which car types or which repeat customers actually pay after costs. They also cannot get a van on finance, because a lender wants three years of accounts they do not have.

This is job software built for that one trade. You book the job in it, it plans the round, it prices by vehicle size and paint condition, it logs the products used per job, and it takes before-and-after photos on your phone. At the end of every week it shows a plain number: what each job actually earned per working hour after fuel, chemicals and travel time.

The idea borrows the way haulage telematics turned a lorry's driving data into a cheaper insurance premium, and applies it to a one-person valeting round. Every job logged is a verified, timestamped, geolocated, bank-matched transaction. After twelve months, the operator holds a machine-readable trading record no bank statement alone can produce.

That record is the second product. UK van finance and asset lenders — Startline, Close Brothers Motor Finance, and the broker networks that serve them — decline young sole traders constantly for thin files. A standardised, source-verified income export from the software gives an underwriter something to price. The startup earns a fee from the lender when a customer funds a van or equipment through it, on top of the monthly subscription.

Why it holds: the trading history sits inside the product and compounds month after month. A rival tool launching next year starts every customer at zero months of history, so the customer who has built eighteen months of finance-grade record will not switch and lose it. That is a real cost of leaving, not a marketing claim.

The growth motion is product-led and self-serve. Valeters and detailers cluster in visible, high-volume places: the UK Detailing Academy and Auto Finesse training courses, the Waxstock trade show, and very large trade communities on Facebook and TikTok where kit and technique are discussed daily. Free tier for job booking and photos, paid tier at roughly £19 a month for the pay-per-hour analysis and the income export.

Retention comes from the customer list, the photo record and the accounts living in one place. The photos also settle the recurring dispute in this trade — a customer claiming a scratch was caused during the wash — which alone justifies the subscription.
