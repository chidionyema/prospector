SweepBack Bond — The Sole-Trader Drain & Sewer Jetter's Section 59 Misconnection Notice Income Micro-Bond.

A pre-funded income bond a solo drainage jetter buys per contract, paying a fixed daily sum out of a member-funded pot when a water authority or lead local flood authority serves a Section 59 / s.111 misconnection or foul-discharge notice that halts his work mid-job — funded and priced from the operator's own compounding ledger of which authorities serve, how fast, and how long stops last.

INVERT lens: as the Environment Agency and water companies escalate storm-overflow and misconnection enforcement (a trend whose intensity only rises), the enforcing body can publish rules and even a free reporting portal — but it structurally CANNOT indemnify the tradesperson whose income it stops, and CANNOT take the regulated party's commercial side. The value asset therefore GROWS with enforcement aggression and is adversarial to the duty-imposer by construction. Structural form is risk_financing: the operator sells a fixed-price, fixed-payout micro-bond (a defined-benefit indemnity contract, not insurance broking — no risk is placed with a third party; the pot is the members' own subscriptions plus retained float), priced off a proprietary loss table the operator alone accumulates because every claim submission surfaces the authority, the notice type, the halt duration and the resumption trigger. That claims ledger is the compounding wedge: no incumbent sees per-notice halt durations at sole-trader granularity, and it makes the bond cheaper to price each year while any copier prices blind.

UK sole-trader and 1-van drainage/CCTV-survey/jetting contractors (NADC or WJA-ticketed, ~£60-140k turnover, no employees) working domestic and small-commercial foul/surface-water connections — they pay a per-contract premium of £18-45 depending on catchment risk band, plus an annual £120 membership.
