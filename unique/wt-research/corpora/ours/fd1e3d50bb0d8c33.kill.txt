ThresholdPass — the evidence file that finally gets a teacher onto the upper pay scale.

A fixed-fee service that writes a teacher's promotion case against the official Teachers' Standards, so a stalled pay rise gets approved instead of quietly refused.

Since 2014, English schools no longer give teachers automatic pay rises for years served. A headteacher and governors decide each year whether a teacher's evidence meets the Teachers' Standards well enough to move up, and crossing onto the Upper Pay Range is worth several thousand pounds a year, every year, for the rest of their career. Many teachers who are doing the job perfectly well get turned down anyway, not because their teaching is weak, but because their written evidence file is thin, badly mapped to the Standards, or missing the kind of proof a panel actually wants to see.

This borrows the way a professional grant writer earns a fee by turning a researcher's real work into the specific case a funding panel will approve, and applies the same discipline to a teacher's own pay progression file. The service interviews the teacher about their last year's lesson observations, pupil progress data, CPD and extra responsibilities, then builds a structured evidence pack mapped line by line to the Teachers' Standards, in the format governors and heads are used to approving. It is delivered once a year, timed to the appraisal cycle each school runs under the School Teachers' Pay and Conditions Document.

The part that compounds: every case handled adds to a private record of which kinds of evidence actually got a 'yes' from which academy trusts and local authorities, and which got knocked back and why. A national teaching union can only ever hand out one generic template to everyone, because it has to stay neutral across every school in the country. This service gets sharper with every trust it has already won a case against.

Individual classroom teachers in England and Wales, mainly in their first ten years of teaching, paying out of their own salary once a year around appraisal season (typically the summer term) for a personal shot at a permanent pay rise.

Teacher pay has fallen behind inflation for over a decade, so the jump onto the Upper Pay Range or through the main scale threshold matters more to household budgets than ever, and heavier academy trust involvement in pay decisions since 2014 means the panel judging a teacher's case is increasingly a stranger, not a head who has watched them teach for years.
