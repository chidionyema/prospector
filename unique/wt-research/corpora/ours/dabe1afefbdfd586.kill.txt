PIPClaimDesk — the fixed-fee broker who wins your parent's PIP claim and handles every renewal.

The fixed-fee broker who takes the 30-page PIP form off the primary carer's hands, gathers the medical evidence DWP needs, wins the award, and manages every renewal.

Primary carers of elderly parents with dementia, Parkinson's, MS or stroke spend weeks filling in the Personal Independence Payment (PIP) form on their relative's behalf. The PIP2 form runs to around 30 pages of dense questions about daily living and mobility, demanding specific medical evidence from GPs, consultants and carers. Around half of new claims are refused. Mandatory reconsideration overturns roughly 65% of refusals, and tribunal overturns around 75% — the system consistently rejects awards that should be paid.

PIPClaimDesk takes plain-English instructions from the primary carer, drafts the form in DWP language, gathers supporting evidence from the parent's medical team, submits the claim, tracks it with DWP, files a mandatory reconsideration if refused, and prepares the case for tribunal if needed. Each successful application adds to a proprietary dataset of DWP decision patterns by condition, Integrated Care Board area and individual decision maker. That dataset trains the form-drafting workflow, improves success rates, and turns renewals into a recurring subscription line rather than a one-off fee.

The DWP does not offer a concierge service. Condition charities such as Alzheimer's Society, Parkinson's UK and MS Society run free advice lines but do not take over the form filling, do not gather evidence, do not handle mandatory reconsideration, do not prepare or represent at tribunal, and do not manage renewals year after year. Specialist solicitors charge £1,500-£3,000 for tribunal representation, which most primary carers cannot afford on top of the caring burden. PIPClaimDesk sits between these — focused, repeatable, fixed-fee, and learning from every case.

Primary carers of elderly or disabled relatives who need to claim PIP (for over-16s) or DLA (for under-16s) on their relative's behalf. They pay £500-£700 per claim, plus a £150/year subscription for ongoing renewal management. The claimant is the disabled relative, but the buyer and daily user is the primary carer who has to fill in the form.

There are 3.5 million PIP claimants in Great Britain and roughly 500,000 new claims each year, with around half refused first time. The DWP is part-way through digitising the PIP2 form, which opens a window for AI-assisted completion against a known template. The primary carer population is growing as the UK population ages, and the published tribunal success rate of 75% shows the system keeps rejecting awards that should be paid. The product has obvious ROI — winning a PIP claim secures £3,000-£18,000/year for the family, against a one-off £500-£700 fee plus a small annual subscription.
