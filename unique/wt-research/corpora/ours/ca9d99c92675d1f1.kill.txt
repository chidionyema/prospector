RateCraft — The Freelance Creative’s Rate‑Setting Data Brief, powered by real contract data from people just like you.

A weekly data brief for UK freelance creatives that shows anonymised project rates and terms from real gigs, so you set your prices with evidence, not guesswork.

RateCraft collects anonymised project‑rate data directly from freelance creatives across the UK — designers, writers, developers, illustrators — and publishes a paid weekly digest of current market rates segmented by role, experience level and client type. This borrows the way real‑estate price indexes are built from actual transaction data, and applies it to freelance creative contracts, giving individuals the same pricing power that agencies hold.

Contributors submit recent project details through a simple web form and immediately get access to the full dataset themselves. Over time the dataset becomes a proprietary asset that grows more valuable with each new submission: no competitor can quickly recreate that longitudinal history. The weekly brief includes not just rates but how terms like revision rounds, kill fees and payment schedules vary, so the creative walks into every negotiation armed with facts.

Because the data is both fresh and structured, a solo operator can run the entire engine — automated collection, anonymisation and report generation — leaving only light editorial polish. Distribution is owned via email, and the subscription price stays low enough that even price‑sensitive creatives can justify it the moment one client negotiation goes better.

The freelance creative pays directly — a monthly subscription of £9. They have budget authority over tools that demonstrably lift their income, and the payback comes from a single project priced correctly.

The UK freelance workforce has ballooned post‑pandemic, inflation is forcing rate recalibration, and IR35 reforms have pushed more work into genuine freelance arrangements. Public salary data for permanent roles is abundant (Glassdoor, Indeed), but no reliable, real‑time source exists for short‑term, project‑based creative work. Creatives are fed up with guesswork and leaving money on the table.
