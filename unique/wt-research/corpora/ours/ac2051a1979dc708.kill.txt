VisitShield — the lone worker’s dog deterrent and incident logger, built for social care visits.

A portable ultrasonic dog deterrent that also records audio and location, then builds the incident report a social worker needs for their employer.

A council social worker visits homes alone, often encountering aggressive dogs. Standard advice is to stay calm and back away, but that’s not enough. This small device, shaped like a key fob, emits a high‑frequency sound that deters most dogs without injury. At the same time, it starts an encrypted audio recording and stamps the GPS location. After the visit, it syncs with a phone app. The app reconstructs the encounter into a pre‑filled incident report template matching the local authority’s format. It even notes the time the worker pressed the alert button as evidence of the incident timing. The worker just fills in a few details and submits. This is not a generic personal alarm; it’s a workflow tool built for the lone‑worker policy environment.

The solo operator builds the device from a Bluetooth module, an ultrasonic transducer and a simple button. Over many users, the aggregated data reveals patterns of dog‑related incidents by housing type, area and time of day. That data can be sold as a safety insight report to local government insurers or safety departments.

A social worker or family support worker employed by a local authority, who feels their employer’s lone‑worker devices are inadequate and wants their own extra layer of protection. They pay £80–£100 from their own pocket, often motivated after a near‑miss.
