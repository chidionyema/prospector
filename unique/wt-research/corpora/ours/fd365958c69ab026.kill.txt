RespiteSwap — sell unused respite care bookings to other carers.

A platform where a primary carer sells a pre-booked respite session they cannot use to another carer, recouping the cost minus a small fee.

RespiteSwap is a platform that lets a primary carer sell an unused respite booking to another carer, instead of losing the full fee to a cancellation penalty. Carers book respite care—a few hours, a day, a weekend—through a care home, a day centre, or a home care agency. When the cared-for person falls ill or the carer’s own plans change, the booking is often non-refundable or subject to a steep cancellation charge. The platform allows the original booker to list the slot at a discount, find a buyer, and have the booking transferred into the buyer’s name. Payment is held in escrow until the provider confirms the change, then released to the seller minus a 10% platform fee. For the buyer, it offers last-minute respite at a lower cost.

Why it works: The pain is immediate and acute—the carer faces a sunk cost they cannot afford to lose. The payer is solvent because they already paid for the booking and are motivated to recoup cash. The channel is repeatable: carer support groups on Facebook, local authority carer newsletters, and care providers themselves (who reduce customer complaints by offering the platform as an option) distribute the link. Retention is built-in because carers need respite regularly and will return to resell if plans change again. The value grows as care providers tighten cancellation terms—making the loss larger—and the pool of users expands.

Why it holds: A care home or agency cannot offer this service themselves without cannibalising new bookings; their cancellation revenue is pure profit, so they have no incentive to help carers resell. A generic classifieds site like Gumtree cannot guarantee the transfer of a personalised care booking or handle the payment securely. By standardising the resale process and holding the funds, the platform earns trust and takes a cut. The more people use it, the faster sellers find buyers, creating a network effect that a latecomer cannot easily replicate.

The seller pays a 10% success fee on the resale price; the buyer pays the agreed discounted amount.

Care providers in the UK are raising cancellation fees and shortening notice periods, leaving carers out of pocket. The cost of living crisis forces carers to reclaim every pound, and last-minute respite is in high demand as waiting lists grow.
