CrateStart — breakfast-club food crates sized for one school, not one warehouse.

A subscription crate of shelf-stable, allergen-labelled breakfast food, priced and packed for the single staff member now running England's new free breakfast club on almost no budget.

Since April 2025, hundreds of English primary schools have had to open a free breakfast club under the government's early adopter scheme, with universal rollout planned for 2026. The grant covers a small amount for kit, but almost nothing ongoing for food, so the job of feeding thirty extra children each morning usually lands on one office manager, pastoral lead or teaching assistant with a spreadsheet and a petty-cash card.

That person cannot place a pallet order with a big wholesaler: the volumes are too small, the delivery bay too shallow, and nobody has checked every product against the School Food Standards (England) Regulations 2014 or logged every allergen. Cash-and-carry chains are built for kitchens ordering hundreds of covers, not for one person ordering thirty portions of something safe and cheap.

CrateStart borrows the model of the everyday-low-margin surplus-food resellers (Company Shop Group's short-dated stock, manufacturer overrun lines) and fuses it with the subscription-box format: a own-branded, pre-sorted crate of shelf-stable breakfast items, each one pre-checked against the 2014 Standards and clearly allergen-labelled, sized to the actual headcount the school reports each half-term. The crate ships direct to the school, addressed to the named breakfast-club lead, invoiced against the DfE grant code so it survives an audit.

The durable edge is the buying relationship: consistent access to manufacturer short-dated and overrun stock at volumes small enough for one school, something the big wholesalers structurally won't sort and re-pack for. A small team can hold that relationship and do the compliance checking once per product line, then resell it thousands of times over.

The staff member given responsibility for their school's breakfast club — usually a school business manager, office manager or pastoral TA — paying from the DfE breakfast-club grant, pupil premium underspend, or the school's own petty-cash budget line.
