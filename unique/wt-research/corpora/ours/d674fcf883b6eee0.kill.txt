SplitShift — The Carer's Job-Share Pairing & Placement Desk.

A hand-brokered desk that pairs two primary carers with mirrored availability into one pre-packaged job-share 'unit', then places that unit into a single full-time professional vacancy — charging each half a success fee only when the pair is hired.

Lens: broaden. Instead of narrowly helping one carer find a part-time job (the losing shape: another flexible-jobs board), broaden across the whole employment value chain: the real transaction is TWO carers + ONE employer. Primary carers are locked out of well-paid professional roles because employers won't split a full-time seat and carers can't credibly propose a split alone. The broker manufactures the missing counterparty: it vets and pairs two carers whose caring schedules interlock (e.g., one covers Mon–Wed with Thursday hospital-clinic blackouts, the other covers Wed–Fri), drafts the joint application, the handover protocol, and an employer-ready job-share agreement, and pitches the pair as a single de-risked hire into ordinary full-time vacancies. Each successful placement compounds a proprietary dataset of which availability geometries, seniority gaps, and handover rituals make job-share pairs survive past 12 months — data no job board or HR platform collects because none of them ever sees the pair formed, only individual applicants.

UK-based primary carers with professional backgrounds (nursing, accounting, marketing, project management) seeking 2.5–3 day weeks at professional salaries — specifically carers of young children or elderly/disabled relatives who left roles paying £35k–£70k FTE. Each half of a placed pair pays a £349 pairing-and-pack fee plus a £600 success fee on hire (funded out of the first month's salary), ~£1,900 per placed pair.
