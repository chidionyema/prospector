Exception Engine — turns a junior doctor’s rota into a ready-to-submit claim for missed breaks and overtime.

A web app that transforms a junior doctor’s rota into an exception report, catching missed breaks and overtime they’d otherwise lose.

Junior doctors in England on the 2016 contract are routinely underpaid because the official exception-reporting process is time-consuming and confusing. The BMA estimates that over 60% of junior doctors do not regularly claim back missed breaks or overtime, leaving an average of £3.14 per hour unclaimed. Exception Engine is a niche SaaS where a doctor uploads their rota—as a spreadsheet, screenshot or even a photo—and the tool applies the granular rules of the 2016 contract. It automatically flags shifts that breached rest, break or hour limits, and generates a pre-filled exception report in the exact format each NHS trust expects. The engine also tracks the status of submitted reports and sends reminders. This combines the acute, under-addressed pain of junior doctors losing thousands in unpaid overtime with the capabilities of modern document automation, which can now parse complex contractual rules and generate the exact paperwork required. Over time, each doctor builds a private, portable exception history that becomes a personal record of trust compliance, creating strong switching costs. Anonymised, aggregated data on exception patterns across trusts could also become a proprietary asset for pay negotiations or research.

Junior doctors themselves, paying a small monthly subscription (e.g. £3.99/month) or a per-report fee. They are highly motivated because a single successful exception report typically reclaims £50–£200, so the tool pays for itself instantly.

The BMA’s 2023–24 junior doctor strikes and the ongoing workforce crisis have spotlighted the hidden free labour built into the contract. Trusts have little incentive to make exception reporting easier because it costs them money. Meanwhile, no-code and AI tools have made it trivially cheap for a solo developer to build a rules engine that reads rota patterns. The rise of rotational training and F3/locum life means doctors frequently move between trusts, making a portable, personal tool far more valuable than any single trust’s clunky portal.
