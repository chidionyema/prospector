Council Tax exemption claims for dementia carers.

A solo operator files the doctor's certificate and backdated Council Tax exemption for primary carers whose parent has dementia — fees only on recovery.

Under regulation 4 of the Council Tax (General Provisions) Regulations 1992, anyone with dementia, Alzheimer's, or another severe mental impairment is legally disregarded for Council Tax. If they live alone, or only with their carer, the household pays nothing, and the exemption is backdated up to six years in many councils. The trigger requires the parent's doctor to sign form CT205 confirming severe mental impairment — a step most GPs never volunteer, and most primary carers of parents with dementia don't know to ask for. The carer keeps paying £1,200 to £2,500 a year in Council Tax for years after diagnosis.

ForgetMeRefund is a fixed-fee productised service where one operator takes on the whole claim. For £299 plus 25 percent of the first-year recovery (capped at £1,500), the freelancer confirms the parent qualifies (diagnosis plus a qualifying benefit such as Attendance Allowance, PIP daily living, or DLA care), books and prepares the GP appointment, files the CT205 and the council exemption form, claims the backdated Council Tax, and chases any refusal. Once set up, the same freelancer monitors annual Council Tax bills and renews the exemption if circumstances change.

The durable wedge is the proprietary dataset the freelancer builds as the cases stack up. Each engagement adds three data points the next operator would have to discover from scratch: how fast that GP surgery returns the CT205, which form version that council accepts, and the longest backdating period that council has actually paid. By the 50th case the operator files in days rather than weeks and quotes the maximum recoverable figure up front — neither a benefits adviser nor a new entrant can match that speed without rebuilding the database from zero.

Why this is not a commodity wrap: the Alzheimer's Society welfare line gives information but won't file the CT205 on the carer's behalf. The council's own welfare rights team has been cut in most local authorities since 2010. Citizens Advice has multi-month waiting lists. The freelancer owns the end-to-end execution.
