TerpSheet — The Independent UK Distiller's Botanical Batch-to-Cut Yield & Flavour-Drift Bench Tool.

A vertical bench tool for the ~700 owner-operated UK gin/rum distilleries that records every still run's botanical lot, maceration curve and cut points against sensory outcome, so the one person who owns the palate can reproduce a winning batch and defend it when their botanical supplier silently changes origin.

Lens: analogical — transplant the wine industry's vintage/terroir provenance-sheet model (where lot-level provenance is the product and the record is the asset) onto craft spirits, where nobody keeps it. Specific daily friction: a solo distiller runs 2-4 stills a week, writes cut temperatures and botanical weights on a clipboard or an unversioned spreadsheet, and when batch 47 tastes wrong versus batch 31 they have no way to isolate whether it was the juniper lot (Macedonian vs Albanian), a 40-minute-longer maceration, or a heads cut taken 0.4L early. Their botanical wholesaler ships 'juniper berries' with no origin or harvest-year on the invoice, and origin swaps mid-contract. First-principles rebuild: the unit of record is not 'a batch' but 'a lot-to-cut-to-sensory triple'. TerpSheet is a phone-first bench app where the distiller scans/keys the supplier lot code at maceration, logs the cut by hydrometer reading and time (two taps), and then — critically — logs a structured 12-descriptor sensory score at 2 weeks and 8 weeks post-rest. Over 30-40 runs the tool builds that distillery's OWN drift map: 'your Albanian juniper lots run 18% lower on pine at the same cut; your last three off-spec batches all trace to supplier lot prefix K7'. That map is worth money at three moments the distiller feels acutely: (1) reproducing a batch that won a Great Taste / IWSC medal when the medal is the entire sales pitch; (2) scaling a contract-bottling run for a farm shop or supermarket local range where consistency is contractual; (3) arguing with the botanical supplier for credit or a lot swap with evidence rather than vibes. Solo-operable: a single-tenant web app, no field ops, no inventory, no liquid ever touched. It is NOT a compliance/duty-return tool (HMRC's own W-1/spirits return is free and first-party) and NOT a generic brewery/distillery ERP — it deliberately refuses to touch stock, excise or invoicing, which is why it fits on a wet bench in 90 seconds a run.

Owner-distillers of single-site UK craft distilleries running 1-3 pot stills with 0-4 staff — specifically the ~700 HMRC-registered small distilleries producing gin, rum or new-make whisky under 50,000 LPA/year, where the founder personally makes the cuts. £39/month, plus a £250 one-off 'Medal Batch Reconstruction' service where the operator reverse-engineers a reproducible spec from a customer's historic paper notes.

UK distillery numbers roughly quadrupled over the last decade and the 2023 alcohol-duty reform plus 2024-25 input-cost inflation ended the 'launch and sell out' era — survivors now compete on consistency and awards rather than novelty, and are taking contract-bottling and own-label work that carries written consistency specs. Simultaneously, botanical supply chains (juniper especially) have been disrupted by Balkan harvest volatility and phytophthora dieback in wild juniper stands, so origin substitution mid-contract is now routine and unannounced. Cheap phone-based structured sensory logging plus LLM-assisted descriptor normalisation makes a two-tap bench record viable for the first time; previously this needed a QA technician nobody employs.
