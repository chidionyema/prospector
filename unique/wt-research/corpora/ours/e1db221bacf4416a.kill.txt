Refund clawback broker for Shopify and Amazon sellers.

A commission-only desk that recovers overcharged fees, lost inventory and wrongly-granted refunds from Amazon and Shopify, and only gets paid from money that actually lands.

An online-store operator running £1m–£15m a year on Amazon UK and Shopify loses money in three places at once, and none of it shows up as a line they can chase. Amazon loses or damages stock in Fenchurch and Dunfermline fulfilment centres and does not always reimburse it. Amazon charges the wrong fee band when a carton is measured badly, so a product is billed as 'large' when it is 'standard'. And card refunds go through under the Payment Services Regulations 2017 chargeback route, where the seller quietly eats the money because nobody filed the evidence in time.

This business is a broker in the plain sense: the seller signs a limited authority, the desk works all three recovery routes on their behalf, and takes 20% to 25% of cash actually recovered. Nothing recovered, nothing charged. That is the whole commercial model, and it is what makes the first call easy.

What makes it work rather than just another reimbursement tool is fusing two signals nobody joins up. Amazon changed its FBA reimbursement policy so that from March 2025 lost-inventory claims are paid on Amazon's own estimated manufacturing cost, not the retail price, and it shortened the claim windows. At the same time UK card scheme chargeback volumes rose with the cost-of-living squeeze. Both are deadlines with money attached. A seller misses them because their operations manager is busy shipping, not diarising.

The desk runs on a queue of trigger dates pulled from each client's settlement reports, inventory adjustment reports, and their payment provider's dispute feed. Every client's outcome teaches the desk which claim wording, which carton dimension dispute, and which chargeback evidence bundle actually gets paid, per category and per marketplace. That win-rate history, built across dozens of clients, is the thing a single seller cannot build for themselves — they see their own claims only, roughly forty a year. The desk sees thousands.

A team of three to six people runs this. One does client onboarding and permissions, two work the claim queues with model-drafted evidence bundles, one handles disputes that escalate. Model work handles the drafting and the matching of shipment records to adjustments; humans handle anything a marketplace pushes back on.

This is a sells-to-tech-adjacent play in the AI-native shape: a model does the reconciliation and evidence-drafting work an outsourced claims clerk is paid for today, and the recurring trigger is Amazon's own claim deadline clock.

UK online-store operators doing roughly £1m to £15m a year across Amazon UK and Shopify, usually a founder or an operations manager with three to twenty staff. They pay nothing up front and a fifth of recovered cash. Typical recovery for a £5m seller runs into tens of thousands of pounds a year.
