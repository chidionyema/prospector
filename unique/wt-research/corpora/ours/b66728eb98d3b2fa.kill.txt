Monthly retainer decks built for agencies.

Each month, per active retainer client, a polished status deck with this month's metrics, wins, and one next-step recommendation, delivered for a fixed fee.

Agency owners spend $80,000-$120,000 of fully-loaded senior time every year — an Account Manager or Project Manager, BLS occupation code 13-1082 Project Management Specialists at a median $98,580, or 11-2011 Advertising and Promotions Managers at $108,820 (BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2023) — assembling the same monthly status deck for each retainer client: this month's work, key metrics, wins, blockers, one next-step recommendation, on slightly different inputs each cycle. For a fixed monthly fee per active retainer client (typically $300 to $600), this productized service connects to the agency's project, time-tracking, and ad-platform accounts, has a model assemble a two-page PDF and a five-slide narrative deck, and has a senior account lead sign off. The agency recovers 15 to 25 hours per client per month of senior time. The budget sits with the agency owner without procurement review.

What makes this workable now: every input is already machine-readable in tools the agency already pays for (Asana, ClickUp, Harvest, Meta Business Suite, Google Ads), and the deliverable structure is templated. The senior PM or AM currently spends 25 to 30 hours a week across roughly a dozen clients on this single ritual.

Why the value holds: each monthly cycle adds the agency's prior cadence, voice, and sign-off workflow into the service, and that accumulated corpus becomes the agency's working asset. The recurring trigger is the monthly retainer cycle — the most entrenched client-services friction in any small agency's operating model. The deliverable is the produced artifact, not a dashboard login.

The strongest incumbent is AgencyAnalytics, with Whatagraph, Klipfolio, and DashThis as direct peers. They sell dashboard subscriptions (SaaS seats) and structurally cannot ship the finished deck without cannibalizing their own seat revenue model, which depends on the agency's team logging in to view the dashboard themselves. Their customers also built internal teams around the dashboard, so converting to a produced-deliverable service would directly trigger churn against their own investor base. The cross-tool data ingestion across Asana, ClickUp, Harvest, Meta Business Suite, and Google Ads is the technical footprint almost no small agency has built in-house, and the typical agency owner cannot justify hiring a full-time report engineer for it.

Reframed from first principles: the agency treats monthly client reports as a labor cost to be endured, but the structure is so templated that it is functionally a fill-in form. There is no real justification for human production beyond the fact that the underlying data has not been connected — which is a one-time data-engineering problem, not a recurring human task.

Wedge: switching_cost. Each completed month raises the cost of leaving, because the agency's prior cycle's reports, voice, and sign-off workflow all live in the system.
