SchoolPlace Swap, buys your child's place at a full school.

We buy the reserved school place a family is giving up, then sell it to a family stuck on the waiting list, handling the timing so neither side loses a deposit.

Here is the plain version. Every year thousands of UK families accept an independent school place, pay a deposit, and then their circumstances change. A job moves. A grandparent needs care. The deposit is gone and the school quietly refills the place from its waiting list. Meanwhile another family has been waiting eighteen months for that exact year group, and finds out too late.

We stand between those two families as the buyer and the seller, not as a listings board. When a family wants out, we make them a cash offer for their notice position and their deposit claim, on the spot. We then place a waiting-list family into that slot and charge them a placement fee for skipping the queue. We take the risk in the middle. That is what makes us a dealer rather than a noticeboard, and it is why we can pay the leaving family in days rather than after a match.

The reason this holds is the notice clause itself. Almost every UK independent school contract requires a full term's written notice, and the standard terms most schools use come from the Independent Schools' Bursars Association template. Miss the date and you owe a term's fees, often £5,000 to £15,000. That date is knowable, it is fixed, and it repeats every year. We learn, school by school, which bursars will release a family early when a replacement is ready on the same day, and which will not. No one else is collecting that.

That record compounds. After a few hundred deals we know which schools in which year groups have real queues, which have empty desks they will not admit to, and which bursars answer in 48 hours. That tells us what to pay a leaving family and what to charge an arriving one. A school cannot build this because it only sees its own roll. A comparison site cannot build it because no school will publish that its Year 7 is half empty.

The wit of it is that our value grows as schools get stricter about notice. Fee rises after VAT was added to independent school fees in January 2025 have pushed more families to leave mid-year, and bursars have tightened enforcement of the notice clause in response. The harder a school enforces, the more a leaving family will pay to escape, and the more a waiting family will pay to arrive.

Two named payers. First, parents aged 35 to 55 who have accepted a place at a UK independent day school and need to withdraw outside the notice window; they sell us their exit for cash and avoid a five-figure bill. Second, parents on the waiting list at an oversubscribed day school in the same catchment, who pay a placement fee of £1,500 to £3,000 to take the released slot. Both are people with property and pensions but tight monthly cash.

VAT at 20% was applied to independent school fees from 1 January 2025, and the Independent Schools Council census published in May 2025 recorded the largest single-year fall in pupil numbers it has measured. More families are leaving mid-year, and more schools are enforcing the term's-notice clause to protect income. That collision creates a real, repeating supply of unwanted places and a real queue of families who want them.
