DB Transfer FOS Atlas – the retiree's map to winning a defined-benefit pension transfer complaint, built from real Ombudsman decisions.

A searchable data intelligence platform that uses Financial Ombudsman Service decision data to show you how to win a mis-selling claim for a pension transfer.

Thousands of retirees who were advised to transfer out of their final-salary pension now face financial loss, and the FCA's crackdown on unsuitable advice is causing a surge of complaints to the Financial Ombudsman Service (FOS). The Atlas aggregates every published FOS final decision on defined-benefit transfer suitability, classifies the arguments that won, the size of redress, and the common lender or advisory firm patterns. A retiree who suspects they were poorly advised can search by their adviser's name or the specific type of suitability failing, and the Atlas instantly returns a pre-populated complaint letter template with the most effective arguments, backed by similar successful cases. It also estimates their likely redress based on the published awards. This is not a compliance tool but an adversarial intelligence tool: as the FCA imposes tougher requirements under the Consumer Duty and the FOS publishes more decisions, the Atlas grows in resolution and value. The FOS itself cannot offer this because it must remain impartial and cannot coach complainants, and the FCA does not provide individualised case law search. The wedge is proprietary_data: the tagging, grouping and redress modelling of FOS decisions is a unique dataset that cannot be scraped from the FOS website without significant processing, and each new decision entrenches the moat. A solo operator can maintain it with automated data pipelines and sell monthly subscriptions (£15/month) to worried retirees or one-off search access.

A retiree aged 60-75 who transferred out of a defined-benefit pension in the last decade and saw their pension value fall, or their financially savvy adult child investigating the transfer. They pay a £15 monthly subscription or £40 for a single-case access.

The FCA's Consumer Duty, which came into force in July 2023, and the ongoing British Steel Pension Scheme review have led to a surge in DB transfer complaints. The FOS received over 10,000 new complaints about pensions in the last year, many from retirees, and the volume of published decisions is now large enough to build a meaningful dataset.
