For a flat $99, we send the demand letter, file the small claims case, and even stand with you in court—so you never lose a day's pay fighting your landlord.

Many Gen Z renters in California lose their security deposits when they move out. Landlords often claim damages that are normal wear and tear, or fail to provide a proper itemized statement within 21 days. California law is strong for tenants: Civil Code § 1950.5 requires the landlord to justify deductions, and bad-faith withholding can lead to double damages. But most renters don't have the time, knowledge, or nerve to fight back. They accept the loss because the alternative—hiring a lawyer—is too expensive. DepositRescue turns this into a flat-fee service. For $99, we collect the tenant's evidence, write a formal demand letter citing the law, and negotiate with the landlord. If the landlord doesn't pay, we prepare and file a small claims case for an additional $149, which includes having a trained representative appear in court with the tenant (California allows lay representation in small claims with the tenant's consent). We only take on cases where the deposit is at least $500, so the economics work for the tenant: they stand to recover $500 or more for a $99 investment. The process is templated, and a solo operator can handle dozens of cases a month. Marketing is straightforward: partner with moving companies, post on TikTok and Instagram showing deposit rescues, and offer a referral program. The demand is real: a casual search of tenant forums shows hundreds of renters asking what to do about withheld deposits. Free alternatives exist—legal aid and tenant unions—but they are overwhelmed and often have income limits. Paid services like ours offer immediate action and a personal touch. This is not a regulated legal service because we act as a non-lawyer assistant or a 'legal document preparer' under California law, which requires registration and bond in some counties, easily obtained by a small business.
