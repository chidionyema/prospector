The Late-Onset Atlas — The Retiree's Per-Trial Clinical-Study Eligibility & Reimbursed-Travel Decision Brief.

A data intelligence product that reconstructs, from public trial registries and site-level protocol documents, which UK/EU clinical trials a 60-75-year-old with a specific comorbidity stack would actually be admitted to — and which of those sites pay travel, accommodation and companion costs — sold as a personalised per-condition decision brief.

Ignore how this is solved today (a retiree is told 'ask your consultant' or handed the ISRCTN/ClinicalTrials.gov search box, which lists trials but hides the two facts that decide everything). Raw pain rebuilt from zero: a newly-retired person with, say, early Parkinson's plus atrial fibrillation plus metformin, wants access to a drug five years before NICE approves it. The registry entry says 'Recruiting'. It does not say that this site closed recruitment nine weeks ago, that Exclusion Criterion 14 silently disqualifies anyone on a DOAC, that the run-in requires eleven visits over four months, or that Site 7 in Leeds reimburses mileage and a companion's hotel while Site 3 does not. Those facts live in protocol PDFs, PIS/ICF documents, ethics-committee summaries, per-site amendment logs and monthly recruitment-status deltas — scattered, unstructured, and never joined. The asset is a longitudinal scrape: snapshot every UK/EU registry record and its attached participant-information documents weekly, diff them, and accumulate a structured table of (site × exclusion criterion × visit burden × reimbursement policy × observed recruitment-velocity × observed closure). Recruitment velocity and silent-closure detection are only derivable from a time series nobody else is keeping — the registries overwrite, they do not version. Sold as a £180 personalised brief: 'Given your seven conditions, six medications, postcode and mobility, here are the 4 trials that will not reject you at screening, ranked by expected screening-pass odds, total travel burden in hours, and cash reimbursement available; here are the 23 that look eligible on the registry and are not.' Renewal is a £15/month watch that fires when a new arm opens within travel range or an exclusion criterion is amended in the buyer's favour.

UK adults aged 60-75 with a recently-diagnosed progressive condition (Parkinson's, early Alzheimer's, wet AMD, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, myeloma) who have pension assets, spare time, and a strong preference for early access over waiting — plus their adult children, who frequently buy the brief on the parent's behalf. Secondary payer: retirees in the healthy-volunteer aged cohort who treat reimbursed trials as income.

Three converging changes: (1) the UK's 2024-2026 clinical-trials regulation overhaul and the MHRA/HRA push for combined review has made per-trial documentation more machine-readable and mandated public plain-language summaries, meaning protocol-level exclusion detail is newly extractable at scale; (2) post-pandemic decentralised and hybrid trial designs have exploded the variance in per-site visit burden and reimbursement generosity, so the site you pick now materially changes cost and feasibility; (3) sponsors under recruitment pressure in an ageing-population trial boom increasingly fund participant travel and companion costs, but advertise it inconsistently and only inside site-level documents — creating a real, cash-valued information asymmetry that did not exist five years ago.
