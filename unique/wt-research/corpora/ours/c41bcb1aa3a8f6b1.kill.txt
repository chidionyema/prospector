QuietRig, silent ex-datacentre GPUs for home AI.

QuietRig sells silent, tested ex-datacentre GPUs to UK developers running local AI, with power presets timed to cheap electricity hours.

UK indie developers running local large language models on their own machines — using tools like Ollama, llama.cpp or vLLM instead of paying for cloud GPU time — are buying secondhand enterprise cards such as the Nvidia P40, A4000 or V100 straight off eBay or Alibaba. These cards were built for rack-mounted servers with forced airflow, so raw they are loud, run hot, and often draw more power than a home office breaker or a flat's electricity bill can comfortably absorb.

QuietRig is a UK-based ecommerce brand that buys these decommissioned cards in bulk under the WEEE Regulations 2013 (which govern the legal resale of electronic equipment retired from UK and EU datacentres), bin-tests every unit under sustained load, 3D-prints and fits a custom quiet-acoustic shroud, and flashes a power-limit firmware profile before selling the finished card direct through its own Shopify store and Amazon UK listing.

The real trick is the firmware presets: each card ships with named profiles tuned to specific UK time-of-use tariffs — for example an 'Octopus Agile' profile that throttles the card to near-idle during expensive peak hours and unlocks full power only in the cheap overnight window Octopus publishes each day. A developer plugs it in, picks their tariff from a dropdown in the bundled utility, and the card behaves accordingly with no manual scheduling.

This borrows the way solar-battery brands sell hardware pre-configured to a household's specific energy tariff, and applies the same idea to secondhand AI compute — fusing the local-LLM hardware boom with the UK's own time-of-use electricity market, something no generic GPU reseller bothers to touch.

Each unit sells for a fixed margin over acquisition and refurbishment cost, with a 90-day warranty that a marketplace listing never offers, and repeat sales come from developers upgrading to bigger cards as their local models grow.

UK indie software developers and small AI/ML hobbyist-engineers who want to run local models for side projects or small SaaS products without a recurring cloud GPU bill, and who care about noise and electricity cost because they work from a home office or a flat.
