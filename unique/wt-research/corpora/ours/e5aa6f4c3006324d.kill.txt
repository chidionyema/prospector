Deprecation Drift, tells you which model update broke you.

A weekly readout for AI product teams showing which of your prompts a model vendor's silent update has quietly changed the answer to, before your customers notice.

This is an AI-native product: a model does the job a QA engineer or applied-AI engineer is paid to do today, which is re-running your prompts after every vendor change and reading the diffs.

Here is the plain version. You ship a feature built on a hosted model. The vendor updates that model, or silently reweights it, and your carefully tuned prompt starts producing a different shape of answer. Nothing errors. Your tests pass. Your support inbox fills up three weeks later. This product runs your own prompt set against every version and every provider, every day, and tells you exactly which prompts moved and by how much.

The angle is to build the thing that gets more valuable the harder the duty-imposer pushes. The UK's new rules put the burden on the deployer, not the model vendor. Under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, and under the ICO's guidance on automated decision-making, a UK company using a third-party model still owns the outcome its customers get. The vendor can publish a model card. The vendor structurally cannot tell you what its update did to YOUR prompts, because it has never seen them, and it has a commercial interest in describing its own change as an improvement. That gap is the business, and it widens every time a regulator asks a UK software company to evidence that its automated output stayed consistent.

The compounding asset is the prompt-behaviour history. Every customer who connects a prompt set adds runs to a corpus of how specific real production prompts behaved across specific model versions and dates. That corpus cannot be scraped, cannot be bought, and cannot be reconstructed after the fact, because old model versions get retired. Within a year you can answer a question nobody else can: 'when this vendor shipped that version, what class of prompt broke, and how badly?' That is the readout AI teams pay for, and it is also the evidence pack their enterprise buyers and their ICO-facing paperwork need.

Growth is product-led. A free tier watches five prompts against public models and emails a weekly drift note. That note is inherently shareable inside engineering teams, and it arrives on the day a vendor ships a change, which is exactly when the team is arguing about whether anything moved. Paid tiers price by prompt volume and by retained history. Retention is structural: the value of your account is the length of your own behaviour history, so leaving resets it to zero.
