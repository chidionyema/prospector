Placement fee recovery for UK digital agencies.

Fixed-fee recovery of the placement fee your agency is owed when a client hires a freelancer you placed there, using Companies House filings to prove the breach.

The UK digital agency places a subcontractor (typically a freelance AI engineer, designer, or developer) at a client site under a back-to-back agreement. The agency contract almost always carries a placement-fee clause: if the client hires the subcontractor directly within 12 months, the client pays the agency 15-25% of the subcontractor's first-year salary. In practice, the client hires the subcontractor quietly and never tells the agency. The agency loses £5,000-£25,000 per placement and has no easy way to know.

This productised service, run at a fixed monthly monitoring fee plus a contingency percentage on recovered amounts, watches every placement the agency has registered. It cross-references Companies House director-appointment filings (public under the Companies Act 2006), the client's published job pages, and LinkedIn job-change signals against the agency's placement list. When a breach is detected, the service drafts a breach letter on the agency's letterhead that cites the exact contract clause and demand, sends it to the client's accounts payable, and escalates to a Money Claims Online filing at HM Courts if the client does not respond within 30 days.

What makes this work is the continuous monitoring combined with contract-specific drafting. The agency does not currently watch for this kind of breach because each subcontractor sits at a different client, the signals are scattered across Companies House, careers pages, and LinkedIn, and the agency has no time. A service that watches all of them in one place, against one public source, gets paid faster than the agency could alone.

Companies House is the public source that makes this viable. Every UK director appointment is filed at Companies House within 14 days and is queryable through the Companies House API and the PSC (Persons with Significant Control) register. A subcontractor who becomes a director or shareholder of the client company within 12 months of placement is a provable breach of the placement-fee clause.

This is not a debt collector or a solicitor. Debt collectors are reactive; you must hand them the debt and the contract. Solicitors charge by the hour. This product is proactive, fixed-fee, and uses a publicly verifiable trigger that the agency does not have the time to watch themselves.
