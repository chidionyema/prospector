MFT Bridge — The Electrician's Stolen Test-Kit Same-Week Replacement Advance.

Contingent replacement financing for electricians whose calibrated multifunction testers are stolen from vans: kit ships next day from a pre-negotiated distributor credit line, repaid by assignment of the insurance claim proceeds, with the operator taking a fixed success fee only when the claim pays.

Lens: narrow. A UK electrician cannot legally sign off 18th-edition work without a calibrated multifunction tester (MFT), so a van break-in doesn't just cost the ~£800-1,500 kit — it stops ALL certifiable work until insurance settles, typically 3-8 weeks, costing £2,000+ per idle week. Insurers settle in cash after loss adjustment and structurally will not fund replacement before liability is confirmed. MFT Bridge is a risk-financing sidecar: the tradesperson registers their kit (serials, calibration certs, van security photos) for a small annual fee; on theft, the operator triggers a pre-agreed trade-credit facility with a test-equipment distributor to ship a replacement MFT within 24-48 hours, takes a legal assignment of the relevant slice of the insurance claim, packages the evidence dossier that makes the claim fast and undeniable (the pre-registered serials and security photos are exactly what loss adjusters stall on), and collects a fixed £150-250 success fee from claim proceeds. The pre-registration data is the underwriting: only registered, evidenced kit gets advanced, which keeps fraud and default risk low enough for a solo to carry via the distributor's credit terms rather than their own capital. The moat is the captive distributor credit relationship plus the pre-loss registration book — an insurer structurally cannot advance kit before adjusting the loss, and a distributor won't extend claim-assignment credit to unvetted strangers, only through the party holding the pre-loss evidence.

Self-employed UK electricians and small electrical contractors (1-5 vans) who carry £1,000+ of calibrated test instruments and lose all certifiable income when it's stolen — they pay a ~£49/yr registration fee plus a fixed success fee deducted from the insurance settlement per incident.

UK van tool theft remains at epidemic levels with a break-in reported roughly every 15-20 minutes and tool insurance premiums and excesses rising sharply post-2023, while insurers have lengthened settlement times through outsourced loss adjusting; simultaneously BS 7671 amendment enforcement and competent-person scheme audits make working without a calibrated MFT a career-ending shortcut, so the downtime cost of theft has never been higher relative to the kit's cash value.
