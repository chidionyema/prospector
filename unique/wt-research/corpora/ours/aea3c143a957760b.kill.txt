Prospect AI churn score for agencies.

Prospect-level score that flags which new business will quietly replace you with AI before the pitch goes out.

A US marketing, design, or dev agency owner pitches 10-15 new prospects a year and loses 3-4 mid-pitch to 'we are bringing this in-house with AI.' The pitch hours vanish and the agency never knew which prospects were AI-replacement risks from the start. This vertical tool, sold to agency owners in California, New York, and Texas, watches the public signals that predict a prospect will quietly buy a ChatGPT Enterprise seat, hire a Head of AI, or stand up a Midjourney workflow that does what the agency does. The signals are all public and free at point of use: the US Department of Labor WARN Act notice database (dol.gov/agencies/eta/layoffs/warn) and California EDD's WARN feed (edd.ca.gov/en/jobs_and_training/Layoff_Services_WARN) flag layoff rounds that often precede an AI-first pivot. LinkedIn public job posts reveal when a prospect hires its first Head of AI or prompt engineer. BuiltWith's free tier (builtwith.com) and Wappalyzer-style detection show when a prospect adds Jasper, Midjourney, Copy.ai, or ChatGPT Enterprise to its marketing stack. State legislative calendars on LegiScan (legiscan.com) flag when a prospect's state is about to pass an AI disclosure law that creates a 'we need to do this fast' panic. The tool's deliverable is a 1-100 AI churn risk score per prospect, surfaced inside the agency's HubSpot or Pipedrive record via a sidebar widget, plus a monthly email that maps the next 90 days of AI policy events against the agency's active client roster. The buyer role is owner or new-business lead; the recurring trigger is each new prospect added to the agency's CRM. Agency owner uses the score to walk away from prospects above 70, price 20-40% higher on prospects between 40 and 70, and pitch AI-augmented (not AI-replacement) positioning on the rest. Pricing clears $200-500 per month per agency from existing tooling budget; 200 paying agencies across the three named states clears $700K-$1.5M ARR with one founder and a part-time data engineer. The asset that gets harder to copy as more agencies sign up is the cross-agency benchmark (median AI churn rate by agency vertical, by prospect industry, by deal size). Horizontal incumbents Clay, Apollo, and ZoomInfo enrich on AI adoption as one column among fifty and offer no policy-event forecast or industry-specific scoring; they cannot rebuild the benchmark without onboarding a comparable vertical customer base from zero.
