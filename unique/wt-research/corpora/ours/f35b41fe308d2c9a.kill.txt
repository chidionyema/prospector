EU VAT and customs filing for UK Shopify sellers.

A parcel-by-parcel broker that lets UK Shopify and eBay sellers ship to EU customers with VAT, customs and packaging fees all handled in one go.

UK Shopify and eBay sellers lost their frictionless EU sales channel after Brexit. Any parcel under €150 going to an EU consumer must carry VAT registered at the point of sale under the EU's IOSS scheme, plus a customs declaration, plus (in many states) EPR packaging fees. When a UK seller tries to ship without that stack, the EU customer sees a surprise VAT plus courier handling fee on delivery and abandons the basket, or the parcel is seized at the border. Most UK small sellers just switch off EU shipping, which is why HMRC's Overseas Trade in Goods Statistics still show UK parcel exports to the EU running roughly 30% below 2019 levels even five years on.

The broker sits between the UK seller and the EU's tax and customs authorities. For each parcel going out, it files the CN22 declaration, classifies the goods to an HS code, charges EU VAT at checkout on the seller's behalf, and remits monthly to each EU member state's tax authority. The seller integrates once through Shopify or eBay and a payment provider like Stripe or Adyen; after that, every EU order is a single transaction the broker clears.

This works because four separate regimes now fire on one parcel: a customs declaration under EU Union/customs rules, an IOSS VAT return under EU VAT Directive, EPR packaging contributions under member-state regimes that are live or rolling out (France's AGEC since 2022, Germany's Verpackungsgesetz update since 2023, Spain's Real Decreto 1055/2022 from 2024, with Italy, the Netherlands and others following), and a UK export declaration under post-Brexit rules. Today a seller needs four separate agents to ship one parcel; the broker collapses all four into a single transaction and charges a fee per parcel cleared.

The wedge is a proprietary HS-code classification model trained on millions of small-parcel flows, where multiple SKUs combine in one box and a wrong classification gets the parcel seized. Existing classification tools only work at the SKU level, not the parcel level, and the UK-to-EU corridor is structurally too small per shipper for the US-focused incumbents to bother with. Once a seller's catalogue is classified and their checkout is wired to charge EU VAT at the point of sale, switching cost is real.

UK DTC brands on Shopify Plus and eBay UK stores shipping 100 to 5,000 EU orders a month. The buyer is the founder or head of operations. Today they either disable EU shipping entirely, losing roughly 20 to 30 percent of potential revenue, or they pay legacy carriers like DHL and FedEx £8 to £15 a parcel who remit VAT badly and don't touch EPR at all.

HMRC Overseas Trade in Goods Statistics show UK parcel exports to the EU still down about a third on 2019, and EU member states tightened IOSS enforcement through 2024 and 2025. EPR for packaging is now live or legislated in eleven EU states, including Germany's LUCID registration, France's Citeo contributions and Spain's Royal Decree 1055, with Italy's EPR scheme live from 2025.
