UPSprint — the teacher's UPS pay progression pack, tailored to your school's pay policy.

Turns five years of teaching evidence into a school-specific Upper Pay Spine application that headteachers sign off on first read.

Every September, eligible teachers in England and Wales can apply to move from the Main Pay Scale (M1 to M6) to the Upper Pay Scale (U1 to U3). The threshold and what counts as acceptable evidence are set by each school's pay policy, drawn from the School Teachers' Pay and Conditions Document (STPCD) but varying meaningfully between schools. To pass, a teacher must show two consecutive years of sustained, high-quality teaching against the eight Teachers' Standards since their last threshold assessment.

The pain is acute and recurring. A failed application means losing between £3,000 and £8,000 of gross pay per year for the rest of the teacher's career. Many eligible teachers either never apply, because they assume they'll fail, or they apply with thin evidence that gets bounced for poor organisation, missing categories, or a letter that doesn't address the headteacher's stated criteria.

The tool is a prosumer pack. The teacher uploads their school's published pay policy (PDF) and logs evidence across eight categories that the Teachers' Standards already require schools to consider: lesson observations, pupil progress data, parent and pupil feedback, continuing professional development, extra-curricular contribution, assessment results, behaviour management, and contribution to the wider school ethos. The tool then maps each evidence item against the specific criteria named in that school's pay policy, drafts the application statement in the format the school's policy expects (length, tone, references), and outputs a paginated evidence portfolio with proper cross-references ready to hand to the headteacher.

The borrowed model is the grant-writing consultancy industry, where charities and small businesses pay £500 to £2,000 for a structured, evidence-mapped application to a funder. UPSprint productises that process for the teacher's threshold panel, at a price a single teacher can afford (£149 for the basic pack, £249 with a half-hour review by a serving senior leader).

The durable wedge is proprietary data. The tool builds a national index of school pay policies (scraped from school websites and recovered via Freedom of Information requests where schools do not publish) and a model trained on anonymised successful threshold applications drawn from public headteacher reports, social media case studies, and opt-in teacher submissions. That dataset compounds each September as the new cohort of policies lands and as the model ingests more anonymised successes.

Growth motion is search-led content marketing around the long-tail queries teachers actually type: 'how to write a UPS application', 'UPS application template', 'how to pass the UPS panel', 'failed UPS application what next'. YouTube case studies from serving UPS assessors and successful teachers build trust. Partnerships with the National Education Union (NEU), the NASUWT and the National Association of Head Teachers (NAHT) provide reach into staffrooms without the cost of paid acquisition.

Why now: the 2023 to 2024 teacher strikes put pay progression on the agenda in every staffroom, and tight school budgets mean headteachers are scrutinising threshold applications more rigorously, which raises rejection rates and makes a structured pack more obviously worth paying for than it was five years ago.
