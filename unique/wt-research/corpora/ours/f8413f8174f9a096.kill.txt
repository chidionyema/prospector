SitePass — the fixed-fee pack that gets a sole trader onto a main-contractor site.

A done-for-you service that prepares and submits a sole trader's CHAS or SafeContractor accreditation, so they can work for main contractors without spending days on paperwork.

Main contractors in England increasingly require SSIP accreditation (typically CHAS, SafeContractor, or SMAS) before letting any tradesperson on a commercial site. For a sole trader, the application demands a written health-and-safety policy, several risk assessments, method statements for each activity, training evidence, insurance certificates, and a current DBS check. The forms take 15 to 25 hours for a first-timer, and incomplete submissions are routinely bounced back for revision. The only paid help available today is a generalist health-and-safety consultant who charges £1,500 to £5,000, well beyond what a sole trader can justify for one job.

SitePass borrows the model used by immigration visa agencies: collect the trader's circumstances, produce the supporting documents, submit on their behalf, and charge a fixed price. For £450, the customer receives a tailored health-and-safety policy, four to six risk assessments, method statements for their specific trade, the supporting evidence pack, and the application itself completed and submitted. Resubmission on rejection is included. Annual renewals repeat the fee at a discounted rate, giving a recurring revenue base.

The wedge is the document library. Each completed application feeds the templates for the next: a method statement for boiler installations, a risk assessment for working at height, a COSHH sheet for solvent use. After two years, the library covers the common trades well enough that drafting time drops below two hours per job, which keeps margin at roughly 60% even at the £450 price point. CHAS itself cannot act as the applicant's paid representative without a conflict of interest, and the scheme rules forbid it; the free templates on its website stop at guidance, not bespoke drafting.

Self-employed electricians, plumbers, gas engineers, scaffolders, and decorators who want to work on commercial building sites. They are typically aged 30 to 55, charge £250 to £500 per day, and currently subcontract to main contractors. Budget authority sits with them personally; the £450 fee is a one-day invoice, below their usual sign-off threshold.

Since the Building Safety Act 2022 and the post-Grenfell competence reforms, most tier-one main contractors in England now require SSIP accreditation before issuing a purchase order. The HSE's CDM 2015 duty-holder rules have trickled down to procurement, with main contractors passing the burden of proof onto their subcontractors. CHAS alone has reported a sharp rise in sole-trader applications year on year. At the same time, the cost of a generic H&S consultant has risen, leaving a fixed-fee productised service aimed at the sole trader market wide open.
