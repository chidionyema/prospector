GearRelay — same-day loan cameras and lighting for freelance photographers and videographers when their kit breaks or gets stolen.

A monthly membership that gets a freelance photographer or videographer a same-day loan camera, lens or lighting kit from a nearby partner store the moment their own gear fails or is stolen, so a booked shoot never gets cancelled.

A freelance photographer or videographer earns nothing on the days they can't shoot. If a camera body dies, a lens gets dropped, or a kit bag is stolen from a van the night before a paid job, the work still has to happen tomorrow. Right now the only backstop is a specialist camera insurer like Photoguard or Aaduki, and even their loan-while-repaired benefit works by posting a replacement out from a central warehouse, which typically takes one to three working days by courier. For a freelancer with a shoot at 9am, that gap is the whole problem.

GearRelay is a membership, not an insurance policy. Each member keeps a simple kit list on file — camera bodies, lenses, flashes, tripods, gimbals — and pays a flat monthly fee. When something breaks or goes missing, they open the app, mark the item down, and get matched to the nearest partner camera or hire shop in a small network across cities such as London, Manchester, Bristol and Leeds, where a loaner from that shop's own rental stock is waiting for same-day collection. The partner shop earns a fee per loan plus footfall from photographers who often end up buying accessories while they're there; GearRelay takes the membership revenue and a cut of every loan.

The reason a national insurer can't simply add this: their loan-kit benefit is built around a repair claim workflow and a small number of centralised warehouses, because insurance economics reward minimising held stock, not maximising local physical density. Turning that into a same-day, in-person, multi-city depot network means becoming a logistics and retail-partnership operator, which sits outside how an insurer prices and staffs itself. A small team can instead go city by city, signing exclusive loan-stock agreements with the two or three busiest independent camera and hire shops in each creative hub — once a shop is locked in as GearRelay's local partner, a rival needs its own separate depot relationships in the same handful of shops, and most towns don't have room for two.

Freelance photographers, videographers and content creators who earn day rates or project fees and cannot afford a missed shoot day — paying roughly £15 to £30 a month depending on how much kit is on their list, plus a small collection fee per loan used.

The UK freelance creative sector has kept growing since the pandemic, and camera-owning creators increasingly work solo without a studio's spare kit cupboard to fall back on. Peer-to-peer camera rental platforms like LensesForHire and the second-hand market MPB have already proven UK photographers will pay for flexible access to gear rather than owning everything outright — GearRelay applies that same appetite to emergency continuity instead of planned rental.
