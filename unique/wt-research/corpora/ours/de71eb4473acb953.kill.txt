ChargeBreak – find and fight the overcharges in your California hospital bill, for $29.

A web app that uses hospital price transparency data to slash your out-of-pocket medical costs.

Gen Z gig workers in California often face surprise hospital bills with no coverage to cushion the blow. Hospitals now post their full chargemaster prices online, but the data sits in unusable spreadsheets. ChargeBreak scrapes that data, matches it to your bill line by line, and drafts a formal dispute letter demanding the price be reduced to a fair cash rate. 

It works like a procurement cost‑benchmarking tool transplanted to personal healthcare. Every charge is flagged if it exceeds a threshold, and the letter cites the hospital’s own numbers – a tactic vendors use against each other, now weaponised for patients. 

The company compiles and normalises chargemasters from all California hospitals, adding cash‑price benchmarks from insurer transparency files. That dataset, refreshed weekly, is the moat: a new entrant would need to build the same pipeline from scratch. The tool is self‑serve, so one person can run it. 

Growth comes from TikTok and Instagram content showing real bill reductions, plus partnerships with gig‑worker unions in Los Angeles and the Bay Area. Users pay $29 per bill, with a refund if the hospital doesn’t cut the total within 30 days.

The federal Hospital Price Transparency rule and California’s own laws (like SB‑24) force hospitals to publish their prices. But no consumer‑facing tool turns that data into easy bill reductions. Enforcement is ramping up, flooding the public domain with usable numbers for the first time.
