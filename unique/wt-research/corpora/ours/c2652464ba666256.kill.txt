BelowElbow — the cooling base layer built to fit any NHS Trust uniform.

A direct-to-consumer brand of short-sleeved, 60°C-washable cooling base layers, designed to be worn under NHS tunics during UK heatwaves without breaking Trust uniform policy.

UK heatwaves are getting worse — the 2022 heatwave pushed temperatures above 40°C for the first time on Met Office records, and most NHS hospitals in England don't have air conditioning. NHS staff wear the same uniform regardless of temperature, and the rules are strict: bare below the elbows in clinical areas, washable at 60°C or higher for infection control under the NHS decontamination standard, no visible logos, and a close fit. Athletic cooling wear from Nike, Under Armour and Cool-jams is the obvious alternative, but it is designed for sport, not clinical work — long sleeves, logos that fail uniform rules, and fabric that degrades at 60°C wash temperatures. The brand designs specifically for the NHS rule set: short sleeves, no external branding, a fabric blend that survives the wash cycle clinical staff actually use, and a fit that disappears under a tunic. It sells direct to NHS nurses, healthcare assistants, midwives and paramedics through a website that knows the buyer's Trust by name, with bulk procurement by the Trust itself as a secondary channel. The unit economics clear at £35 to £50 per garment, with a 30 to 40 percent gross margin and a six to twelve month repurchase cycle. The specific value is the years of design expertise built up from working through every NHS Trust's uniform policy in England — more than 200 Trusts, each with different rules on sleeve length, colour, fabric and fit. This is replicable in principle, but takes years to build properly.

NHS nurses, healthcare assistants, midwives and paramedics in England — roughly 1.4 million staff who buy their own workwear and work 12-hour shifts, often in non-air-conditioned buildings. They pay out of take-home pay capped by years of public sector pay restraint, but they pay because nothing else on the market fits the Trust uniform rules.
