ShiftEase, mobile chair-massage for NHS shift changeover.

A mobile chair-massage service that parks outside NHS hospital staff exits at shift changeover, so night and day shift workers ease muscle pain before driving home.

A solo massage therapist, working from a fitted van or a fold-out gazebo, sets up right at the staff exit or car park walkway of one named NHS acute Trust site. They time it to the 07:00 and 19:00 changeover that 12-hour shift rotas under Agenda for Change create. Staff book a 15-minute chair massage on a simple link before they clock off, pay by card, and are back in their car in under 20 minutes.

This borrows the model of post-marathon recovery tents and the old US on-demand massage-van services, and plants that exact idea at the one pinch point every shift worker already walks through exhausted: the route from ward to car park. It is not another wellbeing app. It is a chair, some oil, and 15 minutes, sited at the queue that already forms.

What holds it in place once it exists is the pitch itself. NHS Trusts already licence external vendors — coffee carts, food trucks, faith rooms — through Estates & Facilities commercial licensing. Once one operator holds the standing site agreement for that exit at that Trust, a copycat therapist cannot simply turn up; they would need to win the same site licence away from an incumbent who already has the relationship and the reliability record. That is a genuine local exclusivity, not a app-store feature that anyone can copy overnight.

Individual NHS nurses, porters, healthcare assistants and other shift staff, paying roughly £12-15 out of their own pocket for a single session straight after a 12-hour shift. It is a personal spend, like a coffee or a car park snack, not a Trust procurement.

The NHS Staff Survey 2024 found musculoskeletal problems are among the leading causes of staff sickness absence, and the NHS Long Term Workforce Plan and People Promise are actively pushing Trusts to open their sites to external staff-wellbeing vendors rather than relying only on in-house occupational health, which has multi-week physiotherapy waiting lists. Shift-pattern fatigue and burnout remain elevated post-pandemic, and Trusts are under pressure to be seen supporting staff retention with visible, low-cost, on-site perks.
