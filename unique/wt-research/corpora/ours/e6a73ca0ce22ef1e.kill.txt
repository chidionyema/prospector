ComputeSheet, where AI founders track GPU supply.

A weekly subscriber brief that reads hyperscaler capex filings, foundry earnings, and grid queue data so an AI founder knows when GPU compute will loosen or tighten.

ComputeSheet is a weekly paid brief for AI startup CTOs and CFOs. Each issue translates the public filing calendar of hyperscaler capex, foundry revenue, equipment vendor bookings, grid interconnection queues, and EIA data-center electricity demand into a forward view of when and where GPU capacity will loosen or tighten over the next 6 to 18 months.

This borrows the way commodity analysts forecast oil, copper, and lithium supply by reading OPEC output, refinery utilization, and rig counts, and applies the same playbook to AI accelerators. The same model exists in shipping intelligence (Clarksons, Drewry) and partially in semiconductor analysis, but no one has normalized it specifically for AI compute buyers. At a $30 to $80 monthly subscription plus sponsorship from colocation brokers and GPU resellers, an AI founder can avoid signing a three-year cloud commitment at the peak of the cycle, or pull forward a training run when capacity loosens.

The dataset compounds. Each quarter adds normalized observations across MSFT, GOOGL, META, AMZN, and ORCL capex lines; TSMC, UMC, and Intel Foundry monthly revenue; ASML, Applied Materials, and Lam Research bookings; ERCOT, PJM, MISO, and CAISO interconnection queues; and EIA data-center demand. After four quarters the analyst has a calibrated baseline; after eight, a defensible forward view. A Bloomberg Terminal subscriber could read each filing individually, but no publication aggregates them into a unified GPU-availability forward view for compute buyers, and hyperscaler IR teams strategically avoid cross-firm comparisons.

The buyer role is the CTO or CFO at a Series A through C AI startup, a budget-holding person inside a software company who faces the recurring chore of planning cloud and training contracts. The recurring trigger is the quarterly capex cycle, monthly foundry revenue prints, and new grid interconnection filings. The deliverable is a five-to-ten page weekly brief with three to five vendor or facility profiles plus a forward supply forecast.
