The Inheritance Heat Map.

A solo-operated data intelligence service that maps and scores UK residential properties by their inheritance tax (IHT) exposure risk, sold to regional estate agents and solicitors as a prospecting tool for probate and downsizing instructions.

Invert: while most IHT advisory tools target the wealthy individual, the real pain is on the supply side — estate agents and solicitors who need a repeatable, data-driven way to identify properties likely to come to market via inheritance (death, downsizing, or IHT-driven sale). Land Registry data, death registrations and council tax bands, when cross-referenced, can flag high-probability leads. A solo operator can scrape public data, build a scoring model, and sell territory-exclusive reports monthly. The buyer is a named partner (e.g. 'Churchills of Cheltenham') who pays a subscription for a feed of pre-qualified leads they can convert into instructions, bypassing generic Rightmove scraping. The wedge is proprietary_data: the scoring algorithm and its integration of death data with property characteristics is hard to replicate without similar data assembly and domain judgment.

Regional estate agents (10-50 staff) and probate solicitors in affluent areas with high property values, e.g. 'Carter Jonas, Oxford office', who need a repeatable, high-conviction prospecting channel for probate/downsizing instructions — a pain that currently relies on networking and luck.

UK inheritance tax receipts hit £7.5bn in 2023 (up 10% YoY) and the nil-rate band is frozen until 2028, forcing more estates into tax. Death registrations are public but not cross-referenced with property data anywhere. Agents are desperate for instruction flow as transaction volumes slump.
