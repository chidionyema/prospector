PumpWire — the Monday briefing UK heating engineers read to win more heat pump work.

A weekly 10-minute audio briefing UK heating engineers listen to in the van, on heat pump brand reliability, BUS grant changes, and demand hotspots.

PumpWire is a free weekly email and 10-minute audio briefing that aggregates proprietary installer-side data on UK heat pump installs and delivers it in the same short, punchy trader-floor format used by Bloomberg and Morning Brew for finance readers.

The UK is mid-way through a structural shift away from gas boilers. From April 2024, the Clean Heat Market Mechanism sets annual sales thresholds for boiler manufacturers; manufacturers that fall short must buy credits or pay fines, which has triggered a recruitment push for installer partners. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme was topped up with £1.5 billion in the Spring 2024 Budget and now runs through 2028, paying homeowners £7,500 towards an air source heat pump. The Future Homes Standard, when enacted, will require new builds to be low-carbon heating ready. The installers who move first capture the surge in domestic demand; the installers who wait face shrinking gas work and no heat pump pipeline.

The problem is information. Installer-side reliability data is locked inside the heads of working engineers. Manufacturer data is marketing. Trade press arrives monthly. Boiler Upgrade Scheme amounts and MCS standards (the trade's MIS 3005 quality mark for heat pumps) change without much fanfare. The reader is the working UK heating engineer, Gas Safe registered, on the tools, often self-employed, who cannot attend the trade shows but needs to know: which heat pump brands are failing in real installs, where BUS applications are stalling, which training providers have space, what the latest MCS update changes for the quotes sent yesterday.

PumpWire is free on Monday morning, monetising through a paid premium tier (£15 a month or £150 a year) that includes a quarterly proprietary installer survey (compiled from 200-plus responses), a regional demand heatmap built from planning permission data, and an off-the-record monthly call with a manufacturer guest. Additional revenue comes from training-provider sponsorships, because MCS-approved heat pump courses cost £500 to £2,000 a student, and from one-off deep-dive reports sold to merchants such as City Plumbing, Screwfix Trade, and Travis Perkins.

The wedge is the compounding installer survey dataset. Each quarter the same respondents report what they are seeing on real UK installs: failed compressors, slow MCS certifications, BUS payment delays. That dataset grows more valuable with every wave and is structurally unavailable to monthly print magazines such as HVP or Plumbing, Heating & Plumbing News, whose business model is print advertising rather than subscriber-funded depth. A free first-party alternative does not exist because the regulators (MCS, DESNZ) publish standards, not installer sentiment, and the manufacturers publish marketing, not failure rates. PumpWire sits in the gap, and the weekly email cadence gives the solo operator a search footprint the monthly print incumbents structurally cannot build.
