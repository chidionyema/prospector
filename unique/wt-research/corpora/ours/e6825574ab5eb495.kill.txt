ReScreen — The Gig Driver's Deactivation Reversal Desk.

A done-for-you dispute service that reconstructs and challenges the erroneous background-check records that got rideshare and delivery drivers deactivated, packaged as a platform-specific reinstatement dossier.

Lens: analogical — transplant the consumer credit-repair firm model (contingency/fixed-fee specialists who dispute errors on a report you can't easily fix yourself) out of lending and into the gig background-screening sector, where the exact same FCRA machinery governs Checkr/Sterling reports but no productized service exists. A Gen Z rideshare/delivery driver's single acute daily fear is a sudden 'you've been deactivated pending re-screen' notice that zeroes their income overnight — usually triggered by a mismatched county court record, a stale expunged charge, or an identity mixup in the background vendor's data. The Why-Now: platforms now run continuous/rolling re-screens (not just at hire), so the deactivation volume has structurally multiplied, and Checkr-class errors surface mid-tenure to people already dependent on the income. ReScreen sells a fixed-fee dossier: it pulls the driver's report, identifies the disputable record, generates the FCRA §611 dispute letter plus the platform-specific reinstatement packet (the exact evidence and appeal path Uber/DoorDash/Lyft's trust-and-safety team responds to), and tracks it to resolution. The compounding asset is a proprietary outcome dataset — which error types, on which vendor, disputed which way, got which platform to reinstate, and how fast — that no first party will ever build.

Rideshare and delivery drivers (Uber/Lyft/DoorDash/Amazon Flex, aged ~19-27) who have just been deactivated or suspended pending a background re-screen — a specifically nameable payer whose entire income was cut this week and who will pay a fixed fee to get reinstated fast.

Platforms have shifted from one-time hiring checks to continuous/rolling re-screening via Checkr and Sterling, multiplying mid-tenure deactivations; simultaneously FCRA §611 dispute rights and free annual report access exist but are unmapped to gig-platform reinstatement paths, leaving a navigable gap.
