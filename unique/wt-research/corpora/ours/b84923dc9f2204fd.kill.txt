BIPA pre-delivery scans for Illinois app agencies.

AI scans your agency's app for Illinois biometric privacy violations before client delivery, so a $5,000-per-violation lawsuit never lands.

Plain version: an AI reviews the code, UI flows and consent strings of an app the agency is about to ship, flags every BIPA (740 ILCS 14) violation it can find, and returns a fixed-fee remediation pack an engineer can action before release.

Why it works: Illinois biometric privacy law carries statutory damages of $1,000 per negligent scan and $5,000 per intentional or reckless scan, with attorney's fees, and the Rosenbach v. Six Flags and Cothron v. White Castle lines mean a single agency-shipped app can produce millions in exposure. Illinois dev agencies shipping biometric features cannot ignore this; they also cannot afford a $500-per-hour BIPA attorney to review every release.

Why it holds: the scan rules are compiled from BIPA case law (Mosby v. Ingersoll-Rand, Cothron, Rosenbach) and update as courts refine what counts as written consent, retention schedule, or purpose. Borrows from the FDA 510(k) pre-market review pattern, where a third-party reviewer flags device issues before submission; this is the same kind of review before the app reaches the client. A free BIPA attorney consultation is one-shot; this runs on every commit and updates with case law automatically.

Illinois-based dev agency owners (typically 3 to 15 people) building consumer apps with face, finger, voice or iris recognition for their clients.

White Castle decision expanded damages to per-scan; agencies are increasingly named as developers alongside deployers.
