SteerClear Atlas — The Over-70 Driver's DVLA Renewal Decision Brief.

A retiree-facing data intelligence product that aggregates per-condition DVLA medical declaration outcome probabilities and per-driving-assessment-centre pricing/wait times into a personalised decision dossier for over-70s facing licence renewal.

Retirees aged 70-85 with DVLA-notifiable medical conditions face acute anxiety about whether their disclosure will trigger restriction or revocation, because public DVLA statistics are coarse annual aggregates that give no personalised probability for their specific condition combination. By drilling narrowly into this single retiree decision point and aggregating three proprietary data layers — (1) per-condition DVLA outcome breakdowns via templated FOI requests to DVLA's Medical Driving Standards team, (2) per-region driving assessment centre pricing and wait times scraped from RoSPA/IAM/Driveability/KMPT member sites and cross-checked against published NHS referrals, and (3) opt-in retiree-reported outcome submissions that compound with each user — we can sell a personalised dossier that quantifies the retiree's likely outcome and routes them to the cheapest, fastest assessment centre if restriction is likely. The lens is narrow: rather than chase the broader over-70 driving market, we go deep on the highest-anxiety sub-segment where the data asymmetry between the retiree and DVLA is worst.

Retirees aged 70-85 holding a UK driving licence with one or more DVLA-notifiable medical conditions (Type 2 diabetes on insulin, primary open-angle glaucoma, obstructive sleep apnoea, prior stroke/TIA, Parkinson's disease, mild cognitive impairment, monocular vision, implantable defibrillator, etc.), plus their adult children who frequently manage the renewal process on the retiree's behalf. Specifically the segment within 8 weeks of a renewal deadline — too late to take chances and willing to pay £29-£79 for clarity.

Three converging signals: DVLA tightened medical enforcement with its reformed Medical Driving Standards framework from 2023 onward, the UK over-70 population has grown roughly 35% in a decade creating a far larger payer pool, and the proliferation of private driving assessment centres (RoSPA, IAM, Driveability, KMPT, Mobility Centre variants) has fragmented pricing and wait-time data that no incumbent consolidates. FOI response rates for DVLA medical outcome breakdowns have also improved markedly under post-COVID transparency pressure, making the proprietary dataset buildable by a solo operator within a single quarter.

Passages show DVLA makes nearly 900,000 medical licensing decisions per year and has removed licences from over 500,000 car/motorcycle owners due to medical conditions, with standards actively amended (e.g., the cognitive impairment update). This confirms the medical-renewal decision is a real, recurring, high-consequence event for licence holders, forming a durable basis for a personalised decision dossier rather than a fabricated or evaporating need.
