FFI Strikeback — Contingent Funding for HSE Fee-for-Intervention Invoice Disputes.

A no-win-no-fee risk-financing desk that fronts the full cost and outcome risk of formally disputing an HSE Fee for Intervention invoice for small trades firms, taking 35% of whatever the invoice is reduced by and nothing if the challenge fails.

Lens: invert. The UK Health & Safety Executive bills duty-holders at a rising hourly rate (£180+/hr) the moment an inspector records a 'material breach', so a routine site visit becomes a surprise £700–£15,000 invoice for a self-employed builder — and the invoice grows the longer HSE investigates. FFI invoices are disputable through a formal query/dispute process, and a meaningful share are reduced or withdrawn on grounds of over-recorded hours, misclassified breach severity, or activity not attributable to the breach — but a time-poor tradesperson facing an adversarial regulator neither knows the win-rate nor wants to pay a consultant hourly to find out. The business is pure risk financing: the operator underwrites each individual dispute with their own time and absorbs the loss when a challenge fails, funded by a contingency cut of successful reductions. The asset compounds with enforcement intensity — every rate rise and every HSE inspection blitz on small construction sites increases both the invoice pool and the fee per win. HSE structurally cannot ship this: a regulator cannot take a commercial side against its own cost-recovery invoices, cannot indemnify duty-holders against its own charging errors, and its 'free dispute process' is the venue, not the advocate. Selection is the whole game: a private, compounding ledger of which breach categories, inspector write-ups, and hour-logs actually get cut lets the operator fund only positive-expected-value disputes — the underwriting judgment IS the product, and it is solo-holdable taste plus data, not capital or headcount.

Self-employed builders, roofers, scaffolders and small trades firms of 1–9 operatives who have received an HSE Fee for Intervention invoice, typically £700–£15,000; they pay nothing upfront and 35% of the achieved reduction on success, so the payer is solvent by construction (the fee comes out of money they no longer owe).

The FFI hourly rate has been raised repeatedly and HSE has run intensified inspection campaigns targeting small construction and refurbishment sites (dust, work-at-height, welfare), pushing more first-time FFI invoices onto micro-firms with no in-house safety function; simultaneously, LLM tooling collapses the cost of parsing an inspector's notification of contravention, reconciling logged hours against the visit record, and drafting a structured dispute — making per-case underwriting viable for one person.
