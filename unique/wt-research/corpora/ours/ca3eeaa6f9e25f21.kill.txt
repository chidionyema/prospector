HomeAdapt Bridge — the primary carer's fixed-fee loan broker that gets home adaptations started now with the council's own grant money.

We arrange a bridge loan secured on your council's Disabled Facilities Grant, so your disabled relative doesn't wait months for essential adaptations.

The Disabled Facilities Grant (DFG) under the Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996 provides up to £30,000 for adaptations like stairlifts, wet rooms and ramps. But councils routinely take 6–12 months from application to payment – a dangerous delay for a child who can't access a toilet or an older person at risk of falling. HomeAdapt Bridge connects the family to a panel of specialist lenders who advance the grant amount upfront, secured against the council's binding obligation to pay. We use a proprietary dataset of council processing times, grant refusal rates and local authority financial health to score applications in real time, approving bridge loans within 48 hours. The family pays us a flat fee of £349 for the arrangement; lenders pay us an introducer commission. This borrows the invoice-financing model – unlocking trapped statutory cash – and turns a council's growing administrative bottleneck into our engine. Every loan we place deepens our risk data, making our scoring model harder to replicate. Distribution flows naturally through occupational therapists, GP practices, care managers and charities like Age UK, who all want adaptations to happen without delay.

The primary carer or disabled homeowner pays the flat £349 fee; the lender pays us a commission on each loan.

The LGA and the Ombudsman are increasingly critical of these delays, so demand for a faster private alternative is acute and growing.
