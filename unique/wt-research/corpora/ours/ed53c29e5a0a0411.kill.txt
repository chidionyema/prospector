RangeProof — a sensor kit that logs when your hens go outside and proves their free-range life to buyers.

Automatically generate batch-level free-range evidence for supermarket buyers, using door sensors and weather data to replace manual logbooks.

Small UK free-range egg producers struggle to convince retail buyers that their hens genuinely spend enough time outdoors each day. The current method is a daily manual logbook — imprecise, easy to forget, and often met with scepticism by auditors. RangeProof gives them a simple, self-install sensor kit (door reed switch, outdoor weather station, base unit with GPS) that records each flock's pop-hole openings and the exact local conditions. Every batch of eggs leaves with a tamper-proof digital report showing hours of outdoor access in all weathers, timestamped and mapped. The producer uses it to secure better contracts with regional supermarkets or farm shops, because the buyer can trust the evidence. A free dashboard preview shows a sample report; a one-off hardware purchase (£199) plus monthly subscription (£19) unlocks live logging and automated batch reports. The solo behind it writes the firmware and web app once, dropships pre-assembled kits, and supports users with a searchable knowledge base and weekly community video call.

Owner-operators of free-range egg farms with 500–2,000 hens, selling to regional supermarkets or high-end farm shops. They pay for the hardware and a monthly subscription because the alternative is losing a premium supply contract or getting a lower price.

Major UK retailers are tightening free-range verification after repeated welfare scandals. The British Lion Code already demands range-access records, and the Defra ‘cage-free’ transition plus the Animal Welfare (Kept Animals) Bill (which contains stricter labelling rules) makes verifiable proof urgent. At the same time, cheap IoT components and low-code platforms make it possible for one person to build and run this without venture capital.
