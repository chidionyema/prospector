AdaptClear Desk — The Carer's Post-Care Major Home-Adaptation Removal & Resale Broker.

When the person a primary carer has been caring for at home dies or moves into a care home, the home is left with a stairlift, a level-access shower, a ceiling hoist and a wet-room floor — adaptations that block re-sale of the property, cost £300–£1,500 each to remove, and that an estate agent has just told the carer will knock £4–£9k off the asking price. AdaptClear aggregates the small, fragmented UK trade of specialist adaptation-removal contractors, brokers the booking within 48 hours, and also brokers resale of cleaned-and-tested units to second-user installers — converting an invoice into a £150–£400 net rebate in the carer's pocket.

Primary carers (current or very recent) handling the property transition at the end of home-based caring are uniquely time-pressed, emotionally overwhelmed, and unfamiliar with the small specialist trade that exists for safe adaptation removal and resale; a solo broker who has pre-vetted 5–8 regional contractors and a proprietary database of refurbisher buyers can convert a transaction the carer would otherwise pay full retail for, into a competitive removal bid bundled with a small rebate paid out of the buyer's margin.

Two payer legs of the broker's transaction: (1) the primary carer pays the chosen removal contractor the agreed quote at market rate (the broker charges the carer 0%), and (2) the refurbisher buyer pays the broker 8–12% of the resale value once the cleaned-and-tested unit is delivered. Where the bundled resale value exceeds the removal quote, the carer receives a small rebate rather than an invoice — outcomes-aligned, because the carer's time-budget makes price-shopping functionally impossible.

Three simultaneous 2025–26 forces: (a) a record cohort of over-85s dying or transitioning into residential care after the 2020–24 home-care surge, leaving behind an unprecedented volume of high-spec domestic adaptations that estate agents are now flagging as material drag on listed prices; (b) UK property transactions averaging 9–14 weeks, with RICS surveyors and major chains (Countrywide, Purplebricks estate-agent blog data) explicitly citing wet-rooms and overhead hoists as negotiation leverage for buyers; (c) the second-user mobility/disability equipment resale market has consolidated into a handful of buyers (Recare, Mobility 2 You, Better Mobility, Consolor) with no intermediary actively brokering the private-household supply side, despite ~£40–£80m of used domestic stock clearing each year via word-of-mouth and classifieds.
