Unpaid-hours audits for NHS doctors and nurses.

An API that reads published NHS rotas and timesheets, applies each worker's contract terms, and returns the overtime, unsocial hours uplifts and Working Time holiday pay owed.

NHS staff regularly do hours their trust never pays them for. The 2023 BMA junior doctor survey found 79% worked beyond their contracted hours; the same is true across nurses, midwives and allied health professionals. The shortfall per worker per year runs from a few hundred to several thousand pounds.

Calculating what's owed is messy because pay depends on several overlapping things: the worker's contract (NHS medical and dental terms for doctors; Agenda for Change for nurses and others), unsocial hours uplifts (nights at 30%, weekends at 50%, bank holidays higher), on-call availability allowances, and Working Time Regulations holiday accrual (4 weeks under regulation 13 of the Working Time Regulations 1998, rising to 5.6 weeks).

NHS trusts use e-roster systems (Allocate, HealthRoster, RosterMaster) but those systems show the trust's view of the rota, not the worker's view of what they are owed. There is no commercial product that turns a worker's rota into a per-shift underpayment figure.

HoursBack ingests a published rota (as a PDF, photo or structured data), the worker's contract details and an optional timesheet of hours actually worked. It applies the right NHS terms and returns a structured breakdown: per-shift underpayment, total annual shortfall, and a draft grievance letter plus an employment tribunal evidence pack. It does not file anything; the worker, or their union rep, submits.

Every rota the system processes adds to a private dataset: how each NHS trust pays overtime, which specialties have the worst breaches, which contract clauses are most often misapplied. That dataset makes the next estimate sharper and the next grievance stronger. It cannot be bought or scraped; only paying workers for their data builds it.

This borrows the way auto insurers price premiums against a published telematics index, and applies it to NHS working hours. Just as the insurer's data tells the driver whether the premium is fair, HoursBack's data tells the worker whether their pay is fair.

Growth: BMA membership benefits (about 190,000 members), NHS forums (Doctors.net, Reddit r/JuniorDoctorsUK, Tiko's Hub), employment law firm partnerships that take cases on no-win-no-fee, and TikTok and Instagram where NHS workers openly share pay horror stories. Side door: integration with payroll-on-demand apps (Wagestream, Hastee) that already serve NHS staff.

NHS specialty registrars, consultants and senior nurses (NHS pay band 6 and 7) aged 30 to 60, on stable income and a defined pension, who routinely do unpaid overtime; a recurring £500 to £5,000 per worker per year in unclaimed pay. Also the employment lawyers and BMA local negotiating committee reps who represent them.

Three years of NHS industrial action have made pay a normal conversation. The NHS Long Term Workforce Plan (2023) has intensified workload pressure. E-roster adoption is now near-universal so paper trails exist. AI vision APIs make parsing messy rota PDFs into structured data economically viable at the point of need.
