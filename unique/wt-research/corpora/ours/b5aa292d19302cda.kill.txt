Bench Handover, proves your outsourced code was tested.

A tool for UK startup founders using offshore dev agencies that turns each sprint's delivered code into an evidence pack their next investor or enterprise buyer will accept.

Most early-stage UK software startups build with a contracted agency or a handful of offshore contractors rather than staff engineers. When a paying enterprise customer or a due-diligence team asks "who wrote this, was it tested, and can you maintain it without them", the founder has nothing but a Slack history and an invoice.

Bench Handover sits on the startup's GitHub or GitLab organisation and watches what the outside team actually ships. Every sprint it produces one short document: which contractor account authored which files, which pull requests were reviewed by someone at the startup, which parts of the code have no tests at all, and which parts only one outside person has ever touched. That last number is the one that scares buyers, and nobody currently puts a figure on it.

This borrows the way a factory audit works in clothing supply chains. A retailer will not buy from a factory on the factory's word; an inspector walks the floor each season and issues a report the buyer trusts. The same discipline has never been applied to the outsourced code a startup sells as its own product. The tool is the inspector, and the sprint is the season.

The part that holds is the history. The pack is only convincing because it covers eighteen months of sprints, not one. A founder who starts collecting in month three has something in month twenty-one that a rival who starts the week before diligence cannot fake, because the commits are timestamped and the reviews either happened or did not. Once the pack is the thing the founder sends to every enterprise procurement team, swapping it out means starting the record again from zero.

UK grounding is concrete. The pack maps directly onto questions in the Cyber Essentials and Cyber Essentials Plus scheme run by IASME on behalf of the National Cyber Security Centre, which UK public-sector buyers require for many contracts. It also feeds the supplier questions in the Crown Commercial Service G-Cloud framework application, where a startup must describe its development and testing practices to sell to government at all. Founders re-answer those same questions every year and at every enterprise sale.

Pricing is a flat monthly fee per code repository organisation, roughly £120 to £400 a month depending on how many outside contributors are active, with a one-off fee for a diligence-ready bundle when a funding round or a large contract lands.

This is the sells-to-tech shape. The buyer role is the technical founder or first head of engineering at a UK startup with no in-house security or compliance staff. The recurring trigger is each sprint close, each annual Cyber Essentials renewal, and each enterprise security questionnaire. The deliverable is the evidence pack. The public source proving the pain is already paid for is the Cyber Essentials scheme requirements published by IASME and NCSC, plus the Digital Marketplace G-Cloud supplier questions on gov.uk, both of which UK startups pay consultants to answer today.

Technical founders and first engineering leads at UK software startups of five to forty people who build with an outside agency or offshore contractors, and who are selling into enterprise or public-sector buyers. They typically pay a consultant a few thousand pounds to prepare answers for each security questionnaire and each Cyber Essentials renewal.

UK public-sector buying through G-Cloud and the wider Crown Commercial Service frameworks keeps pushing supplier security questions down to the smallest vendors, and Cyber Essentials certification is now a common condition of contract. At the same time, small UK startups lean harder on contracted and offshore engineering than they did five years ago, so the people who know the code are often not employees. The gap between what buyers ask and what founders can prove has widened, and it is checked at every renewal.
