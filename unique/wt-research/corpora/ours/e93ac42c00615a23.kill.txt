Locker Round, weekly bike-locker cleaning at rural rail stops.

A weekly round that cleans, de-rusts and re-labels the secure cycle lockers at small UK railway stations, so the train operator can prove they are usable and let them out again.

Small stations across Britain have banks of secure cycle lockers and cycle hubs, paid for years ago by grant money. Many now sit unusable: seized locks, wasp nests, fly-tipped rubbish, faded numbering, and a waiting list nobody clears. Station staff are stretched, and the cleaning contract usually covers the platform and the waiting room, not a metal box in the car park.

This is a local round run on foot and by van. Once a week you open every locker on a station, empty abandoned items, oil and replace barrel locks, wire-brush and touch up rust, renumber with weatherproof labels, photograph each unit, and file a one-page condition sheet. You charge per locker per month, roughly £4 to £7, on a standing order from the station's budget holder.

The idea borrows the way commercial window-cleaning rounds work. A window cleaner does not sell a clean window; they sell a route nobody else can serve at that price, because they are already on the street. The same logic applies here: once you hold the round for eight stations on one line, your travel cost per locker collapses, and a national facilities firm cannot bid against you without sending a crew for half a day's work.

The durable part is the photo history. After a year you hold dated condition images for every locker on your line, which is exactly what a station operator needs when a customer claims a bike was damaged, when Network Rail queries an asset, or when a council asks whether its cycle grant money produced anything. That record lives on your drive, not theirs, and a competitor arriving in year two starts with nothing.

Start free: email the station facilities manager of every station on one line, offer to survey one station at no charge, and hand back the photo sheet. The sheet sells the round.

Station facilities managers and cycle-parking leads at train operators such as Northern, Great Western Railway and Southeastern, plus council cycling officers who funded the lockers and must show they are in use. Typical spend is £150 to £400 a month per station.

Cycle parking at stations was heavily grant-funded through the last decade, and much of that kit is now at the age where locks and hinges fail. Train operators are under close scrutiny on station condition, and councils that spent active travel money are being asked what it delivered. Nobody's existing cleaning contract covers the lockers.
