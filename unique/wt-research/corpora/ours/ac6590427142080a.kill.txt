FreelanceCaseWatch — the freelancer’s guide to UK tribunal and court rulings that set your rights.

A weekly digest and searchable archive of employment tribunal, small claims and county court cases where a freelancer sued a client, summarised in plain English so you know your legal standing.

A subscription media product that scrapes public judgments from BAILII and HM Courts & Tribunals Service, uses AI to identify cases involving freelance creatives (designers, writers, developers, consultants) and summarises the ruling, key arguments and what it means for similar disputes. A free tier delivers a weekly email with one featured case; a paid tier unlocks the full searchable archive and alerts when new relevant cases are published. The solo operator uses legal‑AI summarisation tools plus light editorial oversight to produce reliable, jargon‑free briefings. The moat is the proprietary taxonomy and curated database of freelance‑specific case law, which compounds with each new judgment. Competitors would need to build the same classification system and archive from scratch. The product turns a scattered, impenetrable public resource into actionable knowledge that helps freelancers before they end up in court — or gives them the confidence to push back when a client breaches a contract.

Freelance creatives who want to understand their legal rights regarding non‑payment, scope creep, copyright, misclassification or contract breaches. A paid subscription costs £9 per month for full archive access and alerts.

The UK freelance workforce is growing fast, and case law from the IR35 reforms, worker‑status claims (Uber, Pimlico Plumbers) and the post‑pandemic shift to project work is reshaping rights every month. Freelancers desperately need accessible legal guidance but cannot afford lawyers for everyday questions. AI summarisation is now good enough for a solo operator to monitor and explain judgments accurately and affordably, opening a market that was previously only served by expensive law-firm newsletters.
