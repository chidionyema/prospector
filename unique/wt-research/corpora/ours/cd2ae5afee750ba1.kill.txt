LessonSuite — the peripatetic music and drama teacher's term-fee collector and exam-prep tracker.

A peripatetic music or drama teacher signs up free, sends parents a fee link, and that link doubles as the pupil practice and exam-prep record.

The starting pain: a UK peripatetic music or drama teacher visits three to seven schools a week and teaches thirty to sixty pupils in individual or small-group lessons. Their biggest unrewarded job is invoicing parents for each ten-lesson term and chasing the parents who do not pay on day one. Most teachers either waste every Sunday evening retyping the same fee demand, or handwave it and absorb the loss. The second biggest pain is tracking the pupil's exam prep: graded exams (ABRSM, Trinity, Rockschool, LAMDA) are twelve to twenty weeks of work with scales, aural tests and pieces measured against a moving syllabus, and today that prep is paper or a forgotten spreadsheet.

LessonSuite attacks both pains with one object: the term-fee invoice link. The teacher signs up in under five minutes, adds a pupil, sets the term fee (about £240 for ten half-hour lessons, which is what UK peripatetic teachers actually charge) and picks the exam board and grade the pupil is working towards. Stripe collects the fee. The same link the parent receives is the place where the pupil ticks off scales, aural and pieces against the syllabus the teacher has loaded, say the ABRSM Grade 5 violin pieces. From the parent's side the link is a portal: pay the term fee, see what to practise, see what your child has practised, see how close they are to the next exam standard.

The growth motion borrows the link-sharing model Calendly and HoneyBook used in the freelance and wedding sectors, and applies it to a UK professional class too small for either of those tools to build for: every fee link is a bespoke, branded link to LessonSuite. Parents see LessonSuite at fee time and again at every practice update. Parents compare notes at the school gates. Other teachers at the same school hear about it on the parents' WhatsApp groups. The wedge that holds them is the records pile: a teacher three terms in has three terms' worth of pupils, fee histories and practice logs inside LessonSuite, plus a parent-facing link that sits in the parent's bookmark bar. Leaving means re-collecting last term's late fees by hand, rebuilding the practice log for every pupil preparing an exam, and dropping the bookmark parents already trust.

Regulator check: ABRSM, Trinity and Rockschool are private exam boards; they do not give fee-collection or practice-log tools, and structurally cannot serve parents on one side of the exam. The Incorporated Society of Musicians is a membership body, not a regulator. The only specific law the tool has to handle is UK GDPR for the pupil data, which the teacher already holds on paper today.

Pricing: free for the first three pupils; £12 a month for up to thirty pupils; £20 a month unlimited. That sits next to the £10 to £15 a month peripatetic teachers already spend on backing-track libraries and theory-test apps for their pupils.

The buyer is a self-employed peripatetic music or drama teacher, often a member of the Incorporated Society of Musicians, taking home £30,000 to £80,000 a year from school visits and private pupils. They already spend on practice-track libraries, theory-test apps and accountancy software, so a tool that stops them chasing late term fees from parents they see once a week is an easy upsell once the first invoice has cleared by link. They pay out of their own pocket, not from a school budget.

ABRSM moved all practical grade theory submissions online from 2020 and now publishes digital results, so pupils and parents expect everything grade-related to be digital. The post-COVID backlog left a wave of pupils sitting mid-grade with no structured prep, which is exactly the admin glue the teacher has been carrying alone. Stripe Link launched in the UK in 2022, so a parent can complete a one-tap invoice payment on phone in seconds. The Incorporated Society of Musicians' 2023 member survey and Music Mark's State of the Nation report both rank admin burden as the second-largest drain on peripatetic teacher morale, after pay. No existing UK tool owns the end-to-end fee-and-prep workflow for this cohort.
