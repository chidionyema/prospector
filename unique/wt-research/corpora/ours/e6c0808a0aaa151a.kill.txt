Model spend audits for AI agency owners.

A fixed-fee audit that rebuilds an agency's client-facing AI features on cheaper model routing, then hands over a signed savings report the agency can bill its own clients for.

Small agencies that build chatbots, content tools and support bots for clients now carry a real monthly model bill. They pass it through to the client, or they eat it inside a retainer. Either way it is the line item that gets questioned first at renewal.

This is a productized service, not software. You take one client-facing feature, instrument every call for two weeks, and rebuild the routing: cheap model first, expensive model only where an evaluation shows quality actually drops. You hand back a report showing cost per conversation before and after, plus the evaluation set that proves quality held.

Rebuilt from the raw pain rather than from how vendors sell it: the agency owner does not want a dashboard, they want a number they can put in front of a client. So the deliverable is a document with a signature on it, priced at a fixed £4,000 to £9,000 per feature, and the agency resells it as their own 'AI efficiency review' at a markup.

What holds this is the evaluation sets. After thirty engagements you own graded test cases for recurring job types — legal summarising, e-commerce support, property listing copy — that let you quote a savings range on a sales call before doing any work. Nobody else accumulates that because model vendors have no reason to prove their expensive model is unnecessary, and observability tools show spend without ever taking responsibility for a quality claim.

UK grounding: the payer is a UK limited company agency filing at Companies House, the work counts as software development for HMRC's R&D relief conversations the agency is already having, and contracts sit under UK law with the agency's own client SLAs.

This is the sells-to-tech shape. Buyer role: the agency owner or delivery director. Recurring trigger: the monthly Anthropic, OpenAI or cloud invoice arriving before a client renewal meeting. Deliverable: a signed before-and-after cost report plus the reimplemented routing. Public source that proves it is already paid for: published per-token price lists from Anthropic and OpenAI, and AWS Bedrock's public pricing pages, all of which show order-of-magnitude gaps between model tiers for the same task.
