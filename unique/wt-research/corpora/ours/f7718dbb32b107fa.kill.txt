HSE Fee for Intervention exposure for UK manufacturers.

Predicts which UK manufacturing sites will trigger HSE Fee for Intervention charges next quarter, so operations managers cut the risk of paying out.

The plain version: an operations manager at a UK factory wants to know whether the next HSE inspector visit will turn into a Fee for Intervention notice. They pay a monthly subscription for a per-site FFI exposure score, ranked against similar UK sites in the same four-digit industry code.

Why it works: FFI is charged by HSE whenever an inspector finds a material breach, at £169 per hour of inspector time. HSE's published statistics show more than £73 million recovered in 2024/25. Sites cannot easily predict when FFI will fire because the inspector chooses when to invoice. The product pulls HSE's published Notices & Prosecutions feed, the RIDDOR dataset on data.gov.uk, and the industry-code geography of every UK premises, then gives a per-site exposure score. The buyer is the operations manager at a UK manufacturer with three to fifteen UK sites - the persona who loses sleep over unexpected ten-thousand-pound FFI bills that never appeared in the budget.

Why it holds: the data is publicly published, but the cross-industry correlation, the threshold scoring and the not-yet-prosecuted infringement signals produce a feed that no regulator and no incumbent EHS vendor currently computes. HSE publishes raw feeds; the operations manager needs the ranked call. The asset compounds each time a new FFI outcome appears in the public feed.
