AI disclosure packs for UK agencies pitching clients.

The fixed-fee AI disclosure section an agency drops into every new client pitch, written for that client's sector.

Every UK marketing or creative agency pitching new work is now asked the same question by the client's procurement team: did you use AI on this, which tools, and what data flowed in. The agency's three current answers are to skip it (and lose on procurement score), to hand over a vague sentence (and lose on assurance), or to pay a consultancy three to eight thousand pounds for a one-off AI clause pack. None of those three answers happen for five hundred pounds.

The agency uploads which AI tools it plans to use on the engagement, the client's industry sector (FCA-regulated, SRA-regulated, NHS, central government, retail consumer), and its existing SOW template. The service returns, in forty-eight hours, a three-section AI disclosure bundle: a one-page client-readable AI statement, a procurement-readable data flow and IP confirmation, and a contract clause that drops into the SOW. The pack is built from a running matrix of UK regulators' current AI positions, updated quarterly as guidance changes.

The reason this lands now is the changing regulatory floor. The ICO's AI and data protection guidance (published in 2024) made AI disclosure mandatory where personal data flows through the model. The Law Society practice note on AI in legal services (late 2024) gave solicitors a model AI clause that the agency's regulated clients are now borrowing. The FCA has run several rounds of supervisory feedback on AI-led financial services through 2024 and is tightening expectations through 2026. Each sector now reads differently on what counts as compliant AI use. Agency owners cannot track all of them, but their clients' procurement teams can and do.

It works as a small-team business because the work is procedurally repeatable. Roughly eighty percent of the pack is filling the regulator-by-sector matrix, twenty percent is bespoke for the specific AI tool combination. There are roughly thirteen thousand UK marketing, design and dev agencies (ONS business demography, 2024). Even capturing fifty agency retainers at three thousand pounds per year each (one-time setup plus quarterly updates) clears one hundred and fifty thousand pounds in recurring revenue.

The wedge is the running cross-sector matrix. Each new engagement exercises a different cell (sector by AI tool category by client size), and the matrix grows more credible as cells fill. A new entrant would spend six months building the catalogue from primary regulator sources and would still need a mechanism to keep it current. Once an agency's packs have cleared thirty or more procurement reviews, the visible track record becomes a second wedge, which a one-off consultancy cannot produce.
