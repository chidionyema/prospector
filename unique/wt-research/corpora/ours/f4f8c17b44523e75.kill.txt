NightGuard Kit — the blackout sleep bundle sold to NHS and emergency-service shift workers, restocked around their rota.

A sleep kit built for people who sleep in daylight, sold to NHS and police night-shift staff as a one-off box plus a small quarterly refill.

This is a physical product brand, not a service. You sell one box: a properly blacked-out window blind that fits standard UK window sizes without drilling, a weighted eye mask sized to fit over ear defenders, a set of moulded ear plugs, a door hanger a family can actually see, and a low-blue bedside light. Then a small refill subscription every three months for the consumable parts — fresh plug tips, replacement blind adhesive strips, and a filter for the light.

Two separate changes made this a business rather than a wish. First, night working in the NHS is not shrinking. NHS England's own workforce statistics show over 1.5 million people employed in NHS England alone, and Section 2 of the NHS Terms and Conditions of Service Handbook pays enhancements of 30% for nights and weekends — meaning a very large, named group works unsocial hours as a permanent condition of the job, not an occasional inconvenience. Second, UK housing stock is getting brighter and hotter to sleep in. Building Regulations Approved Document O, which came into force in June 2022, requires new homes in England to limit overheating, and one of the accepted routes is more openable glazing. The people who buy or rent those homes and sleep in the day are the ones who lose.

The combination is the point. Blackout blinds exist. Eye masks exist. Nobody sells a kit fitted to the specific problem of sleeping between 8am and 3pm in a rented UK flat you are not allowed to drill into, in a household where other people are awake. That is a fitting problem and a sizing problem, not a technology problem.

The part that compounds is fit data. Every order asks for the window's width, drop and reveal depth, the tenancy type, and the shift pattern. After a few thousand orders you know the exact glazing sizes in ex-local-authority blocks in Leeds, in new-build one-beds in Manchester, and in Victorian conversions in south London. That lets you pre-cut stock to the sizes that actually recur, so you ship next day where a made-to-measure blind firm quotes ten working days. A generic blind retailer never asks what shift someone works, so it can never build that pairing of window size to shift pattern.

Running it is light. You buy blind blanks and cut to your own recurring size list, assemble kits in a small unit, and ship by courier. Two or three people can run it to a few thousand orders a month.

Distribution is the unusual asset here, and it is why a small team can hold it. NHS staff buy through workplace benefit portals — Blue Light Card has over 3 million members and lists retailers directly; NHS trusts run their own staff benefit schemes and salary-sacrifice arrangements. Getting listed on those is a relationship, not a budget. Once you are the sleep kit inside a trust's staff benefits page and its night-shift induction pack, a rival has to displace you from a page that is edited once or twice a year by an overworked staff-wellbeing lead.

Night-shift NHS nurses, healthcare assistants, radiographers, paramedics, police and prison officers, aged roughly 25 to 55, buying for themselves. The kit sells at £129 to £189 with a £14 quarterly refill. A secondary buyer is the trust or force itself: staff wellbeing leads and charitable funds buy kits in batches of 50 to 200 as a retention gesture, often paid from NHS Charities Together grants or a trust's own staff lottery fund.

Approved Document O has applied to new homes in England since June 2022, pushing bigger and more openable windows into exactly the flats young shift workers rent. At the same time NHS retention pressure is public and funded — trusts have money set aside specifically for staff wellbeing, and are actively looking for things to spend it on that staff will actually use. Blue Light Card now has more than 3 million members, so a small brand can reach hundreds of thousands of night workers through one listing rather than through paid advertising.
