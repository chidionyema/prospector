AlertBadge — the clip‑on badge that hears a situation turning hostile and keeps a safe record, for NHS and council staff who deal with the public.

A wearable device that listens for aggression in real time and secures audio evidence without you pressing a button.

This is a small, wearable badge (about the size of a name badge) that contains a microphone and a tiny processor running an aggression‑detection model. It constantly analyses the sound around the wearer—tone, volume, speech rate—but only starts recording when it detects that a situation is escalating. The recording is stored locally on the badge and can be downloaded via a phone app if the wearer needs it for a report or complaint. Over time, the device learns from the anonymised data of all users: it can identify early warning signs specific to different settings (a benefits assessment room vs a hospital A&E waiting bay) and even warn the wearer through a subtle vibration before they might consciously notice the threat. This borrows the way credit‑card companies use real‑time fraud detection models that get smarter with each transaction, and applies it to personal safety for a workforce that faces rising verbal abuse and physical threats. The core technical asset is the training dataset of real‑world aggression audios, which grows and improves with every incident flagged by users—rival devices starting from scratch would have a far less accurate model and no community‑driven intelligence. The business ships a physical product (the badge) and an optional cloud‑sync subscription, but the moat is the proprietary data that makes the detection uniquely reliable.

An NHS nurse, a social worker, a parking enforcement officer, or a council benefits assessor—any public‑facing public sector worker whose role involves regular, occasionally volatile interactions with the public. They buy it with their own money because it directly reduces their anxiety and gives them a credible evidence trail if they need to report an incident (which many currently lack).

The Assaults on Emergency Workers (Offences) Act 2018 created a specific offence, but prosecutions need evidence that is rarely available. At the same time, low‑cost microcontrollers and on‑device AI have become powerful enough to run audio analysis on a badge without needing a phone or cloud processing—making a discreet, always‑on safety device technically viable for the first time.
