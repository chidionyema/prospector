The Freelancer's Wage Watch — a weekly video series using California's public wage claim records to expose which companies stiff creatives and how to get paid.

A YouTube channel and subscriber newsletter that digests California’s DIR public wage claim data to name the worst-paying clients and gives freelancers a ready-to-send demand letter.

Freelance creatives in California regularly get stiffed on payment, but they don't have the time or legal knowledge to research a client's payment history. The State of California's Department of Industrial Relations (DIR) collects detailed public data on every wage claim and adjudication, but it sits buried in individual case lookups. This show borrows the model of 'public-shaming journalism' — like Consumer Reports but for freelance pay — to scrape, cross-reference, and rank companies by their number of unpaid wage claims. Each weekly video episode profiles a specific company with the most recent claims, naming real cases and amounts, and walks viewers through a free template to file their own claim. Subscribers get a weekly newsletter with the full 'Deadbeat Client Watchlist' and a fillable demand letter that references the actual DIR case data, making it hard for the company to ignore. The creator builds a proprietary database over time that compounds with each new public claim, creating an asset no individual freelancer or competitor can easily replicate.
