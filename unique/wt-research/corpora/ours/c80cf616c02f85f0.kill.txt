SchoolRun Refund — the tool that checks your child's school trips, meals and club bills for charges the law says are optional.

A subscription tool for parents of children at English state schools that reads school payment app statements and flags charges the school cannot lawfully require.

A working parent with two children at a state school pays through ParentPay, SchoolMoney, Arbor or iPay. Small charges land constantly: £6 for a trip, £45 for a residential deposit, £12 for a music lesson, £3.50 for a workbook, £25 for a branded PE top. Over a year a family can pay several hundred pounds without ever seeing a single total. Nobody sends them one.

Start from the raw problem rather than from how schools bill today. The parent has no ledger, no benchmark, and no idea which of these charges is a request and which is a rule. The tool builds the ledger first. The parent forwards their termly payment emails to a private address, or uploads a statement export. The tool itemises every charge, groups it by child, and shows the running annual total per family.

Then it applies the law. The Education Act 1996 sections 449 to 462 make education during school hours free, and the Department for Education's statutory guidance 'Charging for school activities' says a school cannot charge for anything that takes place in school hours, for materials needed for the curriculum, or for transport to a required activity. Schools may only invite a voluntary contribution, and they may not treat a child differently if the family does not pay. Music tuition has its own narrow exceptions. Residential trips can only be charged for board and lodging, and even that must be waived for families on qualifying benefits. Uniform is separate: the Education (Guidance about Costs of School Uniforms) Act 2021 made the DfE's cost guidance statutory, so schools must keep branded items to a minimum and allow high-street alternatives.

Each charge in the ledger gets one of three plain labels: you must pay this, you can decline this, or this may be unlawful and here is why. Where a charge looks wrong, the tool drafts the letter — first to the school office, then to the governing body under the school's own complaints procedure, then to the Education and Skills Funding Agency for academies or the local authority for maintained schools.

The part rivals cannot copy is the ledger of what real schools actually charge. Every statement uploaded teaches the tool one more school's charging pattern, priced and dated. Within a year the tool can tell a parent in Leeds that their school charges £38 more per term for the same trip than the three nearest schools, and that the wording of the request breaches the voluntary contribution rule. No school, no council and no payment provider can assemble that view, because each of them only sees their own side.

Pricing is £4 a month per family, or £30 a year, with a free first scan that shows the annual total and the count of questionable charges. That total is the hook. Most parents have never seen it.
