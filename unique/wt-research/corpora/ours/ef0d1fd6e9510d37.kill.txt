StandDown Bond — The Owner-Driver HGV Operator's DVSA Roadside Prohibition Income Micro-Bond.

A fixed-premium income bond that pays an owner-driver HGV/PSV operator a daily cash sum for every day their vehicle is off the road under a DVSA roadside 'S-marked' prohibition, funded and priced from the operator's own pre-enrolment inspection record — with payout rising as DVSA enforcement intensity rises.

Lens: invert. The friction an owner-driver feels daily is not the fine — it is the immobilisation: a DVSA examiner issues an immediate prohibition at a roadside check, the truck sits, the contract goes to another driver, and the operator loses £250-£450/day of gross revenue while paying finance on a parked asset. DVSA's own value proposition GROWS as it gets more aggressive: it publishes enforcement data, raises OCRS scores, and escalates to Traffic Commissioner public inquiry — but it structurally CANNOT take the operator's commercial side, cannot indemnify the party it is prohibiting, and cannot pay income to someone it has just penalised. The risk_financing form is native: a solo operator sells a low-face-value, fixed-term income bond (e.g. £120/quarter buys £200/day for up to 10 days, capped, no more than 2 events/year), pre-underwritten by requiring the buyer to submit a structured pre-enrolment self-inspection dossier and monthly walkaround-defect photo log through the operator's own app. That log IS the wedge: after 18 months the operator holds per-vehicle, per-component, per-route defect-to-prohibition conversion data that no insurer, no DVSA dataset, and no broker can obtain, because it is generated as a condition of the bond and covers the pre-failure window nobody else observes. Underwriting gets cheaper every cohort while copiers price blind. Adverse selection is managed structurally, not by capital: waiting period, per-event cap, aggregate annual cap sized so the whole book's maximum loss is a known number the solo can hold as segregated cash, and mandatory logging that self-excludes the worst operators. Claims are settled off the DVSA prohibition notice number plus the clearance record — a document the operator already receives, verifiable, and impossible to fake without fraud. Distribution is owned and free: the defect-log app itself is given away to owner-drivers via truckstop Facebook groups and TruckNet-style forums, and the bond is sold only to users who have logged 60 days — so the funnel and the underwriting dataset are the same artefact.

UK owner-driver HGV operators holding a Restricted or Standard National O-licence with 1-3 vehicles (roughly 26,000 such licence holders), paying a quarterly premium of £110-£140 per vehicle; secondarily, single-coach PSV operators on school-contract work who face the same immobilisation-with-fixed-contract exposure.

DVSA has shifted to intelligence-led targeting with a rising share of checks landing on the highest-OCRS operators, and post-2024 roadside enforcement increasingly triggers immediate S-marked prohibitions tied to operator maintenance systems rather than driver error — meaning the off-road event is now systematically correlated with documented maintenance history, which is exactly what a pre-enrolment logging requirement can price. Simultaneously, driver-shortage-era contract terms give owner-drivers less slack: a 6-day off-road event now loses the contract, not just the week. Finance costs on tractor units have roughly doubled since 2021, so a parked vehicle is a hard monthly cash outflow rather than a soft loss.
