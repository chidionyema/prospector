Direct Payment Uplift Broker – Carer’s Council Negotiation Engine.

A commission‑based service that re‑negotiates inadequate direct payment budgets offered by local authorities to primary carers, securing a higher cash allocation for home care and taking a flat 25% of the first‑year uplift.

Councils systematically under‑fund direct payments for home‑based care packages, relying on carers’ exhaustion and lack of benchmarking data to accept low offers. By inverting the power dynamic, this broker acts as a hostile negotiator on behalf of the carer, armed with a proprietary database of peer‑authority payment rates, statutory care act obligations, and anonymised case outcomes. We don’t fill in a form — we send a legally‑grounded rebuttal package that forces the council to re‑assess, and we handle all correspondence until a revised budget is issued. The carer never pays upfront; our fee is deducted from the first few months of the uplift. The structural moat is proprietary_data: a closed‑loop intelligence pipeline that tracks council under‑payment patterns across dozens of authorities, which no regulator or charity builds because it requires adversarial information extraction.

The primary carer (or the disabled person’s appointee) pays a success fee from the increased direct payment cash — typically 25% of the first year’s extra amount, capped at a maximum absolute figure to ensure fairness.

The post‑2023 squeeze on council budgets has seen a sharp rise in low‑ball direct payment offers, while the statutory duty to meet assessed needs remains unchanged. There is no free ‘broker’ service that monetises the gap between offer and entitlement; free advocacy (e.g. Carers UK helpline) only provides advice, not a transaction. The timing is urgent because many carers accept sub‑standard packages without realising they can challenge them.
