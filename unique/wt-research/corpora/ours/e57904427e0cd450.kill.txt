GustGuard — daily income for self-employed tree surgeons when the Met Office records gusts above 28 mph in your local area.

A parametric income bond that pays a fixed daily amount when high winds make it unsafe to climb, verified automatically from Met Office station data.

Self-employed tree surgeons lose whole working days when wind gusts are too strong to climb safely, but still carry vehicle, kit and yard overheads. Existing weather insurance is either too broad, too expensive or requires slow claims adjustment, so most absorb the loss. This product borrows the way crop insurers pay out against a published weather index: it uses local Met Office weather station data to trigger a payment the moment gusts exceed 28 mph for two consecutive hours on a standard working day. A solo operator sets a fixed daily benefit, prices it from historical wind-loss patterns in each station’s area, and sells it directly to arborists through trade forums and Facebook groups. The whole system runs on a web app that checks Met Office open data feeds, emails the payout and transfers the money within 24 hours.

Individual self-employed tree surgeons, aged 30–55, who work alone or with one groundie and operate in the domestic and light commercial market. They buy a monthly subscription (roughly £12–£25 depending on postcode exposure) knowing a payout will land without any paperwork the day after a lost day.

More tree surgeons are going self-employed after Brexit and council contract changes, and extreme weather events driven by climate change are increasing the number of unworkable windy days across southern and coastal Britain. Meanwhile the Met Office has made its weather station data freely accessible via APIs, making automated parametric payouts simple to build.
