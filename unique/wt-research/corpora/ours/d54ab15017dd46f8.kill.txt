DescriptorDeck — The Carer's PIP Award Defence Workbench.

A vertical tool for primary carers that converts the DWP PIP2 form into a descriptor-by-descriptor evidence-builder, scoring each response against a proprietary database of HMCTS tribunal decisions and exporting a tribunal-ready bundle the carer submits themselves.

Primary carers lose PIP claims not because the conditions fail to qualify, but because they fill the form as a description of difficulty rather than as legal evidence addressing each of the 12 DWP descriptors with the specific functional facts the tribunal has historically rewarded. A vertical tool that ingests the carer's PIP2 draft, scores each descriptor 0–5 against proprietary tribunal-precedent data, prompts for the missing functional facts (e.g. 'can you reliably prepare a cooked meal — yes/no, with what aid, on how many days, with what consequence if you stop'), and exports a tribunal bundle on submission, will materially improve award rates and become the de facto workbench for self-representing carers. (Lens: narrow + first-principles — drilling to one form, rebuilding from the raw adjudicator's reasoning, not from how benefits advice is sold today.).

Primary carer of a disabled adult or child in the UK, facing a PIP initial claim, mandatory reconsideration, or tribunal hearing. Typical household income £20–50k, often has just received a DWP decision letter cutting or refusing PIP, currently Googling 'how to win a PIP tribunal' and unable to afford the £200–400/hr legal advisers. Pays a one-off £39–79 for the workbench and a £19 tribunal bundle export.

2024–2025 PIP review caseload is the highest on record; ~35% of awards are being cut at mandatory review despite unchanged conditions; tribunal success rate is ~70% but only ~10% of refusals are appealed because the bundle/process feels impossible; tribunal wait times exceed 6 months in many regions, meaning the disabled person has no PIP for half a year while the carer's respite hours vanish. Carers need a self-serve workbench, not a paid hand-holder — and DWP has just tightened the evidence-gathering rules, raising the floor on what a 'good' form must contain.
