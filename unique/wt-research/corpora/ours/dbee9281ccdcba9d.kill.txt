BookBond — per-quote cover that pays a sole-trader tradesperson if the customer cancels, no-shows or disappears after the job is booked.

BookBond pays a sole-trader tradesperson 90% of an accepted quote within 48 hours if the customer cancels, no-shows or disappears after the job is booked.

BookBond is a per-quote insurance product for sole-trader UK tradespeople. When the tradesperson sends a quote and the customer accepts, BookBond attaches a premium of 1.5% of the quote value, capped at £150, that pays out within 48 hours if the customer cancels, no-shows, ghosts or becomes insolvent before the work completes.

Today the loss falls entirely on the tradesperson. They prepay materials, block a week on the calendar and chase a small-claims action that takes months and may never pay. UK self-employed trade surveys in 2024 show three to five jobs a year lost to walk-aways for the average sole trader — £6,000 to £15,000 of annual revenue never recovered. The standard fallbacks (Public Liability cover or a personal buffer fund) cover neither walkaway nor ghosting.

BookBond transplants the rules that run event-cancellation cover, the £4bn UK market that pays promoters and venues the moment a key performer falls through, and applies them to the trade-customer cancellation. Cover fires automatically on three openly published UK feeds: an insolvency notice in The Gazette, a Land Registry Price Paid record showing the customer's house sale has fallen through, or a consented credit-file check on the customer taken at quote acceptance. The customer signs the credit-check consent when they accept the quote, so the permission is in place before the risk attaches, and no human loss adjuster is needed for the payout.

Each accepted quote with BookBond adds to a private dataset of cancellation rates by trade, region and season — data no UK insurer currently holds in one schema. After two years the book is the only source of UK-priced cancellation risk for a £5,000 kitchen fit, an £8,000 heat-pump install or a £3,000 bathroom refit. FCA intermediary authorisation legally gates competitor entry, and once BookBond is embedded into the leading UK trades quoting software (MyJobQuote, PriceRight, Quickscout, Checkatrade Connect) it becomes a switching cost: ripping it out means turning off a revenue stream and re-keying every quote template.

The product launches into a tailwind. UK small-trades quote volumes on Checkatrade, MyJobQuote and Bark have hit record highs through 2024-25, and UK personal insolvencies have nearly doubled between 2020 and 2024 (Insolvency Service quarterly statistics, Q4 2024). Distribution happens at the moment of the quote, with no human underwriter touching the binding — pricing, trigger validation, claim submission and a Faster Payments payout all run on APIs.

The sole-trader tradesperson is the policyholder and the recipient of any payout. They pay nothing until cover attaches; at quote acceptance they toggle BookBond on at 1.5% of the quote value, capped at £150, normally passed to the customer as a transparent line item. The customer can decline the cover, in which case the tradesperson proceeds unprotected. Real buyers are self-employed gas engineers booked three weeks out, kitchen fitters taking £5,000 jobs, electricians on homeowner retrofits — trades where a single walkaway has the biggest income impact.

UK personal insolvencies nearly doubled from 2020 to 2024 (Insolvency Service), and customer walk-away has become a top-three cause of lost revenue in UK sole-trader trade income surveys. No insurer serves this gap. Lloyd's syndicates have ruled out B2C walkaway cover as too small-ticket, and mainstream brokers (Marsh, Aon) won't issue sub-£250 policies. The product is launchable today because Land Registry Price Paid and Gazette feeds are openly published, FCA intermediary authorisation for a non-life broker is achievable in roughly six months, and software distribution is via the established UK trades CRM partners.
