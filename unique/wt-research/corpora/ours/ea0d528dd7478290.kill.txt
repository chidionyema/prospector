AI decision logs for Illinois tech agency disputes.

Fixed-fee service that produces a court-reporter-style verbatim record of what an AI tool actually did during a project, for use in Illinois client disputes.

The agency owner runs an AI deployment agency in Illinois. A client disputes the AI's behavior — hallucination, bias, inaccuracy, IP infringement. The client demands proof of what the AI actually did. The agency has technical logs but needs a legally-formatted, neutral record that meets Illinois discovery standards.

This borrows the court reporter model and applies it to AI behavior. For each dispute, the service produces a fixed-fee 'Decision Log' — a court-reporter-style verbatim record of the AI's behavior at issue, formatted to meet Illinois Supreme Court Rule 201 discovery standards. The log includes timestamps, inputs, outputs, model versions, and human-readable interpretations, all under evidentiary seal if needed.

This commits to shape (A) — an AI-native service where the model does work a named human role (court reporter) is paid for today. The court reporter role is well established; the AI replaces the verbatim transcription and interpretation work that court reporters do for legal proceedings.

The wedge is the technical methodology for converting raw AI logs into legally-formatted, neutral documentation. This is hard to replicate. The AI vendor's own logs are technical and biased by self-interest; clients want third-party documentation that meets Illinois discovery standards and can be admitted as evidence. The service accumulates proprietary data on common AI dispute patterns, judicial expectations, and successful documentation across many cases.

The trigger is each client dispute or audit. The deliverable is a fixed-fee decision log document (20 to 50 pages). The price is $5,000 to $15,000 per decision log.

Illinois-based AI deployment agency owners in active client disputes. The agency owner has direct budget authority and is the named party to the dispute; the agency's clients see the decision log as evidence.
