BadgeBack — The Primary Carer's Blue Badge Refusal-Reversal & Mobility-Evidence Broker.

A fixed-fee transaction broker (£45 upfront + £50 on issue) that reverses council Blue Badge refusals for carers of children and adults with hidden disabilities, using an FOI-sourced dataset of each council's scoring rubric, refusal rates, mobility-assessor patterns and LGSCO precedents to build the exact evidence dossier that flips the decision.

Lens: invert. There is NO statutory right of appeal against a Blue Badge refusal — the only routes are asking the same council to reconsider or a slow LGSCO complaint — so as cash-strapped councils (post-s114 austerity) tighten mobility assessments and refusal rates climb, the value of an adversarial navigator GROWS with enforcement intensity. The council is structurally barred from capturing this value: it cannot coach applicants to defeat its own scoring rubric, cannot take the applicant's side against its own assessor, and cannot indemnify a reapplication. A solo operator runs pure transaction brokerage: intake questionnaire → AI-drafted, council-specific evidence dossier (walking-distance phrasing, hidden-disability descriptors that map to that council's rubric, GP/OT letter templates, LGSCO citations) → files the reconsideration on the carer's behalf → escalates to LGSCO if refused. Every case compounds the per-council outcome dataset (which assessor arguments succeed where), the wedge no reseller of generic advice can copy — the same FOI-sourced-outcomes mechanic that made the School Appeal Dossier Engine pass. Distribution is owned/free: SEND-parent and dementia-carer Facebook groups, where refused-badge threads recur daily.

UK primary carers of children with autism/SEND or elderly parents with dementia or limited mobility whose Blue Badge application (or renewal) was refused after the 2019 hidden-disability expansion — a specifically nameable, acutely motivated payer for whom the badge is worth hundreds of pounds a year in parking and, more importantly, the difference between leaving the house with the cared-for person or not.

The 2019 extension of Blue Badge eligibility to non-visible conditions caused an application surge just as council finances collapsed (multiple Section 114 notices, 2023–2026); councils responded by tightening desk-based mobility assessments, and refusal rates for hidden-disability applications have risen sharply — while the absence of any statutory appeal panel leaves a structural navigation vacuum that no regulator can fill because the refusing body IS the deciding body.
