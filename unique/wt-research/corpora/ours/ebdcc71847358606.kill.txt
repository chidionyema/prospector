Nudge Letter Fix, cuts your Airbnb tax bill HMRC guessed.

For people renting a spare room, driveway or holiday let who get an HMRC letter built from Airbnb or Booking.com data, a fixed-fee service recalculates what's actually owed and drafts the reply.

Since January 2024, UK rules called DAC7 force platforms like Airbnb, Booking.com and Vrbo to hand HMRC each host's gross booking income every year. HMRC now runs 'one-to-many' nudge letter campaigns straight off that data, and the first big wave landed through 2025 and is still running in 2026. The letters use the platform's gross figure, not the taxpayer's real position, so they routinely ignore Rent-a-Room relief (£7,500 tax-free for a room in your own home), the £1,000 property income allowance, jointly-owned splits between a partner, and the transitional mess left by the Furnished Holiday Lettings regime being abolished from April 2025. A mortgage-holding professional who lets out a room or a static caravan for extra income opens one of these letters and sees a number that looks like a tax demand, when their real liability might be a fraction of it, or nothing at all.

This is a fixed-fee, one-person service: send over your nudge letter and your platform payout history, and get back a worked reconciliation showing the true taxable figure against every relief that applies to your ownership structure, plus a drafted response letter to HMRC. It's built once as a repeatable method and templated response library, then delivered by video call plus a short written pack, so one person can run dozens of cases a month without scaling headcount.

The part that inverts the usual compliance-tool shape: HMRC's own guidance and its 'check if you need to tell us' tool can explain the rules, but it cannot take your side in a dispute with itself, cannot advocate for the specific relief mix that applies to a joint-owner with a spare room and an FHL wind-down, and has no incentive to make your letter smaller. Every fresh nudge-letter wave HMRC sends out, using its now-annual DAC7 data feed, makes this service worth more, not less, because the case library of resolved reconciliations for each relief combination gets faster to reuse next time. That's the opposite of most compliance products, which get less useful as rules settle down.

A professional aged 35-55 with a mortgage and kids who lets a spare room or a small holiday let on the side, and has just opened an HMRC letter that estimates tax owed from their Airbnb or Booking.com payout data. They pay a fixed fee, once, to make the number right and get a defensible reply drafted, because the alternative is either overpaying out of panic or ignoring a letter from HMRC.
