The Carer’s Room‑for‑Care Exchange.

A solo broker who matches primary carers needing affordable in‑home help with live‑in caregivers seeking zero‑rent accommodation in exchange for reduced hourly rates, collecting a placement fee from the carer or the care agency.

Invert the typical carer expense: instead of paying the full market rate for care, the carer offers an empty bedroom in return for a steep discount from a caregiver. The broker screens both parties, formalises the barter-plus-cash arrangement, and ensures it complies with local live‑in caregiver regulations. The broker’s proprietary base of pre‑vetted caregivers and co‑living contracts creates a switching cost that generic platforms cannot match.

The carer pays a one‑time placement fee (or the care agency pays a finder’s fee for placing its employee).

Carer fatigue, rising care costs, and an undersupply of affordable housing for frontline care workers create a mutual desperation that a formalised co‑living match can relieve.

One passage states that when a carer lives with the person they support, accommodation is usually already included in the employment package, with a private bedroom provided [c4060c04d7f39bff] — so a free room is the normal baseline for live-in care in this market, not an extra the family can trade for a steeply reduced hourly rate, which removes the discount the whole model depends on.
