BorderCare Split — The Cross-Border Primary Carer's Dual-State Care-Cost Attribution & Recovery Broker.

A fixed-fee transaction broker that reconstructs which of two jurisdictions is legally liable for a dependent's care, treatment or benefit costs — then recovers the wrongly-paid share from the losing authority on behalf of the carer who fronted the money.

Lens: invert. The friction: a primary carer whose dependent's life straddles two jurisdictions (an elderly parent who retired to Spain or Ireland and returns for treatment; a disabled adult child placed in a care setting in a different UK nation or a different local authority's area; a carer who moved counties mid-claim) pays out of pocket for care, equipment, transport or top-up fees while two authorities each insist the other is liable. Each authority has a statutory duty and a free first-party process — but each is a COUNTERPARTY to the other, and neither will ever build the tool that argues its own liability away. That is the structural inversion: as ordinary-residence disputes, cross-border healthcare reimbursement (S2/GHIC pathways), and inter-authority recoupment rules get MORE aggressive, the volume of stranded carer-funded spend grows, and the duty-imposers are precisely the parties who cannot serve the carer adversarially. The broker takes the carer's commercial side: it reconstructs the residence/liability timeline from tenancy, utility, GP-registration, flight and care-invoice records, maps it against each jurisdiction's own published ordinary-residence and reimbursement tests, files the determination request against the WEAKER authority's position, and recovers the carer's fronted cash for a fixed per-recovery fee plus a per-authority filing fee. The compounding asset is a proprietary dataset of which specific authority pairs concede at which stage, which caseworker evidence formats trigger a determination versus a refusal, and the actual paid-out amounts — data neither authority publishes and rivals cannot reconstruct without having brokered the disputes.

Primary carers who have personally fronted £2k–£40k of care, top-up, equipment or treatment cost for a dependent whose residence spans two jurisdictions — specifically: UK carers of parents who retired to Spain, Ireland, Portugal or Cyprus and returned for treatment; carers of adult disabled children placed out-of-area or across the England/Wales/Scotland/NI border; and carers who relocated mid-package. Fixed fee £180 per authority determination filed plus £450 on successful recovery. Secondary payer: private care-home operators and specialist supported-living providers holding unpaid cross-border top-up debt who will pay the same fixed fee to have liability determined rather than pursue the family.

Post-Brexit reciprocal-care pathways (S1/S2/GHIC) now sit alongside pre-2021 legacy entitlements, so the same family can have two different bases of claim depending on when the parent moved — and authorities routinely default to refusal. Simultaneously, adult-social-care funding pressure has made inter-authority ordinary-residence disputes and out-of-area recoupment fights markedly more contested, and devolved divergence between the four UK nations' charging and continuing-care rules has widened. Rising numbers of carers are relocating for affordability, creating mid-package jurisdiction changes. Every one of these trends increases the stranded, carer-funded amount — and none of the duty-imposing bodies can build the adversarial recovery tool because each is the party being recovered from.
