LevyLink — the apprenticeship levy API that lets NHS trusts and councils spend their unused training fund on their own staff before HMRC takes it back.

An API that matches NHS trusts and councils with training providers who can deliver apprenticeship qualifications from the trust's unused Apprenticeship Levy, with the Government's paperwork handled automatically.

Each year, every NHS trust in England pays an Apprenticeship Levy worth up to about £2 million based on its pay bill. Councils and large academy trusts pay similar amounts. If the levy is not spent on approved apprenticeship training within 24 months, HMRC claws it back. The Department for Education has reported more than £200 million of levy written off across UK employers in recent years, and NHS trusts sit on some of the largest unspent pots.

Inside each trust, the person responsible is usually a single workforce development lead, often a former nurse or manager, working without a dedicated system. They know the money is being lost. They know staff want qualifications. But the digital process, from the Apprenticeship Service account, to provider approval, to learner progress recording, to levy transfer rules, is paper-heavy, slow, and built for HR teams many times the size of a single trust's apprenticeship function.

LevyLink is the API that sits between an NHS trust's HR system and the Government's Apprenticeship Service. It tells the trust which of their staff are eligible for which apprenticeship standards, finds an approved provider who can deliver at a date that works, files the digital paperwork for the spend or transfer, and tracks each learner until completion. Pricing is a percentage of the levy that would otherwise have expired, so the trust pays only when LevyLink successfully spends money that would have been clawed back.

This borrows the way last-minute travel apps fill empty airline seats before departure, and applies it to the perishable inventory of unused Apprenticeship Levy that trusts cannot manage alone. It also fuses the Apprenticeship Levy expiry problem with the separate trend toward shorter, stackable, skills-based micro-credentials, so trusts can spend their pot on six or nine-month workforce programmes that fit modern shift patterns rather than the old 18 to 24 month degrees.

The compounding asset is the network of providers and trusts already wired up. A training provider joins once to be visible to every trust in a region. A trust joins once to publish a single intake and be visible to every provider who can deliver it. New trusts joining later see a full marketplace from day one, which is why the first ten trust integrations matter most.

Workforce development leads, heads of learning and development, and HR directors in NHS trusts (around 215 in England), unitary and county councils, and large multi-academy trusts. They have a named budget for apprenticeship spend, a personal pain around year-end write-offs, and accountability for the workforce plan the trust files with NHS England or the Department for Education.

The April 2024 Apprenticeship Levy reforms, the September 2024 launch of Skills England, and the £200m-plus written off in the most recent reporting year all converge on the next 24 months as a hard window where trusts and councils must act or lose their pot. The new Growth and Skills Levy from 2025 will widen what the money can be spent on, but any unspent balance is still at risk of expiry under both the old and new rules.
