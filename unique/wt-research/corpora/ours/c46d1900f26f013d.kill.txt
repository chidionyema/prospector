Rig Rescue, spare test kit for firms whose gear is stuck.

A subscription that ships a startup a calibrated replacement instrument within 24 hours when its own test equipment goes off to be calibrated or breaks.

Hardware startups building anything electrical own a small pile of expensive test gear: signal generators, spectrum analysers, EMC pre-compliance kit, safety testers. UKAS-accredited calibration labs typically hold that gear for one to three weeks. During those weeks the startup's test bench is dark, and so is its release schedule.

This borrows a practice that is normal in commercial aviation, where an airline never waits for a part to come back from overhaul because a serviceable spare rotates in from a pool. Nobody runs that rotation for small electronics firms. The service keeps a small fleet of the twenty or so instruments that show up again and again in UK hardware startups, all under live calibration certificates, and rotates one in the moment yours goes out.

The operator is a two-to-six person outfit: a stores and logistics lead, a technician who checks the certificates and does the swap, and someone selling. Revenue is a monthly retainer of roughly £250 to £900 per firm depending on how much of the bench is covered, plus a per-swap fee. Delivery cost is a courier run and the depreciation on owned instruments that hold their value well on the used market.

What compounds is the swap record. After a couple of hundred rotations you know which instruments fail, which labs actually turn round in the time they quote, and what a given startup's bench looks like at each stage of development. That record becomes the buying guide the customer asks for before they purchase anything, and it is why the retainer renews.

It holds because ripping the service out means the firm has to buy duplicate instruments outright, re-plan its own calibration diary, and absorb the dead weeks again. Once the swap is scheduled into the release calendar, the cost of leaving is the thing that keeps them.
