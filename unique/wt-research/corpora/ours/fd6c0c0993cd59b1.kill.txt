KerbRound — The Primary Carer's Multi-Borough PCN Cancellation & Carer-Permit Stack Broker.

A no-cancel-no-fee transaction broker that kills the parking, bus-lane and moving-traffic penalties a family carer racks up on their daily care round, then re-plans the round onto a stack of discretionary carer/health-worker permits and low-enforcement kerb space — priced per PCN cancelled and per permit granted.

Lens: invert (plus a jurisdictional-arbitrage kicker). A primary carer visiting a parent two or three times a day is structurally a repeat traffic offender: they stop on double yellows to get someone off a commode, they cross a camera-enforced box junction or bus gate on the same route every single day, and they cannot legally park at the door because the exemption regime is discretionary and undocumented. The duty-imposer here is the council — and the council is the one body that structurally CANNOT build this: it is the issuing authority, its free 'challenge' form is deliberately ground-agnostic, and the adjudicator (London Tribunals / the Traffic Penalty Tribunal) is statutorily impartial and may not advocate for the motorist. The asset compounds precisely as enforcement gets MORE aggressive: every new camera site, every new bus gate, every LTN generates a fresh cluster of contraventions whose per-site cancellation behaviour is only visible to someone aggregating published adjudicator decisions, informal-challenge outcomes and FOI returns on cancellation-by-ground, site by site, code by code. The arbitrage: the SAME carer doing the SAME journey is exempt in one borough and penalised in the next, because carer permits, health-worker permits and 'attending a person receiving care' discretion are set per-authority with no cross-authority index. KerbRound brokers three transactions: (1) representations and appeals on live PCNs at a fixed fee only on cancellation; (2) a one-off Round Survey that stacks every discretionary permit the carer qualifies for across the authorities their round crosses; (3) an annual re-file as camera estates and permit rules change. Solo-operable end to end: representations go in via council portals, traffic-penalty work is neither a reserved legal activity nor inside the FCA claims-management perimeter, and the drafting is templated against the outcome dataset. NOT a compliance dashboard, not an appeal-template site — the product is the cancelled penalty and the granted permit, and the moat is a per-site enforcement-behaviour dataset that grows every time a council switches a new camera on. HYPOTHESIS to verify before build: sample 200 published adjudicator decisions and 20 FOI returns to confirm cancellation rates vary materially by site and ground, and confirm at least 15 UK authorities operate a discretionary carer or health-worker permit with unpublished criteria.

Unpaid or low-paid primary carers running a multi-visit daily round by car across two or more enforcement authorities — concretely: the London-borough carer visiting a parent with dementia twice daily who is holding three or more open PCNs; and Carers UK / carers'-centre members in metro authorities (Birmingham, Manchester, Bristol, Cardiff, Glasgow) that have switched on moving-traffic cameras since 2022. Secondary payer: the adult child living 20-40 minutes away who funds the parent's care and settles the fines. Price: £29 per PCN cancelled (nothing if it stands, versus a £65-£130 discounted penalty avoided), £120 for the Round Survey and permit-stack filing, £45 annual re-file.

English councils outside London gained moving-traffic (Part 6 Traffic Management Act 2004) enforcement powers from mid-2022 and have been commissioning camera sites in waves ever since, so the volume of camera-issued PCNs against fixed daily routes is rising sharply rather than randomly; council penalty income is at record levels and under sustained press and audit scrutiny, which raises cancellation pressure on badly-signed sites; School Streets, bus gates and LTNs have multiplied the number of timed restrictions a care round crosses; and carer/health-worker permit schemes remain per-authority discretionary with no national index, so the arbitrage gap between neighbouring authorities is at its widest just as the penalty volume peaks.
