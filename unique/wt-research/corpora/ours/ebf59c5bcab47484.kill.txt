Cue sheet packs for AI music startups.

For UK AI music startups that need a PRS or PPL cue sheet for each broadcast placement, this fills the rights columns AI music breaks and files the form.

PRS and PPL have run a cue sheet workflow for decades — every broadcast cue sheet, filed with the broadcaster, paid out to composers and performers through the two societies. This borrows that established workflow and applies it to AI music, where the rights columns in the cue sheet now break because the composer is an AI tool rather than a named person.

UK broadcasters — the BBC, ITV, Channel 4, Sky, and radio groups like Global and Bauer — must report every piece of music they use to the two UK collecting societies (PRS for Music and PPL). They get that report from a cue sheet that the music supplier files with each broadcast: track title, composer, publisher, duration, usage type. PRS publishes the cue sheet standard and broadcasters hand the template to whoever supplies the music.

For human-composed music this is routine. For AI-composed music the rights columns are now unclear: is the composer the AI tool, the prompt engineer who shaped the output, the underlying training-data rights-holder, or the platform itself? PRS for Music and PPL have both published AI music guidance, and UK broadcasters including the BBC have updated their commissioning music terms to require AI rights disclosure in cue sheets.

This service compiles the per-broadcaster cue sheet template, lets the AI music startup declare its rights split (AI tool, prompt engineer, training data lineage), generates a cue sheet that the broadcaster's commissioning music team will accept, and submits it.
