# Kill-log dash normalisation

**Status:** proposed
**Scope:** `tools/make_kill_log.py`, `tests/unit/test_kill_log_dash_normalization.py`, regenerated `store_platform/src/Store.Web/src/data/kill-log.json`

---

## Problem

The kill-log page on `mumchimp.com/kill-log` displays 60 published entries sourced from
`store_platform/src/Store.Web/src/data/kill-log.json`. The text in those entries is
generated by the engine's LLM at two stages — candidate generation (title, oneLiner) and
verdict reasoning (reason) — and the prose is heavily decorated with em-dashes (`—`) and
en-dashes (`–`).

Counts across the current 60 entries (52,782 chars total):

- em-dashes: 156
- en-dashes: 24
- double-hyphens: 0

That is ~3.4 AI-tell dashes per 1,000 characters of rendered text. Two patterns account
for almost all of them:

1. **Title separator.** `"AssessAid — the carer's statutory assessment evidence dossier
   builder"` — every published title pairs the brand name with a dash-separated
   description.
2. **Hortatory list opener.** `"…the UK leisure-centre slip-testing space is already
   served by multiple established nationwide providers — National Testing, UKAS ISO 17025
   pendulum testers, and Slip Safety…"` — em-dash introducing a list of incumbents.

Em-dashes are the most universally recognised AI writing signature; the kill-log page is
the most-read piece of evidence on the storefront, and it is supposed to read like a
human analyst's rejection note, not a model output. The same `tools/make_sample_report.py`
already uses a `nodash()` helper to strip them from the free sample report — the kill-log
is the outlier.

---

## Fix

Apply the same `nodash()` pattern to the kill-log fields at publish time. The post-process
sits in `tools/make_kill_log.py` so the underlying dossiers, the engine's verdicts, and
the candidate generator are untouched. The moat and the source-or-die invariant are
preserved: factual claims and citation hashes are carried through unchanged; only the
cosmetic punctuation is rewritten.

Substitution rules:

- `—` (em-dash) → `, ` (comma + space)
- `–` (en-dash) → `, ` (comma + space)
- ` - ` (hyphen surrounded by whitespace) → `, ` (so isolated punctuation dashes go too)
- Compound words like `out-of-hours`, `slip-resistance`, `poolside-walkways` are preserved
  because the regex only matches dashes surrounded by whitespace.
- Multiple internal whitespace → single space.

## Acceptance

- `tests/unit/test_kill_log_dash_normalization.py` is green.
- Full pytest suite (`pytest -q`) is green.
- Re-baked `kill-log.json`: zero em-dashes and zero en-dashes across all rendered fields
  (title, oneLiner, reason) in every entry.
- No entry's `reason` loses any of: `National Testing`, `UKAS ISO 17025`, `leisure
  centres`, or any other factual phrase that was present before.
- Compound words (`out-of-hours`, `slip-resistance`, `poolside-walkways`) are preserved
  in every entry.
- Citation URLs in `citations[]` are unchanged.

---

## Why this is not a moat change

The strip happens at publish time, after the engine has written the dossier. The
underlying `reason` text and the candidate's `title` / `one_liner` remain in
`store/dossiers/*.json` untouched. A future re-evaluation, the golden-set regression, and
the moat itself all see the original verdicts; only the rendered page is rewritten. This
is the same architectural pattern the sample report already uses.

---

## Out of scope

- The candidate generator prompt and the verdict prompt are not changed. Future content
  will still be written with em-dashes by the model; the post-processor catches them at
  publish time. Tightening the prompt would be a separate, larger change that crosses
  into the moat.
- The home-page copy (`pages/index.tsx`) and the marketing pages still use em-dashes in
  rendered prose. They are not part of this task.
