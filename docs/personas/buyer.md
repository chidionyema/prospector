**What this is.** A plain description of the product, written so you can decide whether it is worth
your money before you spend any.

**Read this if** you are thinking about buying a pack, or you have bought one and want to know
exactly what you are holding.

**The one-sentence answer.** You are buying a research file on one specific business idea: what it
is, who would pay for it, who is already doing it, what the numbers look like, what would kill it,
and a source you can click for every factual claim in it.

## 2. The checks

Every idea is put through a set of checks before it can be sold. Each check is answered only from
sources found on the open web at the time. If no source could be found, the answer is **"could not
verify"** — never a guess.

Here they are in plain words.

### 2.1 Is the pain real?

**Passes when:** someone has publicly described this problem, in their own words, in a way that
shows it costs them time or money.

**Fails when:** the only evidence is a market-size report, or nobody complains about it anywhere.

**What a "could not verify" means:** the problem might be real and simply not discussed online.
That is common in trades and in business-to-business work.

### 2.2 Does the value last?

**Passes when:** the thing you would sell does not become worthless in a year — the need recurs,
or the work compounds.

**Fails when:** it is a one-off fix for a rule that is about to change, or a wrapper around
something a large company will ship for free.

### 2.3 Who is already doing this?

**Passes when:** competitors exist and are named, and there is a gap they are not covering.

**Fails when:** a well-funded incumbent already owns the segment and the idea offers nothing they
do not.

**Note:** "no competitors found" is not a pass. It usually means no market.

### 2.4 Can the buyer actually pay?

**Passes when:** the people named as buyers have money and a budget line this would come from,
with a source.

**Fails when:** the buyer is broke, or the budget belongs to a department that has to fight for it.

### 2.5 Can you reach them?

**Passes when:** there is a named, specific route — an association, a trade body, an expo, a
directory, a public register.

**Fails when:** the answer is "social media" or "content marketing" with nothing specific behind
it.

### 2.6 Is it legal?

**Passes when:** the regulatory position is clear enough to act on, with a citation.

**Fails when:** it requires a licence you are not going to get, or it is prohibited.

**"Could not verify" is common here** and it is honest. Regulation is often not written down in a
form a search can find. Take it as "check this yourself before you spend money", not as "it is
fine".

### 2.7 What do people already pay for things like this?

This one is different. **It can never kill an idea.** It looks for real prices, published on the
open web, that buyers already pay for comparable work. Every price quoted must appear word for word
in the page it cites.

If no price page exists, that is a fact about the internet, not a fact about the idea. So it is
recorded and moved past.

### 2.8 What the real pack scored

The example pack above was checked eight times. Six came back supported. **Two came back "could
not verify":** whether the buyers can pay, and the legal position.

That is printed in the pack. It is not hidden, softened, or rounded up to a pass. If you are going
to start this business, those are the two things you check first, with your own money on the line.

## 4. What this will not do

This is the honest list. It is here because the product is only worth buying if you know its
limits.

**It does not tell you the idea will work.** It tells you the idea survived a set of checks. A
business that survives research still fails for reasons research cannot see.

**It grades the idea, not you.** Nothing in a pack knows your skills, your capital, your city, your
free hours, or your appetite for risk. Two people buying the same pack are not in the same
position, and the pack cannot tell you which of you should proceed.

**It does not do the work.** There is a build specification and a first-week checklist. Following
them is your job, and it is most of the job.

**It is a snapshot, not a subscription.** Each pack shows the date it was verified. The example
above says 14 August 2026. Sources go stale. A competitor named in "the field" may have shut down
or raised money since.

**Some checks come back unverified.** See §2.8. Six of eight supported, two unverified, is a
typical result — not a bad one. A pack where everything came back supported would be more
suspicious, not less.

**It is not advice.** It is research with sources attached. Legal, tax and regulatory questions in
particular are flagged for you to check, not answered for you.

**It will not tell you nobody else bought it.** The same pack can be sold more than once. If you
need exclusivity, this is the wrong product.

**The numbers are modelled, not observed.** The financial model in section 4 of a pack is built
from stated assumptions. Every one of those assumptions is listed in `Assumptions.csv` so you can
disagree with them individually. Nobody has run this business and reported back.

The page will tell you it timed out. **That does not mean your money is gone.** It means the order
had not finished being set up within 24 seconds. Contact support with the web address of that page
— it contains the session reference that finds your order.

### 6.2 About the download link

When you click download, you are sent to a temporary address that works for **five minutes**. That
is deliberate: a link that lasts forever is a link anyone can forward.

If you copy that temporary address and try it an hour later, it will fail. **Reopen your permanent
link and click download again.** A fresh address is made every time.

### 6.3 How many times can you download it?

Up to **50 times** per purchase. That is generous on purpose, so re-downloading on a new laptop or
a phone never runs you out. If you hit it, contact support and it can be reissued.

### 6.4 If you buy the same pack again later

You will get the version you paid for, both times. Packs are stored so that each purchase is

## 7. How to check a claim yourself

This is the part worth doing before you spend real money on any idea in a pack.

**Every factual claim in a pack carries a source.** Section 13, "Evidence and constraints", lists
them. Individual claims cite them inline.

Here is how to test a pack in fifteen minutes:

1. **Open section 13** and count the sources. The example pack has 41.
2. **Pick three claims that the idea depends on.** Not decorative facts — the load-bearing ones.
   Usually: who the buyer is, what they currently pay, and what the rule or regulation is.
3. **Click through to each source.** Read the actual page, not the pack's summary of it.
4. **Ask whether the source says what the pack says it says.** This is the whole test. A source
   that exists but does not support the claim is the failure mode to look for.
5. **Check the date on each source.** A 2019 page about a 2026 market is a weak source even when
   it is a real one.
6. **Read section 14, the check report, before section 1.** It tells you which checks came back
   unverified. Those are the parts nobody stood behind.

**If a source does not load**, that is worth reporting. Links are checked before a pack goes on
sale, but the web rots.

### 7.1 What a good pack looks like when you test it

- Sources are primary: a regulator, a register, a court record, a trade body, a named company's own
  page.
- Named companies are real and findable.
- Prices quoted appear word for word on the page cited.
- The "what would sink this" section names a specific, plausible failure — not a generic risk.
- The 30-day test is something you could genuinely do in 30 days for a small amount of money.

### 7.2 What to be sceptical about

- A figure with no link next to it.
- A competitor list with no company names in it.
- A route to market that is a category ("LinkedIn") rather than a place ("the Georgia Poultry
  Federation's member directory").
- A financial model whose assumptions you would not defend to a friend. They are all listed in
  `Assumptions.csv` — read them.

## 8. When something goes wrong

### 8.1 "My link says Order not found"

The link is wrong, or the purchase did not complete. Check your card statement first. If you were
charged, contact support with the email address you used at checkout — that is what finds the
order.

### 8.2 "My link worked before and now says it is gone"

This normally means the order was refunded or disputed. A refund switches the download off. If you
did not request a refund, contact support.

### 8.3 "The download fails with a server error"

That is our fault, not yours. You have a valid purchase and the file could not be served. Contact
support and say so; it is logged on our side and it is fixable.

### 8.4 "It says too many requests"

You have hit the 50-download limit. Contact support and it can be reissued.

### 8.5 "I was charged twice"

Contact support with both dates and amounts. A duplicate is two separate real payments — usually a
double click — and the second can be refunded.

### 8.6 "I want a refund"

There is a **14-day money back** guarantee, stated on the site next to the price. Ask support. The
download is switched off when the refund is processed, which is the only reason a working link
would stop working.

### 8.7 "A source in the pack is broken or wrong"

Report it. Sources are checked before a pack lists, but pages move and get deleted. A pack whose
key sources have rotted is a pack that should be re-verified or pulled.

### 8.8 "The site is showing me an error page"

If the whole site is erroring rather than just your order, it is not your account and not your
purchase. Wait a few minutes and try again. Your permanent link keeps working once the site is
back — it is not tied to your browser or your session.

## 9. Straight answers to the questions people ask

**Do I need an account?**
No. You can buy without one. Accounts exist and you can create one, but the purchase does not
require it and does not create one. Your permanent link is what matters.

**Can I read a pack before buying?**
Yes. A full pack is readable free, with no email required. That is stated on the home page.

**How many ideas were rejected to produce this shelf?**
Thousands. The site publishes its rejections, with the reason each idea was killed, on a page you
can read without buying anything. Note that the published totals lag behind the current figures.

**Is the idea exclusive to me?**
No.

**How current is a pack?**
Each one shows a verification date. Check it. The example above was verified on 14 August 2026.

**What if I disagree with the financial model?**
Good. Open `Assumptions.csv`, change the numbers you disagree with, and see what happens. That file
exists so you can argue with the model line by line.

**Can I use the marketing copy as-is?**
It is a starting point, not finished copy. It is drafted so it does not make claims the pack cannot
support, but you should rewrite it in your own voice.

**Who decides whether an idea passes?**
An automated process, ruled only on sources it actually retrieved. It is not a person's opinion,
and it is not the model's prior knowledge. If it could not fetch a source, it says so.

## 10. The shortest honest summary

**You are buying research, not a business.**

For £19.99 to £99.99 you get one specific idea, checked against public sources, with the case
against it written down, the sources listed, and every unverified answer marked as unverified. You
get it in five files you own permanently, with a 14-day refund and a link that does not expire.

What you do not get is certainty, exclusivity, or anything that knows who you are. The pack tells
you whether the idea stood up to scrutiny. Whether you should be the person to build it is a
question no research file can answer, and this one does not pretend to.
