00What the engine is

A grounded-verification machine that tries to kill business ideas, and sells the ones it cannot.

The engine takes a signal — a news item, a regulation change, a hand-written note — and generates candidate businesses from it. Then it does the opposite of what a pitch deck does: it goes out to the live web, fetches real pages, and looks for evidence that each candidate is dead. Six checks, cheapest first, stopping at the first one that fires. Most candidates die. The survivors are written up as a paid research pack and listed for sale.

Everything downstream of that is plumbing. The part that matters architecturally is the set of invariants the whole system is arranged to protect:

Source-or-die

Every factual claim and every number cites a retrievable source or is stamped unverifiable. No unsourced figure ships.

Verdict from retrieval only

The model rules on passages it actually fetched. Prior knowledge is not admissible. No matching passage means unverifiable — never supported.

A kill needs a citation

Silence cannot kill. Only a refuted verdict backed by a fetched passage fires a hard gate.

Kill fast

Stop at the first hard fail. Never spend research budget on an idea already dead.

Creativity generates, constraint verifies

Nothing is killed at generation time. The two halves never merge.

Two loops never merge

Sales metrics tune what to offer. Truth metrics veto what may ship. Demand never overrides truth.

Who may rule is config

The trusted verdict roster is a line in config.yaml, not code. Anything outside it is stamped provisional and never publishes.

The repo is the whole system

No behaviour lives only in a dashboard or a provider account. A fresh clone plus an env file runs the engine.

The canonical procedure is RUN.md — eight numbered steps, and the file states plainly that the procedure is the guarantee. The sections below map each step onto the code that executes it.

The producer/consumer split

When the trusted verdict brains are exhausted, the pipeline does not stall and it does not guess. generate --no-vet enqueues candidates as DEFER rows (run.py:1938); consume drains them later. This is why an outage costs latency rather than correctness.

The asymmetry, and it is deliberate

Generation may run into a provisional tail. The drain may not. Re-vetting a provisional row on a provisional brain re-stamps it provisional: the row does not move and the money is spent. Measured: provisional −14 / defer +13 in thirty minutes, net −1 row of progress. One shared function with one parameter, so the two paths cannot disagree by accident.

Concurrency

Threads, not processes or async, at three layers: the prescreen pool, the vet pool (both sized by retrieval.vet_workers, overridable with PROSPECTOR_VET_WORKERS), and a per-check search fan-out with one worker per query at verify.py:816. Phases are bounded by wall-clock budgets — gen_time_budget_s, artifact_time_budget_s, vet_time_budget_s — and the whole tick by a hard deadline enforced with a threading.Timer that force-exits the process and lets supervisord restart it cleanly (run_scheduled.py:1623, 2069).

Physical concurrency to CLI-based brains is capped across processes, not within one: prospector/cli_governor.py holds flock-based slot files under ~/.prospector/cli_slots/, so a crashed process cannot leak a slot. claude_cli is clamped to exactly one machine-wide.

What happens to a fetched page

  1. Search through FallbackSearchProvider (retrieval.py:2177), wrapped by DiskCache (:2011) and per-provider circuit breakers.
  2. Resolve and fetch — fetch_page (:751), 8s timeout, 8 workers, 400KB ceiling. An lxml landmark ladder extracts the body; trafilatura is the fallback and only fires below 200 characters, because it costs ~2s a page against lxml's ~0.05s.
  3. Strip consent furniture — strip_consent_sentences (:687). Cookie banners were being cited as evidence.
  4. Select the passage — select_passage (:346) picks the best 1500-character window around the query anchor.
  5. Rank by relevance — RelevanceRankedProvider (:492), overfetch 3×, floor min_relevance: 0.35.
  6. Date the source — _extract_published_at (:605) reads JSON-LD then meta tags. Recency matters to value_durability.
Datacenter IPs change the answer

The free providers behave differently from Fly than from a laptop — measured refusals from prospector-engine including exa returning HTTP 402 NO_MORE_CREDITS. Any retrieval measurement taken locally is not a measurement of production.

05The moat: verification

Six checks, one polarity, and a rule that silence may never kill.

Entry verify() at prospector/verify.py:1043; the work is in _verify_inner at :1071. One check is one call to run_check() at :781.

#GateThe questionKills on
1value_durabilityIs the value still there in three years, or is it already being obsoleted?refuted
2incumbencyIs the space already owned by someone who does this?refuted
3payer_solvencyDoes the payer have money and a budget line?refuted
4distributionIs there a reachable route to the buyer?refuted
5legalityDoes the margin require breaking law or terms?refuted
6pain_realityIs the pain acute and real, or asserted?refuted
7adversarialStrongest evidence-based case that the idea is dead.records only decisive: false
8price_comparablesWhat do comparable things sell for?can never kill barred in kill_filter.is_hard_fail
Do not "fix" the polarity — that is the bug, not the fix

All six checks are framed positively: supported means the space is open, legal, durable, solvent; refuted means an incumbent exists, it is illegal, it is obsolete. So every gate kills on refuted. legality once killed on supported, which inverted it and killed lawful ideas — receipts on disk, store/dossiers/459b72f3630d21be.kill.json, killed because heirloom tomatoes are "completely legal to grow, sell, buy, and eat anywhere in the United States". A lawful workaround must survive.

Inside one check

  1. Frame the question — _check_question (verify.py:358), with market variables injected.
  2. Generate disconfirming queries — gen_queries_batched (:420), driven by prompts/query_gen.md. The queries are written to disprove, not to confirm.
  3. Fetch, in parallel — one thread per query into the grounding chain (:816).
  4. Rule from passages — verdict_for (:481), driven by prompts/verdict.md, on a moat_primary brain only.
  5. Score confidence — _calc_confidence (:91) over sources and citations. Floor to count as a supporting check: min_supported_confidence: 0.3.
An exception is never evidence

A verdict call that raises returns retrieval_failed=True (verify.py:365) and fires the DEFER gate (:693). It does not contribute an unverifiable check to the kill gates. The counter-example is on disk: store/dossiers/2102bacc6dd75cf9.kill.json — a candidate killed by our own outage, in a dossier that reads as fully reasoned.

The old weights paid out 0.30 of the composite for "trivially easy to build and automate" — which is the same sentence as "trivially easy to clone" — while defensibility, the only moat axis, carried 0.15. A generic AI wrapper was the global maximum of that formula. Composite must clear min_composite_to_pass: 2.5.

Price — a rung, never a continuous number

Segment (ambition tier × market) selects a rung index; the rung is a fixed price. GBP rungs [1999, 2999, 4999, 7999, 9999], USD rungs [2499, 3999, 6999, 10999, 13999, 19999, 26999], ladder L1-ladder-2026-08-09-charm. Tier offsets: side_hustle 1, smb 2, growth 3, venture 4; market offset uk 0, us 1.

Evidence and action are separate switches

Price comparables are retrieved by default (comparables.enabled: true). Letting them move a rung is a second, independent flag — rung_adjust_enabled: false. One flag for both is how a catalogue re-prices itself the day a feature merges. FX is config-declared with a dated source, never inferred.

Why backup_store.py is loud when R2Uploader is silent

R2Uploader returns False rather than raising and no-ops entirely when unconfigured, so a missing credential can never stop a pack from selling. That is right for the publish path and exactly wrong for backup. scripts/backup_store.py fails loudly and reads the copy back, because a backup that "succeeds" by doing nothing is worse than no backup — you stop looking. It was written after a day when 1,153 dossiers and an 84MB ledger existed in exactly one place, with no Time Machine destination configured.

10Model routing

Three separate rosters. Crossing them is the failure mode the design exists to prevent.

ChainConfig keyLive valueMay rule a verdict?
Moatoperator:[minimax, claude_cli, groq, mistral]Only if also in moat_primary
Trusted setmoat_primary:[minimax, claude_cli]yes — final

Anything ruling from outside moat_primary is stamped provisional (is_provisional_provider), never publishes on PASS, and is automatically re-vetted. Promotion into the trusted set is that config line plus a golden-set pass — never a patch.

claude_cli shells out to the local Claude CLI pinned to the cheapest model, concurrency clamped to one machine-wide. Operator classes in prospector/operator.py: Gemini, MiniMax, DeepSeek, OpenRouter, Ollama, an OpenAI-compatible generic, a Mock, and FallbackOperator which chains named tiers behind circuit breakers. A removed tier name raises ValueError at startup, so a stale config fails loudly rather than silently building a shorter chain.

A dead brain must leave a trace

A fallback chain that works hides its own degradation. Permanence is classified by one shared, tested function — errors.looks_exhausted — used by every metered adapter. Transient backpressure (429/503/529, overloaded_error) benches a brain for 60s; permanent exhaustion (402, credit balance, any usage allowance) for an hour, and permanent wins ties. HTTP codes match on word boundaries: a bare substring once let a request id bench a live brain.

Gate on the rate, not the stock

A stock brake has unbounded memory: one outage suppresses generation indefinitely. The grounding brake measures the rate instead. And when the backlog count fails, run.drainable() returns None, never 0 — so generation stops rather than being waved through by a failed measurement.

test_a_huge_page_is_selected_in_reasonable_time asserted a cost property with time.monotonic(). It passed serially and at 8 workers, and failed at 12 — identical code, purely from being descheduled. It now uses time.process_time(). Wall clock plus parallelism makes a test a coin toss, and the fix always looks like "raise the budget".

CI jobs

ci.yml fans out on a changes filter into: guard (doc lint, link check, capacity, runner tools), python (the suite plus the golden set), engine (verify_engine_change.sh), dotnet (Store.Tests), nextjs and ops-console (typecheck, lint, CSS lint, unit, npm audit --audit-level=high, build, Playwright, a11y, e2e), converging on ci-ok.

Deploy is three workflows — deploy-engine, deploy-api, deploy-web — each a checkout plus flyctl deploy with --config and --dockerfile both explicit, so a stale remote config cannot decide what ships. deploy-web goes further: it proves the site serves after deploying and rolls back automatically if it does not.

The rest are estate guards: main-admission-guard, main-green-guard, merge-when-green, pr-keeper, ci-fleet-keeper, ci-fleet-watch, cancel-ci-on-pr-close, approve-parked-runs, dns-drift-drill, escape-hatch-drill, e2e-live-smoke, production-runs-main, weekly-estate-review.

Why so many guards

Native auto-merge is not available on this plan — 403 Upgrade to GitHub Pro on both branch protection and rulesets. The workflows above are the substitute. One consequence worth internalising: ci.yml sets cancel-in-progress for every ref that is not main, so an agent pushing to its own branch could kill the in-flight run about to merge someone else's work. Measured at the time: 7 of 60 CI runs succeeded, 16 were cancelled.

14Gaps and cautions

What an architect should not take on trust.

  • run_v2.py is a second pipeline. A standalone moat runner using prospector/pipeline/ (generator.py → verifier.py → middleware.py), not wired into run.py or the scheduler. Whether it is a live path or a dev harness is unverified.
  • No checkpointing inside a single vet. The in-flight marker records that a vet started; a kill mid-vet loses that candidate's partial check results and it re-runs from scratch. Six checks of paid retrieval.
  • The figure fence ships OFF. Up to 30% of the catalogue is listed without figure verification. That is a recorded, deliberate revenue decision, not an oversight — but it is a decision, and it is still open.
  • The laptop store is dead data. Any number derived from store/ on a dev machine describes a snapshot frozen at the Fly cutover, not production.
  • Both search-provider classes named "search" are not search. DeepSeekSearchProvider, MiniMaxSearchProvider and OpenRouterSearchProvider are LLM synthesis and will hallucinate URLs. They exist as classes; nothing should put them in a grounding chain.
  • The 393-finding ruff baseline includes 10 F821 undefined names. Those are NameErrors waiting for the branch that reaches them, not style debt.
  • Prose in docs drifts; commands do not. Whether the pre-commit gate is installed has been documented wrongly in both directions. Ask git config --get core.hooksPath, never a paragraph.
