## The thesis, in one paragraph

The pack is not badly written by accident. **Every one of the founder's six complaints traces to
a rule we deliberately wrote, each of which was the correct fix for a real earlier defect.** The
programme that produced today's pack optimised for *evidentiary integrity* and *anti-padding*, and
each optimisation removed a storytelling affordance without anyone noticing, because no gate,
linter or spec has ever measured whether the pack is worth reading. The result is a document with
the structure of an audit report and the voice of a disclaimer. It is true, checkable, correctly
packaged, and nobody would enjoy owning it.

**This is not a prompt-tuning job. The pack has no narrative spine to tune.** Its spine is the
engine's check list.

## Receipts — the six complaints, and the line of code that causes each

Verified on disk in this worktree unless marked otherwise.

### 1. "there is no background"

`prompts/artifacts.md:18-34`, the HARD RULE added to fix P4 (de-duplication):

> *"apply the evidence, do not re-argue it … **Never open a section by summarising the evidence.
> Open with the decision or the step.** … **If a point is background rather than an instruction
> for THIS artifact, cut it.** It is already in the evidence document; repeating it is what makes
> the pack feel padded."*

The fix for *saying the same thing three times* was implemented as *never set a scene at all*.
Background was banned outright rather than being given one home and one voice. There is now no
document in the pack whose job is to explain the world the reader is walking into.

### 2. "we ramble about composite scores, things our engine does that does not concern us"

The buyer's **first screen** is generated by `prospector/pack_floors.py:116` `exec_summary_md`.
Verbatim, in order:

```
# Executive summary — <Title>
<one-liner>

## Start here — the next ten minutes
1. Open **The QA Report, with the receipts** and pick any claim marked SUPPORTED.
2. Click its source link and find the sentence the claim rests on.
3. If the source does not say what we say it says, stop reading and claim the refund
   — the pack is wrong and you should not build on it.

## Grounded signals
- **buyer intent:** …

## What this pack does not claim
No unsourced TAM/SAM figures, guaranteed revenue, or legality shortcuts. If a check was
unverifiable, it is absent here on purpose.
```

**The first instruction a paying customer receives is how to try to get a refund**, followed by a
section of things we are not claiming. Three of the four blocks on the opening screen are about
*our process*, not about *their opportunity*. The cover above it
(`prospector/pack_html.py:60` `_cover_html`) is title, one-liner, `Verified <date>`,
`Checked N claims against M sources` — a provenance stat block, then a table of contents.

The docstring at `pack_floors.py:117-129` argues this deliberately: *"the page opens with the
shortest action that ends in the buyer knowing something they did not know when they paid …
That action is to check us."* The reasoning is honest and the outcome is a product that opens by
talking about itself.

Reinforcing it: `prospector/bridge.py:284` `PACK_DOCUMENTS` ends the reading order on
`QA_Report`, and `pack_floors.py:29` names that section *"The QA Report, with the receipts"*.
The audit is a headline deliverable, not an appendix.

### 3. "we dont motivate or inspire user"

`prompts/style/voice.md:15-17`, the reader definition:

> *"WHAT THEY NEED FROM YOU is enough to judge whether this is worth doing: who pays, what it
> costs them today, what the work actually is, and what would sink it. **Not encouragement.**"*

That clause is a direct instruction not to do the thing the founder is now asking for. It was
right when it was written (it was aimed at hype), and it is now read by the model as *withhold
all energy*. `voice.md:18-21` then makes the reader rule **outrank every style rule below it**,
so the flatness propagates to every document.

### 4. "we talk down the opportunity"

`prompts/content_gen.md:23-32`:

> *"(d) never call the evidence 'clear', 'proven', 'strong', or 'guaranteed' … hedge to the
> claim's strength; (e) attribute every external figure to its source as THAT source's own claim
> … (f) an incumbent existing or having traction is evidence the PROBLEM is real, NOT evidence
> the reader's new venture will succeed."*

Each rule is individually correct and defends against a real failure. **Together they leave no
grammatical construction in which the pack is allowed to sound like it believes what it found.**
The pack's own positive findings arrive pre-apologised. Compounded by
`pack_floors.py:170-174`'s `## What this pack does not claim` section and by
`prompts/artifacts.md:32-34`'s `"assumption — unverified"` convention.

Note the shape of the trap: our confidence rules are calibrated against *the evidence for a
market claim*. They are then applied to sentences that are not market claims at all — the
sequencing, the operating design, the stop conditions. **We hedge our own judgement as if it
were someone else's data.**

### 5. "our tone and language is hurried and cryptic"

`prospector/evidence_budget.py` (shipped 2026-08-14, `config.yaml:1750 enforce_length_budget:
true`) caps prose at `900 + 1.0 × evidence_words`, split three ways, floor 600, ceiling 3600
(`config.yaml:1759-1761`, `evidence_budget.py:168-175`). The prompt fragment
(`evidence_budget.py:132-148`) says *"AT MOST N words. Shorter is better and there is no minimum.
Do not pad to reach a length … A paragraph that only restates what the reader has already been
told is deleted, not rewritten."*

The measurement behind it was correct and is quoted in the module docstring: 6,330 prose words
from ~800 words of evidence, **78.3% of sentences containing no number, price or percentage.**
The diagnosis was *padding*. **The prescription was a length cap — which removes padding and
narrative with the same cut**, because a cap cannot tell connective tissue from filler. Combined
with §1's ban on background and §3's ban on encouragement, the model has been told: no
scene-setting, no warmth, no words to spare. Hurried and cryptic is the specified output.

### 6. "not passing any useful info nor engaging nor entertaining the user on the website"

`docs/SITE_SPEC_PROGRAM.md:27` sets the design thesis as *"the site is the visible **console** of
the vetting engine, not a brochure"*. A console is a status surface. **No spec anywhere in this
repo assigns the storefront the job of teaching a visitor something they did not know.** The
catalogue is a filterable grid of packs; the free content is the card line and the facets.
`docs/HANDOFF_PACK_CONTENTS_REVIEW.md:259` separately records that Marketing_Assets is thin on
8 of 59 packs — flagged there as *"a content-generation problem, not packaging"*, and never
picked up.

Confirmed gap, not duplicate work: no existing programme doc contains rules for pack narrative,
scene-setting, motivation, or storefront editorial content.

## What this means for the fix

Three consequences follow from the receipts above, and they constrain every option below.

1. **Do not simply reverse the rules.** Each one was written against a defect that will return:
   triple-repeated background, hype, padding, unsupported confidence. The fix has to give each
   banned thing exactly one licensed home with its own bar, not re-legalise it everywhere.
2. **Confidence must be split by KIND of claim.** A market fact stays hedged to its evidence. Our
   own operating judgement — the sequencing, the failure modes, the stop conditions, the thing
   the founder called *"better than a consultant would write"*
   (`docs/PACK_QUALITY_PROGRAM.md:218`) — is ours to state plainly. Today one rule governs both.
3. **The pack needs a spine that is not the check list.** Section order today is the engine's
   pipeline. It has to become a reader's journey.

## What the best comparables at this price point actually ship

Sources cited inline; every URL here was retrieved, not recalled. The closest formal
comparable to a £20–100 opportunity dossier is **Trends.vc** (~$299/yr, ~2,000–2,500 words per
report, 160+ reports), whose skeleton is public and stable:

> What You'll Learn → Why It Matters → Problem → Solution → **Players** → Predictions →
> Opportunities → Key Lessons → **Haters** → Related Reports → Links → Thanks
> — https://trends.vc/trends-0064-agencies/

Not Boring's company dives run *What is X → The Thesis → **The Bear Case** → Strategy → The Future*
(https://www.notboring.co/p/stripe-the-internets-most-undervalued). a16z's State of Crypto titles
every chapter with the **finding** ("Stablecoins went mainstream") and proves it beneath
(https://a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/state-of-crypto-report-2025/). Bessemer ships **benchmark
bands** a reader grades themselves against — CAC payback 12–18mo good / 6–12 better / 0–6 best
(https://www.saastr.com/state-of-the-cloud-2023-with-bessemer-venture-partners/).

**The two sections every good comparable has and we have none of:**

1. **Players / the field.** Trends.vc's agencies report names 40+ companies with links, taglines and
   revenue figures (Procreate $30M, Lemlist $1M). We run an `incumbency` check, retrieve the
   evidence, and throw it away as a one-line verdict. Naming three competitors without saying what
   they charge, what they cover and what they miss is the single biggest gap between our pack and a
   product worth ten times its price. IPVM's teardown of commodity research mills names *"missing
   the obvious players"* as the tell of a fake report (https://ipvm.com/reports/scam-research).
2. **The bear case, as a named section with counterargument.** We already write the bear case — it
   is scattered through every document as apology. Giving it a chapter converts the same sentences
   from hedging into a trust device.

**Worth-10x ingredients we do not ship**: named operators with real
numbers; benchmark bands; a named reusable framework; proprietary artefacts that exist nowhere else;
downloadable re-usable data; visible labour (Trends.vc states 20–30 hours per report); a stated
sourcing/ethics policy; multi-format delivery of one research spine; a dated 90-day ending with
owner, metric and proof.

**Buyer-rage patterns we should be measured against** (https://ipvm.com/reports/scam-research):
repackaged search results; template bleed-through; missing obvious players; forecasts with no
derivation; no way to tell who did the work. Note we are structurally strong on the last two and
weak on the first three.

## What journalists actually do — the craft we are told to borrow

Founder's instruction was to *"borrow fron journalists and newspapers in how to tell a story"*. This
is what the trade actually teaches. Every rule below carries a retrievable source; where a primary
text refused to fetch, the substitution is named.

### The four-part shape (the WSJ formula)

**Anecdotal lede → nut graf → body → kicker.** It "became a staple of The Wall Street Journal's
long-form front-page features… where reporters were trained to follow a colorful anecdotal lede with
a tightly argued nut graf summarizing the thesis", and the form "emphasizes explanation over
information and understanding over knowledge"
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nut_graph).

### The nut graf — the single most load-bearing paragraph, and the thing we have no equivalent of

- Ken Wells, WSJ Page One editor: *"It's a paragraph that says what this whole story is about and
  why you should read it… It's a flag to the reader, high up in the story: You can decide to proceed
  or not."* (https://www.poynter.org/archive/2003/the-nut-graf-part-i/)
- Jack Hart, The Oregonian: it answers *"the basic question lurking in the mind of every reader:
  'Why should I bother with this story?'"* (same source)
- **Jan Winburn, CNN: *"The nut graph is the promise of the piece, not the summary."***
  (https://niemanstoryboard.org/2019/02/01/a-nutcracker-suite/) — this is precisely the distinction
  our executive summary fails. Ours summarises our process; it promises the reader nothing.
- The three questions it must answer (Beth Kaiman, Seattle Times): *"What's at stake? For whom? Why
  does it matter?"* (same source)
- Placement: *"the second paragraph at the earliest, and shouldn't fall beyond the fifth"*
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nut_graph); Poynter adds it *"shouldn't be longer than two or three
  paragraphs"*.

### The lede

- Zinsser: *"The most important sentence in any article is the first one. If it doesn't induce the
  reader to proceed to the second sentence, your article is dead."*
- David Quammen, National Geographic: the opening *"has to… offer a promise and establish tension."*
- John McPhee: *"It should shine like a flashlight down through the piece."*
- Tim De Chant, NOVA: the best ledes *"stand alone as their own tiny story."*
  (all: https://www.theopennotebook.com/2015/07/14/good-beginnings/)

### The kicker — how to end so the reader is moved rather than dropped

Bruce DeSilva's three-function test: an ending must **(1)** tell the reader the story is over,
**(2)** *"nail the central point of the story to the reader's mind"*, **(3)** *"resonate… It should
stay with you and make you think a little bit."* Superior endings *"surprise readers while feeling
inevitable upon reflection."* (https://www.chipswritinglessons.com/2019/10/29/put-ends-first/).
Steve Volk: *"The best endings echo the beginning in some essential but surprising way."*
(https://www.theopennotebook.com/2015/11/24/good-endings-how-to-write-a-kicker-your-editor-and-your-readers-will-love/).
Our packs currently end at day 10 with *"Write down what changed your mind"*.

### The hourglass — the shape that resolves "evidence first" against "story first"

Roy Peter Clark's 1983 hybrid, and the closest fit to a product whose credibility rests on evidence:
**top** (summary lede + the three or four most pressing answers) → **the turn** (an explicit
signalled transition, *"a transitional phrase that contains attribution for the narrative that
follows"*) → **the narrative** (beginning, middle, end, with detail and background). It *"satisfies
editors who prefer a traditional approach… as well as impatient readers who tire easily of leisurely
approaches"*
(https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2003/the-hourglass-serving-the-news-serving-the-reader/).

**This settles the BLUF objection against the founder's ask.** The Economist's own instruction is
*"Catch the attention of the reader and then get straight into the article. Do not spend several
sentences clearing your throat, setting the scene or sketching in the background"* (*Style Guide*,
12th ed., Introduction p.1 — verified by local text extraction of
https://profilebooks.com/wp-content/uploads/wpallimport/files/PDFs/9781781258316_preview.pdf). A
short scene and a bottom-line-up-front are not in conflict; a *long* scene and BLUF are. Our
opening scene is capped at 120 words for exactly this reason.

### Background is not padding — it is a contract with a specific length

- The Economist: *"Articles in The Economist should be like essays, in that they have a beginning, a
  middle and an end. They should not be mere bits of information stitched together. Each should be a
  coherent whole… and, ideally, will suffer if even one sentence is cut out."* (12th ed. Intro p.2)
- Clark's explanatory toolkit: *"Slow down the pace of information," "Translate jargon," "Use
  analogies,"* a conversational voice
  (https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2014/a-new-explanatory-journalism-can-be-built-on-a-strong-foundation/).
- Background is meant to **shrink over reissues, not vanish**: *"with the passage of time… the
  background paragraph can go lower and lower in the piece, and can run shorter and shorter"*
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nut_graph). `prompts/artifacts.md:18-34` deleted the slot instead of
  bounding it.

### The failure mode of explanatory writing, which we must not import

The explainer genre earned real contempt — *"extremely condescending… as if they were four years
old,"* a *"dumbed-down, cloyingly didactic tone"*
(https://democracyjournal.org/arguments/nine-questions-about-explainer-journalism-you-were-too-embarrassed-to-ask/).
Teju Cole: *"Seeing explainer sites explain a subject you know well is a vivid demonstration of the
limitations of explainer sites."* Henry Farrell's cure is the one we are already built for: it
*"needs to build a sense of the explainer's own fallibility into the argument"*
(https://nyujournalismprojects.org/newsliteracy2016/topics/explainer-journalism/). Our §6 bear case
IS that device.

### Numbers — the diagnosis of "we ramble about composite scores"

This is not a length problem, it is a referent problem.

- KSJ Handbook: *"Don't fall into the trap of thinking that numbers in and of themselves are
  meaningful"*; prefer *"slightly less than one out of four"* to *"23.7%"*
  (https://ksjhandbook.org/making-sense-of-science-stats/helping-readers-understand-the-numbers/).
- Poynter: *"When you use a statistic, compare it to something people can relate to"*; Paul Hemp's
  test is **"Compared to what?"**; Jeff South: *"Words, not data, make a story."*
  (https://www.poynter.org/archive/2002/writing-with-numbers/)
- Alberto Cairo, the annotation layer: *"Explanatory visualizations shouldn't consist of visualizing
  data alone, but also of adding words to put the data in context, highlight the most relevant
  facts, or dispel possible misinterpretations."*
  (https://thefunctionalart.blogspot.com/2020/01/an-example-of-how-to-annotate.html)
- Alan Smith, FT, the editorial test: **"Does it surprise you? … if it doesn't surprise you, how
  many readers is it going to surprise?"** (https://statsandstories.net/economics1/start-with-the-charts)

**Applied: `composite 2.6500` fails all four tests at once.** It has no comparison a reader holds,
no annotation, and it cannot surprise because the reader has no scale for it. The fix is not to
soften the sentence. It is to delete the number and state its consequence.

### Hedging vs. stated uncertainty — the reconciliation this product needs

Johns Hopkins, on weasel words: *"don't undercut your argument with weasel words — empty palliatives
such as 'to a certain degree,' 'it may seem likely that,' or 'in some cases.' If your points are
weak, they need no additional burdens… Weasel words dilute your thought, and hence your argument."*
(https://hub.jhu.edu/2020/03/20/weasel-words-writing-tips/)

But hedging is not uniformly a vice — Purdue OWL calls hedges *"barriers to protect academic writers
from making statements beyond the level of their certainty"*
(https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/multilingual/multilingual_students/tips_for_writing_in_north_american_colleges/reasonability.html).

**The reconciliation, and it is the key sentence in this whole document: replace hedging language
with stated uncertainty.** Not *"this may to some extent suggest"* but *"one source, dated 2024,
says X; nothing corroborates it."* Wikipedia's own MOS:WEASEL exempts exactly this — the phrases are
acceptable *"when the article body… can supply attribution"*
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch). **This converts
source-or-die from the cause of our flat prose into the mechanism of our voice.** We have the
attribution. We were spending it on hedges instead of on confidence.

### Sentence-level rules to adopt wholesale

The Economist, 12th ed. Introduction — four that read as direct corrections of our current output:

> *"Do not be hectoring or arrogant… When you express opinions, do not simply make assertions. The
> aim is not just to tell readers what you think, but to persuade them; if you use arguments,
> reasoning and evidence, you may succeed. Go easy on the oughts and shoulds."*
> *"Do not be too pleased with yourself. Don't boast of your own cleverness…"* ← our exec summary
> *"Do not be too didactic. If too many sentences begin Compare, Consider, Expect, Imagine, Look at,
> Note, Prepare for, Remember or Take, readers will think they are reading a textbook."* ← our
> checklists
> *"Do not be stuffy… prefer let to permit, people to persons, buy to purchase."*

Plus: Helen Sword's **zombie nouns** — *"they cannibalize active verbs, suck the lifeblood from
adjectives and substitute abstract entities for human beings"*, with a mechanical test (paste prose
into https://writersdiet.com/writing-test/; *"'flabby' or 'heart attack' in the noun category
indicates that 5 percent or more of your words are nominalizations"*)
(https://archive.nytimes.com/opinionator.blogs.nytimes.com/2012/07/23/zombie-nouns/). This is
**mechanically checkable in `copy_lint.py`** — the one rule here that can become a gate rather than a
prompt instruction.

And Clark's tools, of which two are directly actionable for us: *"Put the most powerful word of each
sentence at the end"*, and *"Place gold coins along the path. Don't load all your best stuff high in
the story."* (http://www.swopnet.com/misc/writing/writing_tools.html)

**Caveat carried from the research, not suppressed:** Geoffrey Pullum's demolition of Strunk & White
("50 Years of Stupid Grammar Advice", https://www.lel.ed.ac.uk/~gpullum/50years.pdf) also rejects
*"Omit needless words"* as useless — *"The students who know which words are needless don't need the
instruction."* Keep the behaviour (cut, use verbs, be concrete); do not write a rule that only
restates the goal. Our evidence budget is exactly that mistake in executable form.

## Proposed pack structure — a reader's journey, not the engine's pipeline

Today's reading order is the pipeline: exec summary → build → GTM → ops → financials → checklist →
marketing → evidence → QA. Proposed:

| # | Section | Job | Where the content comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The opening** (~350 words) | A dated, cited, concrete scene. The wall this buyer's customers hit. | The paragraph we already write and bury in QA_Report's "How it works" |
| 2 | **Why this is a business** (nut graf, ~200 words) | Who pays, what they pay today, what the gap is, why now. A reader may stop here and still have got their money's worth. | candidate fields + strongest supported checks |
| 3 | **What you would be selling** | The offer, its price, its buyer, in one page | build spec §1 |
| 4 | **The field** ⚠️ NEW | Named incumbents and adjacents: what they charge, what they cover, what they miss | the `incumbency` check's retrieved passages, currently discarded |
| 5 | **The numbers** | Unit economics with good/better/best bands | `financial_model`, rebuilt |
| 6 | **What would sink this** ⚠️ NEW SECTION, EXISTING CONTENT | The bear case, consolidated, with counterargument where we have one | every hedge currently scattered across the plans |
| 7–9 | The build / the route to market / the operation | unchanged, they are the good part | unchanged |
| 10 | **The first fortnight, then the first quarter** | dated, owner + metric + proof; currently stops at day 10 | `pack_checklist`, extended |
| 11 | **The toolkit** ⚠️ NEW | Templates the buyer can use tomorrow: an intake form, an outreach script, a proposal outline, a pricing sheet | deterministic renderers over dossier fields, no model call, so it can be backfilled |
| 12 | **The evidence** | Every claim, every source, the checks — compressed, and at the back | today's QA_Report, cut from 52% of the pack to an appendix |
| 13 | **How to know in 30 days** (the kicker) | The falsification test and what to do either way | new, short |

Plus a standing **method and sourcing policy** page (the Stratechery `/about` pattern) stating how
many ideas were screened, what we refuse to cite, and that nobody pays to be listed.

**Mapped to the journalistic devices**, so the shape is borrowed rather than invented: §1 is the
**anecdotal lede** (≤120 words, Economist's no-throat-clearing rule); §2 is the **nut graf** — the
promise, answering Kaiman's *what's at stake / for whom / why does it matter*, and it is the
paragraph the pack currently has no equivalent of; §3 is the **turn** into evidence; §4–§9 are the
**body**, each gate written as claim + cited evidence + consequence rather than as a score; §6 is
Farrell's **fallibility device**; §13 is the **kicker**, circling to the named buyer in §1. §1–§2
together are Clark's **hourglass top** — which is why a scene and a bottom-line-up-front do not
conflict here.

**Structural moves this encodes:**

- Background gets **one licensed home** (§1–2). `prompts/artifacts.md:18-34` survives unchanged for
  §7–9 — the plans still may not re-explain, they reference. The de-dup fix is preserved, not reversed.
- The audit is demoted from headline deliverable to appendix. It stays complete and checkable; it
  stops being the largest thing the buyer scrolls through.
- The refund instruction moves out of the opening screen to the evidence appendix and the receipt
  email. It is a promise, not a welcome.
- `## Grounded signals` and `## What this pack does not claim` are deleted as sections. The first
  ships empty on 36 of 140 packs; the second is a paragraph of negatives on the opening screen.

## Proposed voice-rule amendments — the three edits that unlock the rest

1. **`prompts/style/voice.md:15-17`** — replace *"Not encouragement."* with a rule that separates
   hype from energy: *no adjective may assert an outcome; stakes, specificity and consequence carry
   the weight instead.* The clause was written against hype and is now read as "withhold all energy".
2. **`prompts/content_gen.md:23-32`** — split the confidence rule **by kind of claim**. A statement
   about the market stays hedged to its evidence exactly as today. A statement of our own operating
   judgement — the sequencing, the failure modes, the stop conditions — is stated plainly, because
   hedging it is not honesty, it is a category error. This is the single change that stops the pack
   talking down its own opportunity.

   The operative rewrite is **hedging language → stated uncertainty**. Not *"this may to some extent
   suggest"* but *"one source, dated 2024, says X; nothing corroborates it."* Both are equally
   honest; only the second is readable, and only the second spends the attribution we already paid
   to retrieve. MOS:WEASEL exempts hedge-free phrasing precisely *"when the article body… can supply
   attribution"* — which is the one thing this engine always has.
4. **`prospector/copy_lint.py`** — add a nominalisation check. Sword's zombie-noun test is
   mechanical (`-tion`, `-ment`, `-ance`, `-ity` density over a threshold) and is the only rule in
   this document that can be a gate rather than a prompt instruction, so it will not drift.
3. **`prospector/evidence_budget.py` + `config.yaml:1759-1761`** — do not lift the cap; **reallocate
   it.** `base_words: 900` prices the operational judgement that is legitimately in no source; §1,
   §6, §11 and §13 are exactly that content and are currently priced at zero. The evidence-scaled
   part (`words_per_evidence_word`) should fund §4 (the field), which is the section that most needs
   retrieved material. A flat per-artifact cap cannot distinguish narrative tissue from padding —
   per-section budgets can.

## Storefront — what to publish free

The site already holds the raw material (kill log, counters, one free sample pack, "New this week").
None of it is browsable, dated, cited or entertaining. Six builds, evidence cited inline:

1. **Turn the kill log into a database.** Killed-by-Google form (https://killedbygoogle.com/):
   filter chips carrying live counts (`Killed on incumbency 412 · on payer solvency 288 …`), search,
   and a card per kill = title, date, the gate it died on, the one-sentence verdict, and **the
   outbound citation that killed it**. CB Insights' 483 startup post-mortems is the same page
   earning its keep as a lead product (https://www.cbinsights.com/research/startup-failure-post-mortem/).
   Highest-leverage page on the site; today it is a nav link with no furniture.
2. **A weekly "gate autopsy" essay from real kill data** — *"we ran 60 marketplace ideas through
   payer solvency; 51 died; here is the passage that killed each"*. Product-led content: it teaches
   a real screening skill and demonstrates the engine.
3. **"Kill my idea"** — a free box where a visitor's own idea runs the gates with the fetching
   visible in front of them, ending in a cited verdict and a shareable card. This is literally the
   labor-illusion experiment (Buell & Norton, *Management Science* 57:9 — showing work in progress
   raised perceived value 8% on identical output; https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=40158).
4. **A public engine dashboard** (Buffer's `/open` form, https://buffer.com/open): ideas screened
   this week, kill rate by gate over time, median citations per pack, spend per verdict. Zero content
   cost — it is telemetry we already write.
5. **Editorial furniture on the catalogue**: a hand-picked shelf, themed collections, and per-pack
   display of *which check nearly failed*.
6. **Keep the free sample generous, and pair it with a concrete refund line.** Metering collapsed
   from 35% of digital publishers in 2017 to 9% in 2023
   (https://www.websiteplanet.com/blog/2025-paywall-index-a-data-driven-study-across-industries/);
   Hilton & Wiley measured +26% print sales from free digital release
   (https://quod.lib.umich.edu/j/jep/3336451.0014.109/--free-e-books-and-print-sales?rgn=main;view=fulltext).

**Caveat that constrains #5.** The pratfall/blemishing effect *reverses* under high elaboration
(Ein-Gar et al.; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratfall_effect). A buyer weighing a £99 pack is
elaborating. So near-miss checks must be published as cited evidence with a stated confidence,
never as charm.

**Showing negatives is the best-evidenced lever here**: Buell, Kim & Tsay measured customer-reported
quality +22.2% from operational transparency (*Management Science* 63:6,
https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2015.2411).

## What shipped — the implementation ledger (2026-08-15)

Everything above this line is the DIAGNOSIS, written before any code was touched. Everything
below is what was actually built on `feat/pack-narrative-overhaul`, so that a future reader can
tell the proposal from the product without running the engine. Where a claim here is checkable,
it is written as a path or a `file:line`; check it rather than trusting the sentence.

### The reading order, and why five new modules

The pack is now fourteen sections in a fixed reading order — `bridge.BUNDLE_READING_ORDER`, with
`bridge._SECTION_TITLES` mapping each filename to the title the buyer sees. Five renderers were
added for the shape §"Proposed pack structure" argued for:

| Module | Section it renders |
|---|---|
| `prospector/pack_offer.py` | the offer, stated before the evidence |
| `prospector/pack_field.py` | "The field: who is already there" |
| `prospector/pack_bear_case.py` | the bear case, which ABSORBS the financial model's weaknesses |
| `prospector/pack_toolkit.py` | the toolkit |
| `prospector/pack_kicker.py` | the kicker — how the pack ends |

They join `pack_floors`, `pack_checklist` and `pack_reference`, which already existed.

**All eight are deterministic: they render from dossier fields and make no model call.** That is
not a style preference, it is what makes them BACKFILLABLE onto the 145 bundles already in
`publish/bundles/`. A renderer that called a model would produce a different document every time
it ran, so a pack bought last month and the same pack rebuilt today would disagree, and the
restructure would reach future buyers only.

Two shapes reach these renderers and both must work: a live `Dossier`, and the `SimpleNamespace`
tree that `pack_manifest.dossier_from_dict` builds from stored JSON (`pack_manifest._ns`,
`pack_manifest.py:356`, which builds attributes from dict KEYS ONLY, and whose verdicts are plain
strings, not enum members). Read every field with `getattr` and a default, or the backfill path
raises on a pack the generator handled fine.

### The backfill — the reason any of this reaches the existing shelf

`tools/backfill_bundle_html.py:335-390` renders the same five modules, in the same order as
`bridge._create_bundle`, through the same prose pass, and performs the same bear-case absorb into
`04_Financial_Model.md`. `tests/unit/test_backfill_renders_the_new_sections.py` pins the property
that matters — `test_the_backfilled_reader_is_the_generated_reader_byte_for_byte`. Two whole-archive
differences are deliberate and outside the sections: `patched_md`'s legacy `Evidence goes stale
after:` rewrite is backfill-only, and `manifest.jsonld` digests differ because they are digests OF
these bytes.

Putting it onto the live shelf is `scripts/backfill_packs_parallel.sh --apply`. That is an
operator action — it repoints bundles buyers can already download — and it is deliberately not
something the engine or an agent does on its own.

### Three gates that were reporting on less than they appeared to

1. **The prose pass skipped a third of the pack.** `plain_text.publish_pass_document` (imported
   into bridge as `prose_pass_document`) runs on every engine-authored document — but the five
   deterministic sections are appended AFTER it has already run, so until this branch it never
   saw them. It applies to them now, inside the render loop. Safe because the pass is itself pure
   Python with no model call, so a backfilled pack still produces identical bytes.

   **The trap it exists to catch, and which it caused in two places:** the pass DELETES any line
   ending in `…`. `pack_floors` and `pack_field` both truncated to a character budget and closed
   on an ellipsis, so the line was rendered and then silently removed. In `pack_field` that left a
   citation with a link and no passage under it, on a source-or-die storefront. If you write a
   renderer that truncates, do not end the line on the ellipsis — `pack_field._CUT_NOTE` shows the
   pattern (mark the cut, then close the sentence).

2. **The pack linter was grading 9 of 14 sections.** `lint_pack` was handed `artifacts` — the four
   model-written documents. `_create_bundle` now takes a `sections_out` out-param and fills it with
   the assembled read (buyer-visible title -> markdown, drawn from `BUNDLE_READING_ORDER` so what
   is graded is literally what `pack_html` renders), and `publish_pass` passes it as
   `pack_sections`. Anything that stubs `_create_bundle` in a test must accept that keyword.

3. **`check_repetition` had zero callers** while four source comments described it as live. It is
   called from `lint_pack` now, and it is the one check that takes its own corpus, because
   repetition is invisible section by section and exists only in the assembly.

### What is ON, what is OFF, and why the asymmetry is deliberate

| Switch | Default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `listing.lint_repetition_block` | **off** | Grades STYLE. Follows the repo's baseline-first pattern (`lint_grammar`, `shelf_copy_block_on_breach`, `title_block_on_breach`): the check RUNS and `repetition_findings` accrues in `<id>.lint.json`; the actuator turns on against numbers measured across live packs, not the one pack that motivated it. |
| `readability_grades` | recorded, **no actuator at all** | See below. |
| `listing.claim_check_block` | **on** | Grades TRUTH. "No unsourced numbers ship, ever" has no exception for the document the buyer pays for. |

**Readability is a finding, not a threshold.** Measured on pack `e698149e137fc164`, the fourteen
sections spread from grade 5.9 to 17.3 — the deterministic sections at 5.9-7.3, the model-written
ones at 12.6-13.8, and the worst was "Copy you can paste" at 17.3, the section whose entire job is
to hand the buyer sentences for a landing page. The spread grades WHO WROTE a section rather than
whether the writing is good, and a grade ceiling would block a pack for quoting a statute. So
`pack_linter.readability_grades` returns numbers into the lint receipt and nothing acts on them.

### The paid-artifact claim gate — the one behaviour change that will hold packs back

`generate_artifacts` had run a claim-check on the paid prose since it was wired, and its
violations reached a `logger.info` and nothing else: `unverified` was built, filled, read once by
that log line, and `generate_artifacts` returned the documents alone. Meanwhile the FREE marketing
copy on the same gate was DROPPED when it failed. Same check, opposite consequence, and the one
let through was the document the buyer pays for.

The record now rides `candidate.tags["unverified_claims"]` — the same channel the artifacts
themselves travel on — into `bridge.publish_pass`, where `content_ok = pack_complete and
bundle_complete and lint_ok and claims_ok`. The documents deliberately stay IN the bundle so an
operator can see WHICH claim failed; what changes is that the pack does not list.

Blast radius, counted from `store/prospector.jsonl` (the only place the check's output survived):
25 packs have run the check, **20 carry violations that survived the repair turn**; 6 of those 20
have a published listing row. The gate acts at generation time, so it unlists nothing
retroactively — those 6 are held on their next regeneration.

**If you are writing a test for a gate in `publish_pass`, force the other three terms green
first.** The stub fixture in `tests/unit/test_dry_run_gate_mints_nothing.py` fails completeness on
its own, so `assertFalse(content_ok)` is green with or without the gate under test. The pair
`test_unverified_claims_in_the_paid_artifacts_hold_the_pack_off_the_shelf` /
`test_a_clean_claim_check_does_not_hold_the_pack_back` shows the shape.

### Dependencies added, and what each is actually for

`rapidfuzz` (near-duplicate detection; `difflib.SequenceMatcher` took over two minutes on one
pack, which is a gate nobody leaves switched on), `trafilatura` (a fallback in

boilerplate, it rescues total extraction failure: usable passages went 9/12 to 12/12 on one pack's

## A stored gate verdict expires when the rules change (2026-08-17)

A pack's `<id>.lint.json` is a cached verdict. Until now nothing expired it, so a pack blocked by
a linter rule that was later fixed stayed blocked forever. `tools/verify_pass_shelf_coverage.py:14`
records what that cost: "Twenty-four had been published UNLISTED and forgotten."

Freshness used to be mtime only. That answers "has the pack changed", and it cannot answer "have
the rules changed", because editing `pack_linter.py` touches no dossier. Every receipt stays newer
than its pack, so mtime says "current" forever.

Three changes, all small:

1. **`pack_linter.RULESET_VERSION` is a fingerprint of the linter's own bytes**, not a hand-bumped
   integer. `sha256(pack_linter.py)[:12]`, computed at import. Change a rule and every stored
   verdict is stale automatically. Nobody has to remember anything. A comment-only edit
   invalidates every receipt too; that is the deliberate trade, because a re-gate lists nothing
   and mints nothing.
2. **`pack_linter.receipt_is_current(receipt)` is the one definition of staleness**, used by
   `tools/publish_passes.py::_fresh_lint`, `scheduler/run_scheduled.py::_stale_verdicts` and
   `ops/console_api.py::_read_shelf`. Three copies of the same comparison is how they drift.
3. **`_regate_pass` is the counterpart to `_unlist_pass`**, and the half that did not exist. Each
   tick it re-asks the gate about at most `schedule.regate_unlisted_per_tick` stale packs, by
   shelling out to `tools/publish_passes.py --dry-run`.

**The re-gate lists nothing, and that asymmetry is the point.** Unlisting a pack unattended can
only cost a sale. Listing one unattended takes money from a buyer. `bridge.py:1256-1272` returns
on `if dry_run:` before `price_for`, so there is no Stripe Price, no R2 upload and no catalogue
row, and nothing goes on sale. The step refreshes the verdict on disk and prints which packs now
clear the gate. Putting them on sale stays a deliberate act.

**What it costs is network, not model spend, and the gate now runs ten at a time.** Measured
2026-08-17: ~124s a pack, all of it the linter probing the URLs the pack cites. Re-gating the 40
stranded packs took about 80 minutes, because `--jobs` fanned out GENERATION and a dry run does
no generation — the one slow step was the only one still running serially. `publish_passes` now
gates inside the same pool on a dry run and defaults to `_DRY_RUN_JOBS = 10`, which is safe for
the same reason the generation fan-out is: a dry run mints nothing, so there is no money-rail
bookkeeping to serialise, and each pack writes only its own `<id>.lint.json`. Real publishing is
unchanged and still strictly serial.

**A log line cost a round of wrong documentation, and is worth recording as a defect class.**
`publish_passes` printed `stored artifacts incomplete -> regenerating` on every path. On a dry
run that is false — the attempt loop is `range(1, 1)` and never executes. The line was read as
evidence that a rehearsal makes model calls, and a config comment, a docstring and this section
were all written around the wrong number before anyone checked the loop bound. The message now
says what the dry path actually does. A log that states an intention rather than an outcome is
evidence of nothing.

**It self-drains.** A re-gate stamps the current fingerprint whatever the verdict, so a pack that
is still blocked also drops out of the selection. The queue converges to empty instead of
re-running the same failures every tick.

**It is visible and administrable from the Ops Console**, not just a CLI: the stranded page shows
a "stale verdicts" count, tags each affected row `stale — rules changed since`, and offers a
`shelf.regate` button. Pinned by `tests/unit/test_a_stale_verdict_is_re_gated.py`.
